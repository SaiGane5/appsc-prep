# State Executive & State Legislature: Governor, CM & Legislative Council


The constitutional machinery at the state level mirrors the parliamentary structure of the Union. Headed by the Governor as the representative of the Union and constitutional executive, real authority resides in the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers responsible to the Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha). The office of the Governor remains a sensitive focal point of Indian federalism, particularly regarding discretionary powers and recommendations under Article 356.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Executive & Legislative Balance in State Governance

| State Constitutional Organ | Constitutional Articles | Powers, Appointment & Composition | Federal Challenges & Legal Standards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Governor of the State | Articles 153 to 162, 163, 164, 200, 213, 356. | Appointed by President; holds office during presidential pleasure; executive head of state. | Discretionary powers under Art 163; reservation of bills for President (Art 200/201); appointment of CM in hung assemblies; reports on breakdown of constitutional machinery (Art 356 - S.R. Bommai guidelines). |
| Chief Minister & State Council of Ministers | Articles 163, 164, 167. | CM appointed by Governor; ministers on CM's advice; collective responsibility to Legislative Assembly. | Formulates state policy; real executive; communicates administrative decisions to Governor (Art 167). |
| State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) | Article 170. | Directly elected by adult suffrage (max 500, min 60 members; 175 members in Andhra Pradesh). | Supreme legislative authority in state; exclusive power over Money Bills and state budget. |
| State Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) | Articles 169, 171. | Created or abolished by Parliament on special resolution by Assembly (Art 169); bicameral in 6 states (AP, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, UP). | Revisory second chamber (1/3rd elected by MLAs, 1/3rd by local bodies, 1/12th by graduates, 1/12th by teachers, 1/6th nominated by Governor); can delay ordinary bills by max 4 months. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Article 200 Options for Governor on Passed Bills: Give assent, withhold assent, return bill for reconsideration (if not Money Bill), or reserve bill for the consideration of the President (mandatory if bill endangers High Court powers).

* S.R. Bommai Guidelines on Article 356 (1994): Majority of ministry must be tested solely on the floor of the Assembly, not in Raj Bhavan; presidential proclamation subject to judicial review; dissolution of assembly permissible only after Parliament approves proclamation.

* Sarkaria Commission Recommendations on Governor: Eminent person from outside the state, detached from local active politics, appointed in consultation with Chief Minister, guaranteed fixed 5-year tenure save rare exceptions.

* Punchhi Commission (2010): Recommended localizing emergency under Art 355, adopting clear guidelines for government formation (leader of pre-poll alliance first), and amending Art 163 to curtail unfettered discretionary delays on bills.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Under Article 169 of the Constitution of India, which body holds the constitutional power to create or abolish a State Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)?
(a) State Legislative Assembly
(b) Parliament of India
(c) President of India
(d) Governor of the State
Correct Answer: (b) Parliament of India [Explanation: Under Article 169, Parliament can by law create or abolish a Legislative Council if the State Legislative Assembly passes a resolution by special majority.]

* Q2. In which landmark 1994 verdict did the nine-judge bench of the Supreme Court rule that the floor of the Legislative Assembly is the sole constitutional forum to test a Chief Minister's majority?
(a) Rameshwar Prasad Case
(b) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
(c) Nabam Rebia Case
(d) Kesavananda Bharati Case
Correct Answer: (b) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India [Explanation: The S.R. Bommai judgment (1994) curtailed misuse of Article 356, establishing that a ministry's majority can only be verified through a floor test in the Assembly.]

* Q3. How many members of a State Legislative Council are nominated by the Governor from among persons having special knowledge in literature, science, art, cooperative movement, and social service?
(a) 1/3rd of total members
(b) 1/6th of total members
(c) 1/12th of total members
(d) 1/10th of total members
Correct Answer: (b) 1/6th of total members [Explanation: Under Article 171(3)(e), 1/6th of the total members of the Legislative Council are nominated by the Governor from literature, science, art, cooperative movement, and social service.]

* Q4. Which Commission on Centre-State Relations (appointed in 1983) recommended that the Governor should be an eminent person from outside the state and not intimately connected with local politics?
(a) Punchhi Commission
(b) Sarkaria Commission
(c) Rajamannar Committee
(d) Venkatachaliah Commission
Correct Answer: (b) Sarkaria Commission [Explanation: Justice R.S. Sarkaria Commission (1983–1988) recommended that Governors should be distinguished persons from outside the state, appointed in consultation with the Chief Minister.]

* Q5. Under Article 213 of the Constitution, what is the maximum life of an Ordinance promulgated by a Governor during the recess of the State Legislature without legislative approval?
(a) 6 months
(b) 6 weeks from the reassembly of the legislature
(c) 14 days
(d) 3 months
Correct Answer: (b) 6 weeks from the reassembly of the legislature [Explanation: An Ordinance issued by the Governor under Article 213 ceases to operate six weeks after the reassembly of the State Legislature unless approved earlier.]
