# Good Governance: Citizen's Charter, Sevottam Model & RTI Act 2005


Good Governance represents the paradigm shift from bureaucratic authority to citizen-centric public service delivery. Grounded in the United Nations' eight core attributes—participation, rule of law, transparency, responsiveness, consensus orientation, equity, efficiency, and accountability—it is operationalized in India through statutory instruments like the Right to Information Act 2005, the Sevottam Citizen's Charter framework, and grassroots Social Audits.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Instruments of Administrative Transparency & Citizen Centricity

| Governance Instrument | Legal Basis & Origin | Core Operational Mechanisms | Impact on Public Administration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right to Information Act (RTI 2005) | Enacted 15 June 2005; came into force 12 October 2005. | Public Information Officers (PIOs) must provide information within 30 days (48 hours for life/liberty); Section 4(1)(b) proactive disclosure; Section 8 limited exemptions; Central & State Information Commissions. | Empowered ordinary citizens to question executive corruption, audit muster rolls (MGNREGA), and enforce administrative accountability. |
| Citizen's Charter | Originated in UK (1991, John Major); adopted in India (1997). | Written declaration specifying standards, quality, time-frames of service delivery, grievance redress mechanisms, and citizen obligations. | Transforms citizens from passive recipients to active consumers; non-justiciable per se unless backed by statutory service delivery acts. |
| Sevottam Model | Developed by DARPG (Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances). | Three-pillar assessment framework: 1. Citizen's Charter implementation, 2. Public Grievance Redressal mechanism, 3. Service Delivery Capability. | Benchmark quality management tool for certifying government departments (IS 15700:2005). |
| Social Audit | MGNREGA Act 2005 Section 17; pioneered by MKSS in Rajasthan. | Independent audit of government welfare expenditure conducted jointly by beneficiaries and Gram Sabha using primary vouchers and muster rolls. | Exposes ghost beneficiaries, prevents wage theft, and ensures grassroots participatory monitoring; AP Social Audit Society is national benchmark. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Section 4(1)(b) of RTI Act: Mandates all public authorities to proactively publish 17 categories of operational information (budgets, norms, directory of officers, subsidies) on websites to minimize public RTI applications.

* Section 8 Exemptions: Protects information affecting national sovereignty/security, contempt of court, commercial confidence/trade secrets, fiduciary relationships, and unwarranted invasion of individual privacy.

* Andhra Pradesh Right to Public Services Legislation: AP Guaranteed Delivery of Services Act ensures time-bound delivery of essential citizen certificates (caste, income, birth, land records) through village/ward secretariats with penalties on defaulting officers.

* Second ARC (Fourth Report - 'Ethics in Governance'): Recommended amending Official Secrets Act 1923, protecting whistleblowers, operationalizing Ombudsman in local bodies, and establishing clear Citizen's Charters with service compensation.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Under Section 7(1) of the Right to Information Act 2005, what is the mandatory time limit for providing information if it concerns the life or liberty of a person?
(a) 24 hours
(b) 48 hours
(c) 7 days
(d) 30 days
Correct Answer: (b) 48 hours [Explanation: Section 7(1) of the RTI Act 2005 provides that where the information sought concerns the life or liberty of a person, it shall be provided within forty-eight hours of receipt.]

* Q2. The 'Sevottam Model', developed by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG), is an assessment framework for:
(a) E-governance software architecture
(b) Excellence in Public Service Delivery and Citizen's Charters
(c) Environmental impact assessment
(d) Direct benefit transfers
Correct Answer: (b) Excellence in Public Service Delivery and Citizen's Charters [Explanation: Sevottam (Service Excellence) is an assessment-improvement framework for public service delivery based on Citizen's Charters, grievance redress, and service capability.]

* Q3. Section 4(1)(b) of the Right to Information Act 2005 mandates which vital transparency obligation on all public authorities?
(a) Annual confidential reports of civil servants
(b) Proactive suo-motu disclosure of 17 categories of public information
(c) Compulsory audio-recording of cabinet meetings
(d) Disclosure of intelligence agency budgets
Correct Answer: (b) Proactive suo-motu disclosure of 17 categories of public information [Explanation: Section 4(1)(b) mandates every public authority to proactively publish details of its organization, functions, norms, budgets, and employee directories.]

* Q4. Which section of the Right to Information Act 2005 specifies exemptions from disclosure of information, including matters affecting national security and commercial confidence?
(a) Section 4
(b) Section 8
(c) Section 12
(d) Section 20
Correct Answer: (b) Section 8 [Explanation: Section 8 enumerates ten categories of information that are exempt from disclosure, including sovereignty of India, cabinet papers, and trade secrets.]

* Q5. Which civil society grassroots organization, founded by Aruna Roy in Rajasthan, pioneered the 'Jan Sunwai' (Public Hearing) and Social Audit movement leading to the RTI Act?
(a) Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS)
(b) Chipko Movement
(c) Narmada Bachao Andolan
(d) Pratham Foundation
Correct Answer: (a) Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS) [Explanation: The Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS) spearheaded the right to information struggle in rural Rajasthan, demanding access to muster rolls and bills in village Jan Sunwais.]
