# Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles (DPSP) & Fundamental Duties


Parts III, IV, and IV-A of the Constitution constitute the moral and social conscience of the Indian democratic republic. Part III guarantees justiciable Fundamental Rights against state encroachment, revitalized by judicial expansion of Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty); Part IV articulates non-justiciable Directive Principles of State Policy guiding socio-economic welfare; and Part IV-A imposes civic Fundamental Duties upon citizens.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Constitutional Matrix: Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles

| Feature / Attribute | Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts 12–35) | Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts 36–51) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nature & Enforcement | Negative injunctions limiting state power; strictly justiciable under Articles 32 and 226. | Positive commands urging state affirmative action; non-justiciable in courts (Article 37) but fundamental in governance. |
| Ideological Aim | Establishes Political Democracy; protects individual civil liberties against executive tyranny. | Establishes Social and Economic Democracy; establishes a Welfare State (Welfare State vs Police State). |
| Scope of Suspension | Can be suspended during National Emergency (Art 352) except Articles 20 and 21 (44th Amendment 1978). | Cannot be suspended; operate continuously as legislative and administrative benchmarks. |
| Classification | Six Categories: Equality (14–18), Freedom (19–22), Against Exploitation (23–24), Freedom of Religion (25–28), Cultural & Educational (29–30), Constitutional Remedies (32). | Three Traditions: Socialistic (Arts 38, 39, 41, 42, 43), Gandhian (Arts 40, 43, 46, 47, 48), Liberal-Intellectual (Arts 44, 45, 48, 50, 51 - Uniform Civil Code, Separation of Judiciary). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Article 21 & the Golden Triangle: Maneka Gandhi Case (1978) discarded the narrow 'Procedure Established by Law' (A.K. Gopalan 1950) in favor of American 'Due Process of Law'; established that law depriving liberty must be 'just, fair, and reasonable' under Articles 14, 19, and 21; expanded to include Right to Privacy (Puttaswamy 2017), Clean Environment, and Dignified Life.

* Primacy of Article 32: Dr. Ambedkar declared Article 32 as the 'Heart and Soul of the Constitution'; empowers the Supreme Court to issue five prerogative writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, and Quo-Warranto.

* Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A, Article 51A): Added by the 42nd Amendment 1976 on the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee (10 duties); 11th duty added by 86th Amendment 2002 (duty of parents to provide education to children aged 6–14); moral obligations non-enforceable directly by writs but judicially recognized as interpretative guides.

* Uniform Civil Code (Article 44): Directs the state to secure for citizens a uniform civil code throughout India; personal laws currently governed by diverse religious statutes.

1. Examine how the judicial interpretation of Article 21 has evolved from 'Procedure Established by Law' in the A.K. Gopalan case to 'Due Process of Law' in the Maneka Gandhi case. Discuss its impact on expanding civil liberties. (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Contrast the literal, narrow interpretation in A.K. Gopalan (1950) where 'procedure established by law' was held to require only valid formal legislative enactment without examining substantive fairness; analyze the watershed Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978) verdict: inter-linking Articles 14, 19, and 21 (Golden Triangle), establishing that procedure must be 'just, fair, and reasonable', effectively reading substantive Due Process into Article 21; detail the ensuing judicial revolution: expansion into Right to Privacy (K.S. Puttaswamy 2017), Right to Clean Environment, Right to Health, Right to Legal Aid, and Right to Die with Dignity (passive euthanasia); conclude on the transformation of the Supreme Court into the sentinel of human dignity.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Article of the Indian Constitution was hailed by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as the 'very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it'?
(a) Article 14
(b) Article 19
(c) Article 21
(d) Article 32
Correct Answer: (d) Article 32 [Explanation: Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the heart and soul of the Constitution because it guarantees judicial enforcement of all other rights via writs.]

* Q2. In which landmark 2017 ruling did a nine-judge constitutional bench of the Supreme Court unanimously declare the Right to Privacy as a Fundamental Right under Article 21?
(a) Shayara Bano Case
(b) K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India
(c) Navtej Singh Johar Case
(d) Joseph Shine Case
Correct Answer: (b) K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India [Explanation: In Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India (2017), the Supreme Court ruled that privacy is an intrinsic part of the Right to Life and Personal Liberty under Article 21.]

* Q3. The 11th Fundamental Duty (mandating parents to provide educational opportunities to children aged 6 to 14) was added to Article 51A by which Amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment Act 1976
(b) 44th Amendment Act 1978
(c) 86th Amendment Act 2002
(d) 91st Amendment Act 2003
Correct Answer: (c) 86th Amendment Act 2002 [Explanation: The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act of 2002 introduced Article 21A (Right to Education) and added clause (k) to Article 51A as the 11th Fundamental Duty.]

* Q4. Which prerogative writ is issued by the higher judiciary to quash an order passed by an inferior court or tribunal that acted without or in excess of jurisdiction?
(a) Habeas Corpus
(b) Mandamus
(c) Certiorari
(d) Quo-Warranto
Correct Answer: (c) Certiorari [Explanation: The writ of Certiorari is a curative writ issued to quash an order of an inferior court or quasi-judicial body that exceeded its jurisdiction or violated principles of natural justice.]

* Q5. Which Directive Principle of State Policy in Part IV directs the state to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code (UCC) throughout the territory of India?
(a) Article 40
(b) Article 44
(c) Article 48
(d) Article 50
Correct Answer: (b) Article 44 [Explanation: Article 44 of the Directive Principles of State Policy provides that 'the State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a uniform civil code throughout the territory of India'.]
