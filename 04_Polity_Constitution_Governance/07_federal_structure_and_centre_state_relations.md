# Federal Structure of India: Centre-State Relations & Emergency Provisions


The Indian Constitution establishes a 'quasi-federal' union with a strong centralizing unitary tilt ('an indestructible Union of destructible States'). Part XI and Part XII govern the three-fold distribution of legislative, administrative, and financial relations between the Union and the States, fortified by statutory coordination bodies like the Inter-State Council, the GST Council, and the Finance Commission, alongside extraordinary Emergency Provisions under Part XVIII.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Three-Fold Legislative & Financial Distribution under Seventh Schedule

| Legislative List | Original / Current Subjects | Key Subject Domains | Jurisdictional Rule & Conflicts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| List I: Union List | 97 original / 100 current subjects. | Defense, Foreign Affairs, Atomic Energy, Banking, Railways, Airways, Inter-State Trade. | Parliament has exclusive lawmaking power; Residuary Powers vest in Parliament under Article 248. |
| List II: State List | 66 original / 61 current subjects (5 shifted by 42nd Amendment). | Public Order, Police, Public Health, Agriculture, Land, Local Government, Tolls. | State legislatures have exclusive normal jurisdiction, subject to Parliamentary override under Articles 249, 250, 252, and 253. |
| List III: Concurrent List | 47 original / 52 current subjects (Education, Forests, Weights/Measures, Birds/Animals, Administration of Justice added by 42nd Amendment). | Criminal Law, Marriage, Civil Procedure, Trade Unions, Price Control, Electricity. | Both Parliament and States can legislate; Doctrine of Repugnancy (Article 254): Union law prevails unless State law received Presidential Assent. |
| Financial Relations & GST | Articles 268 to 293; GST Council under Article 279A. | Direct taxes to Centre (Corporate, Income); State taxes (Stamp, Excise on alcohol, Land revenue); GST on goods and services. | 101st Amendment (2016) created dual GST; GST Council decisions require 75% weighted majority (Centre has 1/3rd, States have 2/3rd votes). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII): National Emergency (Art 352 - war, external aggression, armed rebellion; 44th Amendment required written cabinet advice and special majority); President's Rule (Art 356 - failure of constitutional machinery in state; max 3 years with periodic approvals per Bommai standards); Financial Emergency (Art 360 - threat to financial stability; never invoked).

* Inter-State Council (Article 263): Constitutional body established by presidential order in 1990 following Sarkaria Commission recommendations; chaired by Prime Minister, comprises all Chief Ministers and 6 Union Cabinet Ministers.

* Inter-State River Water Disputes (Article 262): Parliament enacted Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956; empowers Union to set up ad-hoc tribunals; bars Supreme Court from exercising jurisdiction over river water sharing disputes (Krishna and Godavari Water Disputes Tribunals).

* Commissions on Federalism: Administrative Reforms Commission (1966), Rajamannar Committee (1969, Tamil Nadu), Sarkaria Commission (1983–1988), and M.M. Punchhi Commission (2007–2010).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Residuary powers of legislation—matters not enumerated in the Union, State, or Concurrent Lists—are vested in which authority under Article 248?
(a) State Legislatures
(b) Parliament of India
(c) President of India
(d) Supreme Court of India
Correct Answer: (b) Parliament of India [Explanation: Unlike the US Constitution where residuary powers vest in states, Article 248 of the Indian Constitution vests residuary legislative powers exclusively in Parliament.]

* Q2. Five key subjects—including Education, Forests, and Weights and Measures—were transferred from the State List to the Concurrent List by which Constitutional Amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment Act 1971
(b) 42nd Amendment Act 1976
(c) 44th Amendment Act 1978
(d) 73rd Amendment Act 1992
Correct Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment Act 1976 [Explanation: The 42nd Amendment Act of 1976 transferred Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Protection of Wild Animals, and Administration of Justice from List II to List III.]

* Q3. Under Article 279A, what weighted voting share is held by the Central Government and the State Governments respectively in the GST Council?
(a) Centre: 50%, States: 50%
(b) Centre: 1/3rd (33.33%), States: 2/3rd (66.67%)
(c) Centre: 25%, States: 75%
(d) Equal vote for each member
Correct Answer: (b) Centre: 1/3rd (33.33%), States: 2/3rd (66.67%) [Explanation: Under Article 279A(9), the Central Government has a weight of one-third of total votes cast, while all State Governments combined have a weight of two-thirds, requiring 75% for decision-making.]

* Q4. Which permanent constitutional body to promote inter-governmental coordination was established under Article 263 by a Presidential Order in 1990?
(a) NITI Aayog
(b) Inter-State Council
(c) National Development Council
(d) Zonal Councils
Correct Answer: (b) Inter-State Council [Explanation: Following the Sarkaria Commission recommendations, the Inter-State Council was formally constituted under Article 263 in May 1990 to investigate and advise on inter-state policy issues.]

* Q5. Which ground for proclaiming a National Emergency under Article 352 was substituted with 'Armed Rebellion' by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act 1978?
(a) External aggression
(b) Internal disturbance
(c) War
(d) Insurgency
Correct Answer: (b) Internal disturbance [Explanation: The 44th Amendment Act (1978) substituted 'armed rebellion' for the vague phrase 'internal disturbance' to prevent arbitrary invocation of emergency powers.]
