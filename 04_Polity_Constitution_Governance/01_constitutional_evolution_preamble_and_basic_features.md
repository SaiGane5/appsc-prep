# Constitutional Evolution, Preamble & Basic Structure Doctrine


The Constitution of India represents the world's most detailed written democratic social contract. Evolving through colonial administrative experiments (Regulating Act 1773 to Government of India Act 1935) and crafted by the Constituent Assembly under Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, the Constitution embodies a transformative philosophy in its Preamble and is anchored by the judicial Basic Structure Doctrine established in the historic Kesavananda Bharati case (1973).

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Evolutionary Milestones in the Making of the Indian Constitution

| Constitutional Milestone | Year & Statutory Instrument | Core Provisions & Institutional Innovations | Impact on Contemporary Constitution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Government of India Act 1935 | 1935 (British Parliament) | Federal Scheme, Provincial Autonomy, Bicameralism in 6 provinces, Federal Court, Three Legislative Lists. | Over 60% of current administrative and structural provisions borrowed directly from this Act. |
| Constituent Assembly | 1946–1949 (Cabinet Mission Plan) | 389 initial members; Drafting Committee chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar; completed in 2 yrs, 11 mos, 18 days. | Framed 395 Articles and 8 Schedules adopted on 26 Nov 1949; came into force on 26 Jan 1950. |
| Preamble & 42nd Amendment | Adopted 1949; Amended 1976 | Declared India a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic committed to Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity. | 42nd Amendment 1976 added 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity'; held as integral part of Constitution (LIC of India case). |
| Basic Structure Doctrine | 1973 (Kesavananda Bharati Case) | Supreme Court 13-judge constitutional bench ruled Parliament cannot alter the 'Basic Structure' under Article 368. | Safeguards judicial review, federalism, secularism, democracy, and fundamental rights against majoritarian amendments. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Preamble as the Key to Constitution: Kesavananda Bharati (1973) overruled the earlier Berubari Union (1960) stance, affirming that the Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution and can be amended under Article 368 without altering the basic features.

* Salient Features: Blend of rigidity and flexibility, Parliamentary sovereignty balanced with Judicial supremacy, Single integrated judicial hierarchy, Universal adult suffrage without property qualifications, and Single citizenship.

* Constitutional Sources: Parliamentary system and Rule of Law (Britain), Fundamental Rights and Judicial Review (USA), DPSP (Ireland), Concurrent List and Joint Sitting (Australia), Federal system with strong Centre (Canada), Emergency provisions (Weimar Germany).

* Basic Structure Components: Supremacy of the Constitution, Republican and Democratic form of government, Secular character, Separation of Powers, Federalism, Judicial Review, and Free and fair elections.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which landmark 13-judge constitutional bench ruling of the Supreme Court of India in 1973 enunciated the 'Basic Structure Doctrine'?
(a) Golaknath Case (1967)
(b) Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)
(c) Minerva Mills Case (1980)
(d) Maneka Gandhi Case (1978)
Correct Answer: (b) Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973) [Explanation: In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), a historic 13-judge bench held that Parliament's amending power under Article 368 does not extend to altering the Basic Structure of the Constitution.]

* Q2. The words 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity' were inserted into the Preamble of the Constitution of India by which Constitutional Amendment Act?
(a) 24th Amendment Act 1971
(b) 42nd Amendment Act 1976
(c) 44th Amendment Act 1978
(d) 52nd Amendment Act 1985
Correct Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment Act 1976 [Explanation: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976 amended the Preamble for the only time in history, adding the words 'Socialist', 'Secular', and 'Integrity'.]

* Q3. The concept of 'Directive Principles of State Policy' (DPSP) embodied in Part IV of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the constitution of which nation?
(a) United States of America
(b) Ireland (Irish Republic)
(c) Australia
(d) Canada
Correct Answer: (b) Ireland (Irish Republic) [Explanation: The framers of the Indian Constitution borrowed the Directive Principles of State Policy from the 1937 Constitution of Ireland, which had taken them from the Spanish Constitution.]

* Q4. In which case did the Supreme Court explicitly affirm that 'the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles'?
(a) Shankari Prasad Case
(b) Minerva Mills Case (1980)
(c) Waman Rao Case
(d) AK Gopalan Case
Correct Answer: (b) Minerva Mills Case (1980) [Explanation: In Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India (1980), the Supreme Court ruled that the balance between Part III (Fundamental Rights) and Part IV (DPSP) is an essential basic feature.]

* Q5. Who described the Preamble of the Constitution of India as the 'Political Horoscope' of our sovereign democratic republic?
(a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(b) K.M. Munshi
(c) Pandit Thakur Das Bhargava
(d) Sir Alladi Krishnaswamy Iyer
Correct Answer: (b) K.M. Munshi [Explanation: Dr. K.M. Munshi, a member of the Drafting Committee, famously characterized the Preamble as the 'Horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic'.]
