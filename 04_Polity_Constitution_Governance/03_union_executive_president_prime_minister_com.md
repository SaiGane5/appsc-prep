# Union Executive: President, Prime Minister & Council of Ministers


The Union Executive of India embodies the Westminster model of parliamentary democracy, establishing a constitutional dichotomy between the De Jure titular head of state (the President of India) and the De Facto real executive (the Prime Minister in Council of Ministers). Anchored in the principle of collective ministerial responsibility to the Lok Sabha, the executive exercises supreme governing authority over the Union.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Constitutional Roles & Executive Powers in the Union Executive

| Executive Functionary | Constitutional Articles | Method of Election / Appointment | Core Executive & Discretionary Powers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| President of India (Head of State) | Articles 52 to 62, 72, 123, 352–360. | Elected by Electoral College (Elected MPs of both Houses + Elected MLAs of all States/UTs) by single transferable vote. | Supreme Commander of Armed Forces; summons/prorogues Parliament; Promulgates Ordinances (Art 123); Pardoning power (Art 72); Emergency powers; acts on aid and advice of CoM (Art 74). |
| Vice-President of India | Articles 63 to 71. | Elected by Electoral College consisting of ALL MPs of both Houses of Parliament (elected and nominated). | Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Art 64); acts as President during vacancy (up to 6 months). |
| Prime Minister & Council of Ministers | Articles 74, 75, 78. | PM appointed by President; Ministers appointed on PM's recommendation; must be MP within 6 months. | Real executive head; aids and advises President; allocates portfolios; determines policy; collectively responsible to Lok Sabha (Art 75(3)); individual responsibility to President. |
| Attorney General for India | Article 76. | Appointed by President; qualified to be appointed Supreme Court judge; holds office during President's pleasure. | Chief legal advisor to Government of India; right of audience in all Indian courts; right to speak and take part in parliamentary proceedings without voting right. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Article 74(1) & 42nd/44th Amendments: 42nd Amendment (1976) made President bound by advice of Council of Ministers; 44th Amendment (1978) introduced proviso allowing President to return advice once for reconsideration, but bound if returned unchanged.

* Ordinance-Making Power (Article 123): Exercised only when Parliament is not in session and circumstances require immediate action; has same force as Act of Parliament; must be approved by Parliament within 6 weeks of reassembly; subject to judicial review (DC Wadhwa 1987).

* Pardoning Power of President (Article 72): Power to grant pardons, reprieves, respites, and remissions of punishment; extends to court-martial sentences and death penalties; exercised on advice of Union Home Ministry; subject to limited judicial review against arbitrariness (Kehar Singh 1989, Shatrughan Chauhan 2014).

* Prime Ministerial Discretionary Powers: Appointment of PM when no single party holds clear majority (Hung Parliament); dismissal of Council of Ministers that lost majority in Lok Sabha and refuses to resign.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Who constitutes the Electoral College for the election of the President of India under Article 54 of the Constitution?
(a) All members of both Houses of Parliament
(b) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (including Delhi and Puducherry)
(c) Elected and nominated members of Parliament and State Assemblies
(d) Only members of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha
Correct Answer: (b) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (including Delhi and Puducherry) [Explanation: Article 54 specifies that the presidential electoral college consists solely of elected MPs of Parliament and elected MLAs of state and UT legislative assemblies.]

* Q2. Under Article 123 of the Constitution, an Ordinance promulgated by the President during parliamentary recess ceases to operate within how many weeks of Parliament reassembling?
(a) 2 weeks
(b) 4 weeks
(c) 6 weeks
(d) 6 months
Correct Answer: (c) 6 weeks [Explanation: An Ordinance must be laid before both Houses of Parliament and expires six weeks from the date of reassembly unless approved earlier by both Houses.]

* Q3. Which Article of the Indian Constitution explicitly provides that the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)?
(a) Article 74(1)
(b) Article 75(3)
(c) Article 77
(d) Article 78
Correct Answer: (b) Article 75(3) [Explanation: Article 75(3) states that 'the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People', anchoring the core principle of parliamentary accountability.]

* Q4. The President's pardoning power under Article 72 differs fundamentally from the Governor's pardoning power under Article 161 in which key respect?
(a) The President cannot pardon contempt of court
(b) Only the President can pardon death sentences and sentences by Court Martial
(c) The Governor can pardon military offenses
(d) The Governor has wider pardon powers than the President
Correct Answer: (b) Only the President can pardon death sentences and sentences by Court Martial [Explanation: The President has exclusive authority to pardon sentences inflicted by a Court Martial and death sentences; Governors cannot pardon death sentences (they can only suspend/remit).]

* Q5. Which high constitutional officer has the unique right to speak and take part in the proceedings of either House of Parliament without the right to vote?
(a) Comptroller and Auditor General
(b) Attorney General for India
(c) Chief Election Commissioner
(d) Solicitor General
Correct Answer: (b) Attorney General for India [Explanation: Under Article 88, the Attorney General for India has the right to speak and participate in parliamentary proceedings and committees without voting rights.]
