# Statutory & Regulatory Bodies: NITI Aayog, NHRC, Lokpal & CVC


Modern democratic governance relies on independent statutory, regulatory, and ombudsman institutions to protect human rights, curb public corruption, ensure competitive market fairness, and guide national strategic development. These bodies—including NITI Aayog, the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC), the Lokpal, the Central Vigilance Commission (CVC), and the Central Information Commission (CIC)—operate outside direct ministerial hierarchy to enforce institutional checks and balances.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Charter of Premier Statutory, Regulatory & Ombudsman Institutions

| Institution | Statutory Act / Resolution | Composition & Selection Committee | Mandate & Operational Powers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) | Cabinet Resolution (1 January 2015, replacing Planning Commission). | Chairperson (PM), Vice-Chairperson, CEO, Ex-officio members, Governing Council (all CMs and Lt Governors). | Think-tank fostering 'Cooperative and Competitive Federalism'; bottom-up approach; Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) India Index; Aspirational Districts Programme. |
| National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) | Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 (amended 2019). | Chairperson (retired CJI or SC judge) + 5 members; appointed by committee (PM, Home Minister, Speaker, Deputy Chairman RS, Leaders of Opposition in LS & RS). | Inquires into human rights violations; visits jails; civil court powers; can recommend compensation and prosecution; non-binding advisory powers. |
| Lokpal of India (Anticorruption Ombudsman) | Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013. | Chairperson + max 8 members (50% judicial, 50% SC/ST/OBC/Women/Minority); selection committee (PM, Speaker, LoP, CJI, eminent jurist). | Inquires into corruption allegations against public functionaries including Prime Minister (with safeguards), Union Ministers, MPs, and Group A, B, C, D officers; has inquiry and prosecution wings. |
| Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) | CVC Act 2003 (originally K. Santhanam Committee 1964). | Central Vigilance Commissioner + max 2 Vigilance Commissioners; appointed by committee (PM, Home Minister, LoP in LS). | Apex integrity watchdog; superintends CBI investigations under Prevention of Corruption Act; advises central ministries on vigilance matters. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* NITI Aayog vs Planning Commission: Replaced top-down uniform planning with bottom-up tailored strategy; eliminated discretionary plan funds allocation (devolved to Finance Commission); emphasizes evidence-based ranking of states (SDG Index, Export Preparedness Index, Health Index).

* Central Information Commission (CIC): Established under the Right to Information Act 2005; hears second appeals against denial of public information; enforces proactive transparency.

* Regulatory Authorities: Competition Commission of India (CCI, Competition Act 2002 - prevents anti-competitive cartels and abuses of dominance); Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI Act 1997); Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI Act 1992 - capital market regulator); Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI Act 1999, headquartered in Hyderabad).

* Lokayukta in Andhra Pradesh: Statutory anti-corruption ombudsman established under the AP Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayukta Act 1983; investigates maladministration and corruption by public servants and ministers in AP.

### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Critically evaluate the performance of NITI Aayog in promoting cooperative and competitive federalism in India since replacing the Planning Commission in 2015. (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Explain the institutional shift: replacing the centralized, top-down allocation model of the Planning Commission with NITI Aayog as a national knowledge and strategy think-tank; analyze mechanisms promoting Cooperative Federalism: the Governing Council bringing all Chief Ministers as equal partners in formulating national agendas; examine Competitive Federalism: indices ranking states on performance (SDG India Index, Multidimensional Poverty Index, Aspirational Districts Programme) stimulating peer competition; discuss structural limitations: lack of financial resource-allocation powers (leaving states reliant on Finance Commission and Union ministries), advisory recommendations without binding enforceability, and concerns from regional states on centralized conditionalities.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which body was replaced on 1 January 2015 by the Government of India through a Cabinet Resolution to establish NITI Aayog?
(a) National Development Council
(b) Planning Commission of India
(c) Finance Commission
(d) National Advisory Council
Correct Answer: (b) Planning Commission of India [Explanation: NITI Aayog was established on 1 January 2015 via an executive resolution, superseding the 65-year-old Planning Commission.]

* Q2. The Selection Committee that recommends the appointment of the Chairperson and Members of the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) is headed by:
(a) Chief Justice of India
(b) President of India
(c) Prime Minister of India
(d) Union Home Minister
Correct Answer: (c) Prime Minister of India [Explanation: Under the Protection of Human Rights Act 1993, the selection committee is chaired by the Prime Minister and includes the Speaker, Home Minister, and Leaders of the Opposition.]

* Q3. Which landmark committee on the Prevention of Corruption appointed in 1962 recommended the establishment of the Central Vigilance Commission (CVC)?
(a) K. Santhanam Committee
(b) Sarkaria Commission
(c) Gorwala Committee
(d) Paul Appleby Committee
Correct Answer: (a) K. Santhanam Committee [Explanation: The Santhanam Committee on Prevention of Corruption (1962–1964) recommended creating the CVC to tackle corruption in central government administration.]

* Q4. Under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act 2013, what minimum proportion of the members of the Lokpal must be drawn from SCs, STs, OBCs, Minorities, and Women?
(a) 25%
(b) 33.33%
(c) 50%
(d) 60%
Correct Answer: (c) 50% [Explanation: Section 3(3) of the Lokpal Act mandates that not less than 50% of the members of the Lokpal shall be from SCs, STs, OBCs, Minorities, and Women.]

* Q5. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), which oversees and regulates the insurance sector in India, is headquartered in which city?
(a) Mumbai
(b) New Delhi
(c) Hyderabad
(d) Bengaluru
Correct Answer: (c) Hyderabad [Explanation: IRDAI is a statutory regulatory body established in 1999 and headquartered in Hyderabad, Telangana (formerly undivided Andhra Pradesh).]
