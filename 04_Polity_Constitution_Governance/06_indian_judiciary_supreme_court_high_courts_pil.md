# Indian Judiciary: Supreme Court, High Courts, Collegium & PIL


The Indian Constitution establishes an integrated, single judicial pyramid with the Supreme Court at the apex, exercising supreme appellate authority, constitutional interpretation, and the defense of fundamental rights. Characterized by judicial independence guaranteed through tenure security and the judge-led Collegium appointment system, the Indian judiciary has pioneered proactive constitutional tools like Judicial Review, Judicial Activism, and Public Interest Litigation (PIL).

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Jurisdictional Spectrum of the Supreme Court of India

| Constitutional Jurisdiction | Article Reference | Legal Scope & Subject Matter | Key Precedents & Principles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original Jurisdiction | Article 131 | Exclusive disputes between Government of India and one or more States, or between two or more States. | Federal interstate legal disputes (State of Karnataka v. Union of India); excludes pre-constitution treaties. |
| Writ Jurisdiction | Article 32 (SC) & Article 226 (HC) | Enforcement of Fundamental Rights; power to issue 5 writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto). | Art 32 is itself a Fundamental Right; Art 226 of High Courts is broader, extending to 'any other legal right'. |
| Appellate Jurisdiction | Articles 132, 133, 134, 136 (Special Leave Petition - SLP). | Appeals from High Courts in constitutional, civil, and criminal matters; Article 136 grants discretionary residual power to hear appeals against any court/tribunal. | Article 136 is an exceptional plenary jurisdiction to correct gross miscarriages of justice. |
| Advisory Jurisdiction | Article 143 | President may refer questions of law or public importance to Supreme Court for opinion. | SC opinion is advisory and not binding on President; e.g., Berubari Union (1960), Special Courts Bill (1978), 2G Spectrum (2012). |
| Court of Record & Review | Articles 129 & 137 | Judgments have evidentiary value and cannot be questioned in lower courts; power to punish for contempt of itself; power to review its own judgments. | Enables self-correction of past constitutional doctrines. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The Collegium System: Developed through the 'Three Judges Cases' (SP Gupta 1981, Advocates-on-Record 1993, Special Reference 1998); Chief Justice of India and 4 senior-most Supreme Court judges recommend appointments to SC and High Courts; Supreme Court struck down the National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC, 99th Amendment 2014) in the Fourth Judges Case (2015) to preserve judicial independence.

* Public Interest Litigation (PIL): Relaxed traditional 'Locus Standi' rule; pioneered in late 1970s by Justices P.N. Bhagwati and V.R. Krishna Iyer; permits any public-spirited citizen or NGO to file writ petitions on behalf of disadvantaged citizens unable to access justice (Hussainara Khatoon 1979 - rights of undertrials; Bandhua Mukti Morcha 1984 - bonded labour).

* Judicial Review: Inherent in Articles 13, 32, 136, 142, and 226; power to declare legislative enactments and executive orders null and void if they violate constitutional provisions.

* Article 142 (Complete Justice): Unique constitutional power authorizing the Supreme Court to pass any decree or order necessary for doing 'complete justice' in any cause or matter before it (used in Union Carbide, Ayodhya title suit, ban on liquor shops on highways).

### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Critically evaluate the functioning of the Collegium system for judicial appointments in India. Examine the constitutional tension between Judicial Independence and Executive accountability highlighted by the NJAC judgment (2015). (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Trace the evolution of the Collegium through the Three Judges Cases, creating an unprecedented system where judges appoint judges; analyze the NJAC (99th Amendment Act 2014) proposing a 6-member commission including the Law Minister and eminent persons; explain why the Supreme Court struck down the NJAC in the Fourth Judges Case (2015): holding that executive presence in judicial appointments infringes Judicial Independence, which is a Basic Structure feature; evaluate criticisms of the Collegium: lack of transparency, nepotism, absence of public scrutiny, and delays in filling vacancies; suggest balanced reforms through a revised Memorandum of Procedure (MoP) incorporating objective eligibility criteria and search committees.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Article of the Constitution of India empowers the Supreme Court to pass any decree or order necessary for doing 'complete justice' in any cause or matter?
(a) Article 136
(b) Article 141
(c) Article 142
(d) Article 143
Correct Answer: (c) Article 142 [Explanation: Article 142 provides the Supreme Court with extraordinary equitable power to issue orders necessary for doing 'complete justice' between the parties.]

* Q2. The concept of 'Public Interest Litigation' (PIL), which liberalized the traditional rule of locus standi, was pioneered in India primarily by which Supreme Court jurist?
(a) Justice H.R. Khanna
(b) Justice P.N. Bhagwati
(c) Justice M.N. Venkatachaliah
(d) Justice Y.V. Chandrachud
Correct Answer: (b) Justice P.N. Bhagwati [Explanation: Justice P.N. Bhagwati (alongside Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer) institutionalized Public Interest Litigation in India in cases like Hussainara Khatoon and SP Gupta.]

* Q3. In which landmark verdict in October 2015 did a five-judge constitutional bench of the Supreme Court strike down the National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC)?
(a) First Judges Case (1981)
(b) Second Judges Case (1993)
(c) Third Judges Case (1998)
(d) Fourth Judges Case (2015)
Correct Answer: (d) Fourth Judges Case (2015) [Explanation: In the Fourth Judges Case (Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India, 2015), the court struck down the 99th Amendment Act and the NJAC as unconstitutional.]

* Q4. Under Article 143 of the Constitution, who possesses the exclusive authority to refer a question of law or fact of public importance to the Supreme Court for its advisory opinion?
(a) Prime Minister of India
(b) President of India
(c) Speaker of the Lok Sabha
(d) Attorney General for India
Correct Answer: (b) President of India [Explanation: Article 143 empowers the President of India to consult the Supreme Court on questions of law or fact of public importance.]

* Q5. How does the writ jurisdiction of a High Court under Article 226 differ fundamentally from that of the Supreme Court under Article 32?
(a) High Court cannot issue Habeas Corpus
(b) High Court can issue writs only for fundamental rights
(c) High Court's writ jurisdiction extends to fundamental rights as well as ordinary legal rights
(d) Supreme Court has wider writ jurisdiction than High Courts
Correct Answer: (c) High Court's writ jurisdiction extends to fundamental rights as well as ordinary legal rights [Explanation: Under Article 226, High Courts can issue writs for fundamental rights and 'for any other purpose' (ordinary legal rights), making its scope wider than Article 32.]
