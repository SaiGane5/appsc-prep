# Civil Disobedience: Dandi March, Round Table Conferences & Poona Pact


The Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–1934) marked the second great nationwide mass campaign of the Indian freedom struggle. Catalyzed by the boycott of the all-white Simon Commission (1927), the declaration of 'Purna Swaraj' (Complete Independence) at the Lahore Congress (1929), and Gandhi's historic Dandi Salt March (12 March – 6 April 1930), the movement challenged British imperial authority across the salt pans, forest tracts, and urban centres of India.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The Three Round Table Conferences (London): First RTC (1930 - boycotted by Congress; attended by Ambedkar, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Muslim League); Second RTC (1931 - attended solely by Gandhi representing INC, ended in deadlock over minority separate electorates); Third RTC (1932 - boycotted by Congress; culminated in White Paper leading to the Government of India Act 1935).

* Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ('Frontier Gandhi'): Organized the non-violent 'Khudai Khidmatgars' (Red Shirts) in NWFP; Garhwali soldiers under Chandra Singh Garhwali refused to open fire on unarmed Muslim satyagrahis at Peshawar.

* Government of India Act 1935: Enacted provincial autonomy; abolished provincial dyarchy and introduced dyarchy at the Centre; created an All-India Federation (never implemented); established Federal Court and Reserve Bank of India; 1937 provincial elections saw Congress form ministries in 7 out of 11 provinces.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. At which historic annual session of the Indian National Congress in December 1929 was the resolution for 'Purna Swaraj' (Complete Independence) officially adopted under the presidency of Jawaharlal Nehru?
(a) Madras Session
(b) Lahore Session
(c) Karachi Session
(d) Calcutta Session
Correct Answer: (b) Lahore Session [Explanation: The historic Lahore Congress of December 1929, presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, proclaimed Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence) as the ultimate national goal.]

* Q2. The historic Dandi Salt March commenced from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930 with how many chosen ashramite volunteers accompanying Mahatma Gandhi?
(a) 50 volunteers
(b) 72 volunteers
(c) 78 volunteers
(d) 100 volunteers
Correct Answer: (c) 78 volunteers [Explanation: Gandhi marched 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to the Dandi coastline accompanied initially by 78 handpicked ashramite satyagrahis, reaching Dandi on 5 April 1930.]

* Q3. In Tamil Nadu, who led the parallel coastal Salt Satyagraha march from Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam in April 1930?
(a) K. Kamaraj
(b) C. Rajagopalachari
(c) Subramania Bharati
(d) V.O. Chidambaram Pillai
Correct Answer: (b) C. Rajagopalachari [Explanation: C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) led a group of 100 Congress volunteers from Trichy to Vedaranyam on the Tanjore coast to break the salt law in April 1930.]

* Q4. Which Indian leader attended all three Round Table Conferences held in London between 1930 and 1932 to advocate for the rights of the Depressed Classes?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi
(b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(c) Jawaharlal Nehru
(d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Correct Answer: (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar [Explanation: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar and Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru attended all three Round Table Conferences in London (1930, 1931, and 1932).]

* Q5. The historic Poona Pact, signed on 24 September 1932 between representatives of caste Hindus and the Depressed Classes, replaced separate electorates with:
(a) Universal adult franchise without reservations
(b) Joint electorates with reserved seats for Depressed Classes
(c) Proportional representation by single transferable vote
(d) Nomination by the Governor-General
Correct Answer: (b) Joint electorates with reserved seats for Depressed Classes [Explanation: The Poona Pact discarded separate electorates in favor of joint electorates with reserved seats, increasing legislative seats for the Depressed Classes from 71 to 148.]
