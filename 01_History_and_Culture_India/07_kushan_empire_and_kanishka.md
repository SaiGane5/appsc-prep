# The Kushan Empire: Kanishka, Silk Route, Gandhara & Mathura Art


Originating from the Yuezhi nomadic confederation of Central Asia, the Kushans established a trans-continental empire straddling the Oxus and the Ganga (Uzbekistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan, and Northern India). Reaching its zenith under Kanishka I (78 CE), the empire linked the Silk Route trade networks of Rome and Han China, fostered Mahayana Buddhism, and patronized the twin sculptural traditions of Gandhara and Mathura.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Artistic Dichotomy: Gandhara School vs Mathura School of Art

| Artistic Attribute | Gandhara School of Art | Mathura School of Art |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geographical Centre | Northwest frontier (Taxila, Peshawar, Swat, Hadda, Bamiyan). | Yamuna valley (Mathura, Sonkh, Kankali Tila). |
| Medium & Material | Grey schist sandstone, stucco, clay, terracotta. | Indigenous spotted red sandstone (Sikri sandstone). |
| Stylistic Influences | Greco-Roman / Hellenistic realism; wavy hair curls, Apollo-like facial anatomy, heavy drapery folds. | Purely indigenous Indian aesthetic; fleshy smiling features, transparent thin drapery, spiritual prabhavali (halo). |
| Religious Scope | Exclusively Buddhist (Buddha, Bodhisattvas like Maitreya, Avalokiteshvara). | Pluralistic: Buddhism, Jainism (Ayagapatas / votive tablets, Tirthankaras), and Brahmanical deities (Shiva Linga, Vishnu, Kartikeya). |
| Royal Patronage | Kushans, Saka-Parthians, post-Kushan local chieftains. | Kushans and later Guptas; established the iconic canonical seated Buddha prototype. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Kanishka I (78 CE): Inaugurated the Saka Era (official civil calendar of modern India); established twin capitals at Purushapura (Peshawar) and Mathura; erected a 400-foot multi-tiered wooden stupa at Shah-ji-ki-Dheri.

* Fourth Buddhist Council (Kundalvana, Kashmir): Presided over by Vasumitra and Ashvaghosha; compiled the 'Mahavibhasha Sastra'; formalized the doctrinal schism between Theravada/Hinayana and Mahayana (worship of Buddha as a divine saviour through idol worship).

* Court Luminaries: Ashvaghosha (authored 'Buddhacharita' and 'Saundarananda'), Vasumitra, Charaka (court physician, 'Charaka Samhita'), and Nagarjuna (philosopher of Madhyamaka / Sunyavada).

* Silk Route Trade & Numismatics: Controlled northern trans-Asian overland arteries; issued the purest gold dinars and heavy copper coins matching Roman imperial standards; received Roman gold for Indian spices, silk, and gems.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. The Indian National Calendar, adopted by the Government of India in 1957, is based on the historic Saka Era founded in which year?
(a) 57 BCE
(b) 78 CE
(c) 319 CE
(d) 248 CE
Correct Answer: (b) 78 CE [Explanation: The Saka Era, commencing in 78 CE with the accession of Kushan Emperor Kanishka I, forms the basis of the official National Civil Calendar of India.]

* Q2. Who served as the Vice-President of the Fourth Buddhist Council held in Kashmir and authored the celebrated classical Sanskrit epic 'Buddhacharita'?
(a) Vasumitra
(b) Ashvaghosha
(c) Nagarjuna
(d) Asanga
Correct Answer: (b) Ashvaghosha [Explanation: Ashvaghosha, court poet of Kanishka, was the vice-president of the council and authored 'Buddhacharita' (the earliest complete biography of the Buddha in Sanskrit) and 'Saundarananda'.]

* Q3. The medium predominantly used by sculptors of the Gandhara School of Art to carve realistic Buddhist sculptures was:
(a) Spotted red sandstone
(b) Grey schist and stucco
(c) White marble
(d) Monolithic granite
Correct Answer: (b) Grey schist and stucco [Explanation: The Gandhara artists utilized local grey/bluish schist stone and stucco (plaster) to render Buddha images imbued with Greco-Roman realistic drapery and Apollo-like countenances.]

* Q4. Which celebrated ancient physician, regarded as the founding father of Indian internal medicine (Kayachikitsa), served as the royal court physician of Kanishka?
(a) Sushruta
(b) Charaka
(c) Vagbhata
(d) Dhanvantari
Correct Answer: (b) Charaka [Explanation: Charaka, author of the foundational Ayurvedic compendium 'Charaka Samhita', flourished under Kushan patronage in Kanishka's court.]

* Q5. Which Buddhist philosopher pioneered the Madhyamaka (Middle Way) school and the doctrine of 'Sunyavada' (Emptiness) under Kushan patronage?
(a) Dignaga
(b) Dharmakirti
(c) Nagarjuna
(d) Vasubandhu
Correct Answer: (c) Nagarjuna [Explanation: Acharya Nagarjuna developed the Madhyamaka philosophy and Sunyata doctrine in his treatise 'Mulamadhyamakakarika', active during the late Kushan-Satavahana transition.]
