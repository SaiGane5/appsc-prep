# Revolutionary Movement for Freedom: Ghadar, HSRA, Bhagat Singh & Surya Sen


Parallel to mainstream constitutional and non-violent congress agitations, the revolutionary movement provided a radical, militant dimension to India's freedom struggle. Evolving across two distinct phases—from early secret societies (Anushilan, Yugantar) and international anti-colonial networks (Ghadar, Berlin Committee) to the socialist-inspired Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) and Surya Sen's Chittagong uprising—revolutionary martyrs inspired uncompromising anti-imperialist patriotism.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Bhagat Singh's Ideological Legacy: Authored 'Why I am an Atheist' in Lahore Central Jail; rejected religious communalism; embraced scientific socialism; martyred alongside Rajguru and Sukhdev on 23 March 1931 ('Shaheed Diwas').

* Jatin Das's Martyrdom: 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Central Jail protesting horrific prison conditions and demanding political prisoner status; died a martyr on 13 September 1929.

* Chandrashekhar Azad: Commander-in-chief of HSRA; vowed never to be captured alive by the British; died in heroic gunfight against police at Alfred Park, Allahabad on 27 February 1931.

* Women in Revolutionary Movement: Pritilata Waddedar (sacrificed life by cyanide after leading Pahartali raid), Kalpana Datta, Bina Das (shot Bengal Governor Stanley Jackson at Calcutta University convocation in 1932), Suniti Choudhury and Santi Ghose (assassinated Comilla district magistrate Charles Stevens).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Who unfurled the first design of the Indian National Flag on foreign soil at the International Socialist Congress held in Stuttgart, Germany in 1907?
(a) Sarojini Naidu
(b) Bhikaji Cama
(c) Annie Besant
(d) Kalpana Datta
Correct Answer: (b) Bhikaji Cama [Explanation: Madam Bhikaji Cama proudly hoisted the tricolor 'Flag of Indian Independence' at the International Socialist Conference in Stuttgart, Germany on 22 August 1907.]

* Q2. The revolutionary organization 'Hindustan Republican Association' (HRA), founded in Kanpur in 1924, was reconstituted as 'Hindustan Socialist Republican Association' (HSRA) in 1928 at:
(a) Kakori
(b) Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi
(c) Alfred Park, Allahabad
(d) Lahore Central Jail
Correct Answer: (b) Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi [Explanation: In September 1928, under the leadership of Chandrashekhar Azad and Bhagat Singh, the HRA was reorganized at Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi, adding 'Socialist' to its name.]

* Q3. Who accompanied Bhagat Singh in throwing non-lethal smoke bombs and leaflets ('To make the deaf hear') into the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi on 8 April 1929?
(a) Rajguru
(b) Batukeshwar Dutt
(c) Sukhdev
(d) Jatin Das
Correct Answer: (b) Batukeshwar Dutt [Explanation: Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt hurled non-lethal bombs into the empty benches of the Central Legislative Assembly to protest the repressive Public Safety and Trade Disputes Bills.]

* Q4. Which legendary revolutionary, revered as 'Masterda', led the audacious Chittagong Armoury Raid on 18 April 1930 and proclaimed a Provisional Revolutionary Government?
(a) Rash Behari Bose
(b) Surya Sen
(c) Jatindranath Mukherjee (Bagha Jatin)
(d) Sachindra Nath Sanyal
Correct Answer: (b) Surya Sen [Explanation: Surya Sen ('Masterda'), a schoolteacher, organized the Indian Republican Army and executed the famous raid on the British police and auxiliary armouries at Chittagong.]

* Q5. Revolutionary freedom fighter Jatin Das attained martyrdom after an epic 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Central Jail in September 1929 demanding:
(a) Release of all political prisoners
(b) Civil rights and political prisoner status for undertrial revolutionaries
(c) Commutation of Bhagat Singh's death sentence
(d) Immediate repeal of the Rowlatt Act
Correct Answer: (b) Civil rights and political prisoner status for undertrial revolutionaries [Explanation: Jatin Das died on 13 September 1929 following a 63-day fast demanding humane prison conditions, hygienic food, and dignified status as political prisoners rather than common criminals.]
