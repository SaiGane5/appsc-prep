# Delhi Sultanate: Administration, Agrarian & Market Reforms


The establishment of the Delhi Sultanate in 1206 CE following the victories of Muhammad Ghori inaugurated over three centuries of Turkish-Afghan rule across five successive dynasties: Mamluk/Slave, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, and Lodi. The Sultanate synthesized Perso-Islamic bureaucratic centralization with indigenous agrarian structures, introduced the Iqta land assignment system, and deployed radical economic controls under Alauddin Khalji.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The Iqta System: Sultanate divided empire into Iqtas assigned to military commanders (Iqtadars / Muqtis); responsible for maintaining law and order, raising troops, and remitting surplus revenue (Fawazil) to the central treasury.

* Central Administrative Departments: Diwan-i-Wizarat (Finance under Wazir), Diwan-i-Arz (Military under Ariz-i-Mamalik), Diwan-i-Insha (Royal Correspondence under Dabir-i-Khas), Diwan-i-Rasalat (Religious endowments and foreign affairs under Sadr-us-Sudur).

* Alauddin Khalji's Agrarian Reforms: First sultan to order measurement of cultivable land (Maha) as basis for tax assessment; fixed state demand at 50% of gross produce (Kharaj); eliminated traditional intermediaries (Khuts, Muqaddams, Chaudharis) from extracting illegal cesses.

* Amir Khusrau (1253–1325 CE, 'Tuti-e-Hind'): Court poet of 7 sultans; pioneer of Hindavi, Qawwali, and ghazal forms; invented the Sitar and Tabla; authored 'Khazain-ul-Futuh' and 'Tughlaqnama'.




## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Critically evaluate the market control regulations and price-fixation system implemented by Alauddin Khalji. What were the administrative objectives and economic outcomes of these measures? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Explain military motivation: sustaining a massive standing army on modest salaries to repel Mongol invasions and conquer the Deccan without depleting state reserves; detail mechanisms: price ceilings on grains, cloth, slaves, and livestock; establishment of distinct supervised markets under Shahna-i-Mandi, Diwan-i-Riyasat, and spies (Munhiyans); procurement through government granaries in Doab; evaluate economic impact: effective price stability during wartime but severe hardships for rural peasantry, suppression of merchant profits, and rapid collapse after Alauddin's death.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Delhi Sultan introduced the rigorous military verification practices of 'Dagh' (branding of war horses) and 'Chehra' (descriptive roll of soldiers)?
(a) Balban
(b) Alauddin Khalji
(c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
(d) Sikandar Lodi
Correct Answer: (b) Alauddin Khalji [Explanation: Alauddin Khalji instituted the Dagh and Chehra systems to eliminate military corruption, ghost soldiers, and substitution of inferior horses in royal muster rolls.]

* Q2. The department of agriculture, 'Diwan-i-Amir-Kohi', was established to extend cultivable land through state agricultural loans (Sondhar/Takkavi) by:
(a) Iltutmish
(b) Alauddin Khalji
(c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
(d) Firuz Shah Tughlaq
Correct Answer: (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq [Explanation: Muhammad bin Tughlaq founded the Diwan-i-Kohi to promote state-assisted agriculture, crop rotation, and extending land under the plough via agricultural loans.]

* Q3. The famous silver coin 'Tanka' and copper coin 'Jital' were standardized and introduced into the currency system of the Delhi Sultanate by:
(a) Qutb-ud-din Aibak
(b) Iltutmish
(c) Balban
(d) Sher Shah Suri
Correct Answer: (b) Iltutmish [Explanation: Shamsuddin Iltutmish introduced pure Arabic coinage into India, standardizing the silver Tanka (175 grains) and copper Jital.]

* Q4. Which Sultan of Delhi shifted his imperial capital from Delhi to Devagiri (renamed Daulatabad) in the Deccan in 1327 CE?
(a) Alauddin Khalji
(b) Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq
(c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
(d) Bahlul Lodi
Correct Answer: (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq [Explanation: Muhammad bin Tughlaq ordered the capital transfer to Daulatabad to exercise tighter strategic control over the Deccan and South India, causing severe hardship before reversing it.]

* Q5. Court poet and musicologist Amir Khusrau, known as the 'Parrot of India' (Tuti-e-Hind), was a devoted spiritual disciple of which renowned Sufi saint?
(a) Khwaja Muinuddin Chishti
(b) Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya
(c) Bakhtiyar Kaki
(d) Sheikh Salim Chishti
Correct Answer: (b) Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya [Explanation: Amir Khusrau was the beloved disciple of Chishti master Hazrat Nizamuddin Auliya in Delhi; he died of grief within months of his master's demise in 1325 CE.]
