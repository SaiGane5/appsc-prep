# Post-Mauryan Invasions: Indo-Greeks, Sakas & Parthians


The disintegration of the Mauryan Empire around 185 BCE left northwestern India exposed to successive waves of foreign incursions from Central Asia and Bactria. Indo-Greeks, Scythians (Sakas), and Indo-Parthians introduced Hellenistic artistic idioms, standardized portrait gold and silver coinage, astronomy, and the Gandhara school of Buddhist iconography.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Dynastic Profile of Post-Mauryan Invading Powers

| Invading Dynasty | Key Monarchs & Capitals | Numismatic & Inscriptional Legacy | Cultural & Religious Synthesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Indo-Greeks (Bactrian Greeks) | Demetrius I, Menander I (Milinda); Sakala (Sialkot). | First rulers to issue die-struck gold coins with royal portraits and bilingual legends (Greek & Kharosthi). | Menander embraced Buddhism after theological dialogue with monk Nagasena, immortalized in the Pali classic 'Milindapanha'. |
| Scythians / Sakas (Western Kshatrapas) | Maues, Chashtana, Rudradaman I; Ujjain & Junagadh. | Junagadh Rock Inscription (150 CE) in pure classical Sanskrit; issued dated silver coins. | Rudradaman I repaired the Sudarshana lake dam without levying emergency taxes (Vishti); patronized Sanskrit kavya. |
| Indo-Parthians (Pahlavas) | Gondophares (c. 19–46 CE); Taxila. | Takht-i-Bahi inscription in Mardan; issued copper and billon coinage. | Christian traditions record the arrival of Apostle St. Thomas in northwestern India during Gondophares' reign. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Indo-Greek Innovations: Introduced curtains into Indian theatre (named 'Yavanika' after Yavanas/Greeks); popularized planetary horoscopy and astronomy ('Gargi Samhita' states: 'The Greeks are barbarians, but the science of astronomy originated with them and for this they must be reverenced as gods').

* Western Kshatrapas (Kaharata & Kardamaka): Ruled Gujarat, Malwa, and northern Maharashtra; prolonged warfare with the Satavahanas for control of lucrative Konkan ports (Bharuch/Barygaza, Sopara).

* Sudarshana Lake Reservoir History: Originally excavated under Chandragupta Maurya (Pushyagupta), conduits added under Ashoka (Tushaspha), repaired by Saka king Rudradaman I (Suvisakha) in 150 CE, and repaired again by Gupta king Skandagupta (Chakrapalita) in 456 CE.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. The famous philosophical dialogue between Buddhist monk Nagasena and the Indo-Greek king Menander is preserved in which Pali text?
(a) Mahavamsa
(b) Dipavamsa
(c) Milindapanha
(d) Divyavadana
Correct Answer: (c) Milindapanha [Explanation: 'Milindapanha' (Questions of King Milinda) records the philosophical debate between Menander I and Nagasena, leading to Menander's conversion to Buddhism.]

* Q2. The earliest major royal inscription composed entirely in chaste, refined classical Sanskrit prose without Prakritisms is:
(a) Hathigumpha Inscription of Kharavela
(b) Junagadh Inscription of Rudradaman I
(c) Allahabad Pillar Inscription of Samudragupta
(d) Aihole Inscription of Pulakesin II
Correct Answer: (b) Junagadh Inscription of Rudradaman I [Explanation: Dated to c. 150 CE, the Junagadh rock inscription of Saka Mahakshatrapa Rudradaman I is the celebrated pioneer of classical Sanskrit epigraphic literature.]

* Q3. According to early Christian church traditions, which Apostle arrived in northwestern India during the reign of Indo-Parthian King Gondophares?
(a) St. Peter
(b) St. Thomas
(c) St. Paul
(d) St. Bartholomew
Correct Answer: (b) St. Thomas [Explanation: Apocryphal Acts of Thomas records that Apostle Thomas visited the court of Gondophares in Taxila around 40 CE before traveling to the Malabar coast.]

* Q4. Which term was adopted in ancient Indian Sanskrit drama for the stage curtain, reflecting Hellenistic theatrical influence?
(a) Yavanika
(b) Sutradhara
(c) Nataka
(d) Vidushaka
Correct Answer: (a) Yavanika [Explanation: The Sanskrit theatrical curtain was termed 'Yavanika' (derived from 'Yavana', meaning Ionian Greek), denoting Greek theatrical mechanical influence.]

* Q5. Which Saka ruler was decisively defeated and killed by Satavahana monarch Gautamiputra Satakarni, as recorded in the Nashik Cave Inscription?
(a) Chashtana
(b) Nahapana
(c) Rudradaman I
(d) Bhumaka
Correct Answer: (b) Nahapana [Explanation: Gautamiputra Satakarni exterminated the Kshaharata dynasty by killing Nahapana and counter-striking thousands of his silver coins, as discovered at the Jogalthambi hoard.]
