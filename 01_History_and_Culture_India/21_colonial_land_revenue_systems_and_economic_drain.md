# Colonial Economy: Land Revenue Systems & Drain of Wealth


British rule transformed the traditional self-sufficient Indian agrarian economy into a colonial appendage servicing metropolitan industrial capital. Through three extractive land revenue settlements—the Permanent Settlement (1793), Ryotwari System (1820), and Mahalwari System (1822)—and the systematic de-industrialization of handicrafts, the colonial state extracted massive agrarian surpluses, documented by nationalist economic historians as the 'Drain of Wealth'.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Comparative Analysis of Colonial Land Revenue Settlements

| Settlement System | Architect & Year Introduced | Geographical Coverage & Ownership | Revenue Assessment & Socio-Economic Impact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Permanent Settlement (Zamindari) | Lord Cornwallis & John Shore (1793). | Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Northern Circars of AP, Varanasi (approx 19% of British India). Zamindars recognized as absolute hereditary owners. | State revenue demand fixed permanently at 10/11th, with 1/11th retained by Zamindar; 'Sunset Law' triggered auctions if unpaid by sunset of due date; spawned absentee landlordism, sub-infeudation, and peasant misery. |
| Ryotwari System | Thomas Munro & Alexander Read (1820). | Madras, Bombay, parts of Assam and Coorg (approx 51% of British India). Peasants (Ryots) recognized as registered landholders. | Direct settlement between State and individual cultivator; revenue revised periodically (every 20–30 years); exorbitant rates (45%–55% of gross produce) led to heavy indebtedness to local Marwari/Chettiar moneylenders. |
| Mahalwari System | Holt Mackenzie (1822) & Robert Merttins Bird (1833). | North-Western Provinces, Central Provinces, Punjab (approx 30% of British India). Joint ownership by village community (Mahal). | Entire village/estate jointly and severally responsible for revenue payment through the village headman (Lambardar); survey based on village field maps; high tax pressure triggered peasant destitution. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Drain of Wealth Theory: Articulated by Dadabhai Naoroji in 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' (1867/1901), R.C. Dutt in 'Economic History of India', and M.G. Ranade; defined as unrequited transfer of Indian resources to Britain without any equivalent economic or material return.

* Constituents of the Drain: Home Charges (salaries, pensions, and leave allowances of British civil/military officers in London), interest on foreign debt, guaranteed 5% return on British railway investments, store purchases for the India Office, and outward remittances of British corporate profits.

* De-Industrialization & Commercialization: One-way free trade destroyed the world-famous Indian artisanal handloom textile industry (Dacca muslin, Surat calico); forced cultivation of cash crops (indigo, cotton, opium, tea) distorted local food security and caused catastrophic famines (1866, 1876–78, 1896–97).

* Famine Commissions: Campbell Commission (1866), Strachey Commission (1880 - created Famine Code), Lyall Commission (1897), and MacDonnell Commission (1901).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Who authored the seminal economic treatise 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' (1901), systematically detailing the Drain of Wealth mechanism?
(a) Romesh Chunder Dutt
(b) Dadabhai Naoroji
(c) Mahadev Govind Ranade
(d) Gopal Krishna Gokhale
Correct Answer: (b) Dadabhai Naoroji [Explanation: Dadabhai Naoroji expounded the Drain Theory in his 1867 paper and published the comprehensive book 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' in 1901.]

* Q2. The controversial 'Sunset Law', stipulating that an estate would be confiscated and auctioned if taxes were not paid by sunset on the appointed date, was a feature of:
(a) Ryotwari Settlement
(b) Permanent Settlement of Bengal
(c) Mahalwari System
(d) Malguzari Settlement
Correct Answer: (b) Permanent Settlement of Bengal [Explanation: Under Cornwallis's Permanent Settlement, the Sunset Law mandated that if a Zamindar failed to pay his revenue installment by sunset on the specified date, his zamindari was auctioned off.]

* Q3. The Ryotwari land revenue system, establishing direct fiscal settlement between the colonial state and individual peasant cultivators, was initiated in Madras by:
(a) Lord Cornwallis
(b) Thomas Munro and Alexander Read
(c) Holt Mackenzie
(d) John Shore
Correct Answer: (b) Thomas Munro and Alexander Read [Explanation: Captain Alexander Read introduced Ryotwari on an experimental basis in the Baramahal region (1792), later generalized across the Madras Presidency by Governor Sir Thomas Munro in 1820.]

* Q4. Which Famine Commission, appointed under the chairmanship of Sir Richard Strachey in 1880, formulated the principles that led to the creation of the Indian Famine Code?
(a) Campbell Commission
(b) Strachey Commission
(c) Lyall Commission
(d) MacDonnell Commission
Correct Answer: (b) Strachey Commission [Explanation: The Strachey Commission (1880) established foundational guidelines for famine relief works, suspension of land revenue, and government responsibility during famines, creating the Famine Code.]

* Q5. In the context of British colonial accounts, what did the fiscal category 'Home Charges' specifically encompass?
(a) Taxes on domestic real estate in Calcutta and Bombay
(b) Expenditures incurred in Britain on behalf of India by the Secretary of State
(c) Subsidies paid to princely states under Subsidiary Alliances
(d) Customs tariffs collected on British textile imports
Correct Answer: (b) Expenditures incurred in Britain on behalf of India by the Secretary of State [Explanation: Home Charges represented expenditures incurred in London from Indian revenues, including India Office expenses, pensions of British civil/military officers, and guaranteed interest on Indian railways.]
