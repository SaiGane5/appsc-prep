## The 1857 War of Independence, Socio-Religious Renaissance, Early Nationalist Mobilization, and Armed Revolutionary Resistance

The Great Uprising of 1857 constituted the first massive structural challenge to colonial hegemony in South Asia, precipitating the abolition of Company rule and the assumption of governance by the British Crown. Simultaneously, the 19th-century intellectual renaissance challenged internal socio-religious decay, while armed revolutionaries in India and abroad provided a militant challenge to colonial rule.


### 1. The 1857 War of Independence: Causes, Leadership & Impact in Andhra


*Multi-Dimensional Causes and Repercussions of the 1857 Uprising*

| Dimension | Specific Catalysts & Historical Facts | Colonial Manifestations & Outcomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Political & Administrative | Lord Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse; annexation of Awadh (1856) on pretext of misgovernance; humiliation of Mughal Emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar (refusal to recognize imperial title post-death). | Dispossessed native princes and taluqdars; alienated landed aristocracies across central India. |
| Economic Exploitation | Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari, and Mahalwari extortionate land revenue demands; de-industrialization of traditional weavers and artisans; commercialization of agriculture inducing recurring famines. | Peasantry driven into debt bondage by moneylenders; agrarian distress fueled popular mass rebellion. |
| Socio-Religious & Military | General Service Enlistment Act (1856) requiring overseas service (violating caste purity); proselytization by Christian missionaries; introduction of the Enfield Rifle cartridge greased with animal fat (cow and pig). | Mangal Pandey mutinied at Barrackpore (29 March 1857); Sepoys at Meerut broke into open revolt on 10 May 1857, marching to Delhi and proclaiming Bahadur Shah Zafar as 'Shahenshah-e-Hindustan'. |
| Repercussions in Andhra / Concerned State | Revolt of Turrebaz Khan and Maulvi Alauddin against Hyderabad British Residency; Korukonda Subba Reddy and tribal leaders rising in the Rampa and Agency hill tracts of Godavari; mutinous sentiments in Machilipatnam and Visakhapatnam garrisons. | Heavily suppressed by British forces; highlighted early anti-colonial solidarity across the Deccan. |
| Post-1857 Structural Changes | Government of India Act 1858: Liquidated East India Company; transfer of sovereignty to British Crown; Secretary of State for India established. | Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1 November 1858) promising non-annexation of princely states; Peel Commission reorganizing army on 'divide and rule' racial quotas. |


### 2. 19th-Century Socio-Religious Reform Movements: All-India & Andhra Context


*Pioneering Reformers of India and Andhra Pradesh*

| Movement / Reformer | Ideological Philosophy & Publications | Socio-Religious Crusade & Legislative Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raja Ram Mohan Roy (Brahmo Samaj, 1828) | 'Father of Modern Indian Renaissance'; monotheism; authored 'Tuhfat-ul-Muwahhidin', 'Precepts of Jesus'; published Sambad Kaumudi (Bengali) and Mirat-ul-Akbar (Persian). | Crusaded against Sati (banned 1829), idol worship, child marriage, and polygamy; pioneered modern Western scientific education. |
| Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar | Principal of Sanskrit College, Calcutta; promoted female education (Bethune School). | Spearheaded movement legalizing widow remarriage (Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act XV of 1856, piloted under Dalhousie and enacted under Canning). |
| Swami Dayananda Saraswati (Arya Samaj, 1875) | Slogan 'Go Back to the Vedas'; authored 'Satyarth Prakash'; established DAV schools. | Rejected idol worship, caste by birth, child marriage, and purdah; initiated Shuddhi movement (re-conversion); championed Swadeshi ('India for Indians'). |
| Jyotirao Phule (Satyashodhak Samaj, 1873) | Authored 'Gulamgiri'; opened first girls' school at Pune (1848) with wife Savitribai Phule. | Crusaded against Brahminical supremacy, caste exploitation, and untouchability; pioneered non-Brahmin social movement. |
| Kandukuri Veeresalingam (Andhra Renaissance) | 'Vidyasagar of Andhra'; authored 'Rajasekhara Charitra' (first modern Telugu novel); established Vivekavardhani journal. | Organized the first registered widow remarriage in Andhra on 11 December 1881 at Rajahmundry; established Rajahmundry Social Reform Association, widow homes, and female schools. |
| Raghupati Venkataratnam Naidu & Gurajada Apparao | Brahmo movement in Andhra; Gurajada authored the reformist social drama 'Kanyasulkam' (1892) in spoken colloquial Telugu. | Naidu fought against the Devadasi / Kalavanthulu system and untouchability; Gurajada attacked child marriage and bride-price (Kanyasulkam). |


### 3. Armed Revolutionary Movements in India and Abroad

- First Phase of Revolutionary Nationalism (1907–1917): Anushilan Samiti (Barindra Ghosh, Bhupendranath Datta) and Jugantar in Bengal; Alipore Bomb Conspiracy (Aurobindo Ghosh); Abhinav Bharat Society (V.D. Savarkar) in Maharashtra; assassination of Curzon Wyllie by Madan Lal Dhingra in London.
- Revolutionary Organizations Abroad: India House in London (Shyamji Krishna Varma); Madame Bhikaji Cama unfurled the first Indian National Flag at the International Socialist Conference in Stuttgart, Germany (1907); Ghadar Party founded in San Francisco (1913, Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, Kartar Singh Sarabha) and Komagata Maru incident (1914); Berlin Committee for Indian Independence (Virendranath Chattopadhyaya / Zimmermann Plan).
- Second Phase of Revolutionaries (1920s–1930s): Hindustan Republican Association (HRA, 1924: Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan, Chandrashekhar Azad) executed the Kakori Train Action (1925).
- Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA, 1928, Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi): Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev, Rajguru; adopted scientific socialism; assassinated Saunders (1928) to avenge Lala Lajpat Rai's death; Central Legislative Assembly Bombing (8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt throwing leaflets proclaiming 'To make the deaf hear'); hunger strike of Jatin Das (died after 63 days); Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, and Sukhdev martyred on 23 March 1931 ('Shaheed Diwas').
- Surya Sen & The Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930): Masterda Surya Sen and the Indian Republican Army (including brave women revolutionaries Pritilata Waddedar and Kalpana Datta) raided the British armouries, cut telegraph communications, and proclaimed a provisional independent revolutionary government in Chittagong.


### Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


#### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Evaluate the nature and character of the Uprising of 1857. Was it merely a 'Sepoy Mutiny' or the 'First War of Indian Independence'? Discuss its reverberations in Andhra and the Deccan. (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Contrast imperialist historiography (John Lawrence, Sir John Kaye viewing it as an unpatriotic military mutiny triggered by greased cartridges) with nationalist historiography (V.D. Savarkar's thesis of the 'First War of Indian Independence' and Marxist interpretation as popular agrarian anti-feudal uprising); detail broad social participation of peasants, dispossessed princes, and artisans alongside sepoys; examine Deccan/Andhra sparks: Turrebaz Khan's assault on the Hyderabad Residency, Rampa tribal revolts led by Korukonda Subba Reddy; conclude on its role as the foundational watershed in India's anti-colonial struggle.
2. Critically examine the contribution of Kandukuri Veeresalingam Pantulu to the social renaissance in Andhra. Why is he regarded as the 'Father of Modern Andhra Renaissance'? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Highlight his comprehensive crusades against widow mistreatment, child marriage, and institutional corruption; examine his pioneering institutional mechanisms: Rajahmundry Social Reform Association (1878), performing the first historic widow remarriage on 11 December 1881 despite ferocious orthodox boycotts, constructing widow homes and girls' schools; evaluate his literary contribution: launching the reformist journal 'Vivekavardhani', writing the first Telugu novel 'Rajasekhara Charitra', and pioneering modern Telugu prose; compare his stature with Bengal's Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar.
3. Trace the ideological evolution of the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) under Bhagat Singh. How did their philosophy transcend physical terrorism towards mass revolutionary socialism? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Trace evolution from HRA's armed actions (Kakori 1925) to HSRA's ideological reorientation at Feroz Shah Kotla (1928) adding 'Socialist' to their name; examine Bhagat Singh's intellectual evolution (essays 'Why I am an Atheist', reading Marx and Lenin); explain their distinction between terrorism and revolution (revolution means changing the social order based on the exploitation of man by man, not merely shooting bullets); analyze the Assembly bomb throwing where bombs were deliberately designed without shrapnel to cause no deaths, intended solely to propagate revolutionary ideas in court.


#### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

- Q1. Who among the following historic personalities first unfurled the Indian National Flag on foreign soil at the International Socialist Conference held in Stuttgart, Germany in 1907?
(a) Lala Har Dayal
(b) Madame Bhikaji Cama
(c) Shyamji Krishna Varma
(d) Virendranath Chattopadhyaya
Correct Answer: (b) Madame Bhikaji Cama [Explanation: Madame Bhikaji Cama boldly unfurled the precursor to the Indian tricolor flag in Stuttgart in 1907, demanding international recognition of India's freedom.]
- Q2. The first registered widow remarriage in the Telugu-speaking region of Andhra was conducted by Kandukuri Veeresalingam Pantulu on which historic date?
(a) 15 August 1878
(b) 11 December 1881
(c) 2 October 1893
(d) 12 January 1885
Correct Answer: (b) 11 December 1881 [Explanation: Kandukuri performed the first widow remarriage in Andhra on 11 December 1881 at Rajahmundry, marrying Gogulapati Sreeramulu to Gowramma.]
- Q3. The Ghadar Party, an armed revolutionary organization aiming to liberate India from British rule, was founded in 1913 with its headquarters at San Francisco by:
(a) V.D. Savarkar
(b) Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna
(c) Ras Behari Bose
(d) Sachindra Sanyal
Correct Answer: (b) Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna [Explanation: The Ghadar Party was established in 1913 in the United States by Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, and other expatriate Punjabi immigrants at the 'Yugantar Ashram' in San Francisco.]
- Q4. Which revolutionary hero courted martyrdom after a heroic 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Central Jail in 1929 demanding political prisoner rights for Indian revolutionaries?
(a) Bhagat Singh
(b) Jatin Das (Jatindra Nath Das)
(c) Sukhdev
(d) Batukeshwar Dutt
Correct Answer: (b) Jatin Das (Jatindra Nath Das) [Explanation: Jatin Das died on 13 September 1929 after an epic 63-day hunger strike in Lahore Jail, sparking massive national grief.]
- Q5. The celebrated Telugu reformist drama 'Kanyasulkam', which fiercely satirized the social evils of child marriage, bride-price, and social hypocrisy, was authored by:
(a) Kandukuri Veeresalingam
(b) Gurajada Apparao
(c) Gidugu Ramamurthy
(d) Chilakamarti Lakshmi Narasimham
Correct Answer: (b) Gurajada Apparao [Explanation: Gurajada Apparao composed the landmark social satire play 'Kanyasulkam' in 1892, written entirely in colloquial spoken Telugu.]
