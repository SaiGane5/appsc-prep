# European Advent: Commercial Rivalries, Carnatic Wars & Plassey


The arrival of Vasco da Gama at Calicut in 1498 inaugurated European mercantile expansion in India. Over the next two centuries, the Portuguese, Dutch, French, and English East India Companies contested for maritime dominance over the spice and textile trade. Through the Anglo-French Carnatic Wars and the fateful conquest of Bengal at Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764), the English East India Company transformed from a merchant corporation into a sovereign colonial empire.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The Three Carnatic Wars (1746–1763): Anglo-French proxy wars in coastal Coromandel; First Carnatic War (Battle of St. Thome 1746 - small disciplined European force routed large Nawab army; Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle 1748); Second Carnatic War (Robert Clive captured Arcot 1751); Third Carnatic War (Sir Eyre Coote defeated French General Lally at Battle of Wandiwash 1760; Treaty of Paris 1763 reduced French to unarmed trading posts).

* Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Nawab Siraj-ud-Daulah clashed with Robert Clive over misuse of trade passes (Dastaks) and fortification of Calcutta; Clive conspired with Nawab's treacherous commander Mir Jafar, financier Jagat Seth, and Rai Durlabh; battle won with minimal fighting; marked the foundation of British colonial rule in India.

* Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764): Decisive pitched battle where Major Hector Munro routed the combined confederacy of Mir Qasim (former Nawab of Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daula (Nawab of Awadh), and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II.

* Treaty of Allahabad (1765): Robert Clive secured the 'Diwani' (civil administration and revenue collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa for the EIC from Shah Alam II in return for an annual tribute of 26 lakh rupees, institutionalizing the corrupt 'Dual Government' (1765–1772).




## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Why is the Battle of Buxar (1764) considered far more decisive and politically consequential than the Battle of Plassey (1757) in establishing British hegemony in India? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Contrast the nature of the two battles: Plassey was a skirmish won through treason and palace conspiracy against an isolated young Nawab (Siraj-ud-Daulah), leaving the British as puppet-masters; Buxar was a genuine military contest where Company troops routed the combined forces of the Mughal Emperor (Shah Alam II), the Nawab of Awadh, and Mir Qasim; detail the political aftermath: Treaty of Allahabad (1765) granting sovereign Diwani rights over Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa; establish how Buxar made the Company the de jure rulers of India's richest agrarian region, funding future subcontinental conquests.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Portuguese Viceroy instituted the 'Blue Water Policy' (Cartaz system), prioritizing naval supremacy over territorial land expansion in the Indian Ocean?
(a) Francisco de Almeida
(b) Afonso de Albuquerque
(c) Nino da Cunha
(d) Duarte Barbosa
Correct Answer: (a) Francisco de Almeida [Explanation: Francisco de Almeida (first Portuguese Viceroy, 1505–1509) initiated the Blue Water Policy to control Indian Ocean trade chokepoints through fortified naval supremacy.]

* Q2. The Mughal Emperor Farrukhsiyar issued his famous imperial Farman of 1717, granting the English East India Company duty-free trading privileges (Dastaks) in Bengal, which is often termed:
(a) Charter of Commerce
(b) Magna Carta of the Company
(c) Golden Farman
(d) Treaty of Murshidabad
Correct Answer: (b) Magna Carta of the Company [Explanation: Farrukhsiyar's 1717 Farman granted the Company duty-free trade in Bengal for an annual nominal payment of Rs. 3,000, famously hailed as the 'Magna Carta' of the EIC.]

* Q3. In which decisive battle of the Third Carnatic War in 1760 did British forces under Sir Eyre Coote crush the French army under Count de Lally, permanently ending French imperial ambitions in India?
(a) Battle of St. Thome
(b) Battle of Ambur
(c) Battle of Wandiwash
(d) Battle of Bedara
Correct Answer: (c) Battle of Wandiwash [Explanation: The Battle of Wandiwash (22 January 1760) in Tamil Nadu was a crushing British victory over the French, reducing French presence in India to unfortified enclaves.]

* Q4. Through which historic treaty concluded in 1765 did Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II grant the Diwani (revenue collection rights) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the British East India Company?
(a) Treaty of Alinagar
(b) Treaty of Allahabad
(c) Treaty of Purandar
(d) Treaty of Mangalore
Correct Answer: (b) Treaty of Allahabad [Explanation: Signed by Robert Clive and Shah Alam II in August 1765, the Treaty of Allahabad granted the Company sovereign Diwani revenue rights, inaugurating the Dual System of government in Bengal.]

* Q5. Which Travancore ruler decisively defeated the Dutch East India Company at the Battle of Colachel in 1741, marking the first major victory of an Indian monarch over a European naval power?
(a) Swathi Thirunal
(b) Marthanda Varma
(c) Dharma Raja
(d) Pazhassi Raja
Correct Answer: (b) Marthanda Varma [Explanation: King Marthanda Varma of Travancore routed the Dutch fleet at the Battle of Colachel on 10 August 1741, completely terminating Dutch political influence in Malabar.]
