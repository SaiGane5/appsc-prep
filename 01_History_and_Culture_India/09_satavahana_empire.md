# The Satavahana Empire: Polity, Society, Trade & Amaravati Art


The Satavahanas (known as Andhras in the Puranas) held sway over the Deccan plateau (Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, and northern Karnataka) for over four centuries (c. 1st Century BCE to 3rd Century CE). Founded by Simuka with capitals at Pratishthana (Paithan) and Dhanyakataka (Amaravati), the Satavahanas pioneered royal land grants (Agraharas), built rock-cut Buddhist chaityas across the Western Ghats, and fostered Indian Ocean maritime commerce.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Royal Land Grants (Agrahara System): Satavahanas were the first dynasty in Indian history to issue epigraphically recorded tax-free land grants with administrative immunities (Pariharas) to Buddhist monks and Brahmins, sowing seeds of feudalism.

* Amaravati School of Art: Flourished in lower Krishna-Godavari valley (Amaravati, Nagarjunakonda, Bhattiprolu); characterized by use of white Palnadu limestone (marble), dynamic narrative relief panels, and depicting human emotions in assemblies.

* Administration & Social Life: Provinces called Aharas governed by Amatyas; military governors called Gaulmikas; matrilineal metronymics (naming after mothers: Gautamiputra, Vashishtiputra) combined with strictly patrilineal throne succession.

* Rock-Cut Architecture in Western Deccan: Excavated magnificent chaitya-grihas and viharas at Karle (largest rock-cut chaitya), Bhaja, Bedsa, Kanheri, and Nashik.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which famous Satavahana queen mother issued the Nashik Prashasti inscription eulogizing the military victories and virtues of her son Gautamiputra Satakarni?
(a) Nayanika
(b) Gautami Balasri
(c) Rudradharani
(d) Samgharakkhita
Correct Answer: (b) Gautami Balasri [Explanation: Gautami Balasri recorded the celebrated Nashik Cave Prashasti in the 19th regnal year of her grandson Pulumavi II, commemorating Gautamiputra Satakarni as 'Ekabrahmana'.]

* Q2. The earliest epigraphic evidence in Indian history recording the grant of tax-free land to religious beneficiaries is found in which inscription?
(a) Hathigumpha Inscription
(b) Nanaghat Inscription of Queen Nayanika
(c) Junagadh Inscription
(d) Eran Inscription
Correct Answer: (b) Nanaghat Inscription of Queen Nayanika [Explanation: The Nanaghat cave inscription of Queen Nayanika (wife of Satakarni I) contains the earliest epigraphically documented grant of land and villages to Vedic priests.]

* Q3. The famous Prakrit romantic poetic anthology 'Gathasaptasati' (700 stanzas) was compiled by which Satavahana monarch?
(a) Simuka
(b) Hala
(c) Pulumavi I
(d) Satakarni II
Correct Answer: (b) Hala [Explanation: King Hala (the 17th Satavahana ruler) composed the 'Gathasaptasati' (Gaha Sattasai) in Maharashtri Prakrit, providing rich insights into ancient Deccan rural society.]

* Q4. Which distinctive stone was predominantly utilized by sculptors of the ancient Amaravati School of Art in Andhra Pradesh?
(a) Grey schist
(b) White marble-like limestone
(c) Red sandstone
(d) Black basalt
Correct Answer: (b) White marble-like limestone [Explanation: The Amaravati School of Art made exclusive use of white/greenish Palnadu limestone (often called Amaravati marble) to carve intricate Buddhist narrative reliefs.]

* Q5. Which Satavahana monarch issued unique copper and lead coins stamped with the symbol of a two-masted ship, reflecting trans-oceanic maritime trade?
(a) Gautamiputra Satakarni
(b) Vashishtiputra Pulumavi
(c) Yajna Sri Satakarni
(d) Kuntala Satakarni
Correct Answer: (c) Yajna Sri Satakarni [Explanation: Yajna Sri Satakarni issued coins bearing two-masted seafaring ship designs, demonstrating Satavahana naval power and flourishing Bay of Bengal maritime commerce.]
