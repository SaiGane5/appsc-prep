# Indus Valley Civilization: Urban Planning, Society, Trade & Religion


The Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2600–1900 BCE Mature Phase) represents South Asia's primary Bronze Age urban threshold. Spanning over 1.3 million sq km across the Indus-Ghaggar-Hakra river basins, it was distinguished by standardized baked brick architecture, municipal drainage systems, long-distance maritime commerce with Mesopotamia (Meluhha), and an undeciphered logo-syllabic script.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Urban Planning & Municipal Sanitation: Strict gridiron plan (chess-board pattern) with streets intersecting at right angles; underground covered drains flanked by inspection traps and soak pits.

* Social Stratification & Crafts: Absence of royal palaces or military barracks; dominance of merchant-artisan guilds; lapidary bead-making at Chanhudaro; shell-working at Balakot.

* Religion & Ritual Practices: Iconography of Proto-Shiva (Pashupati seal flanked by elephant, tiger, rhino, buffalo, and deer beneath the seat), Mother Goddess terracotta figurines, phallic lingam stones, pipal tree veneration, and unicorn motifs.

* Decline Dynamics: Multi-causal transformation rather than abrupt Aryan massacre; drying of Saraswati/Ghaggar system, shifting monsoons, ecological degradation, tectonic river blockages, and collapse of Mesopotamian trade.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Indus Valley Civilization site features a unique tripartite urban classification consisting of a Citadel, a Middle Town, and a Lower Town?
(a) Mohenjo-daro
(b) Kalibangan
(c) Dholavira
(d) Banawali
Correct Answer: (c) Dholavira [Explanation: Unlike most Harappan settlements divided into two zones, Dholavira in Kutch is divided into three distinct fortified sectors: Citadel, Middle Town, and Lower Town, accompanied by an elaborate water-harvesting reservoir system.]

* Q2. The famous 'Pashupati Seal' discovered at Mohenjo-daro depicts a horned yogic figure surrounded by which group of four animals?
(a) Elephant, Tiger, Rhinoceros, Buffalo
(b) Lion, Tiger, Elephant, Bull
(c) Horse, Elephant, Bull, Rhinoceros
(d) Tiger, Lion, Horse, Deer
Correct Answer: (a) Elephant, Tiger, Rhinoceros, Buffalo [Explanation: The Pashupati (Proto-Shiva) seal shows a three-faced seated deity surrounded by an elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, and water buffalo, with two deer depicted below his stool.]

* Q3. The earliest archaeological evidence of an agricultural field ploughed with criss-cross furrows was discovered at which Harappan site?
(a) Harappa
(b) Kalibangan
(c) Lothal
(d) Chanhudaro
Correct Answer: (b) Kalibangan [Explanation: Excavations at Kalibangan in Rajasthan revealed a pre-Harappan ploughed agricultural field with grid furrow lines showing simultaneous dual-crop cultivation (mustard and gram).]

* Q4. In cuneiform inscriptions of the Akkadian Empire in ancient Mesopotamia, which geographical term was used to denote the Indus Valley region?
(a) Magan
(b) Dilmun
(c) Meluhha
(d) Punt
Correct Answer: (c) Meluhha [Explanation: Akkadian and Sumerian texts refer to maritime commerce with Meluhha (Indus region), Dilmun (Bahrain, trade intermediary), and Magan (Oman peninsula).]

* Q5. At which Harappan site was an exclusive craft workshop for bead manufacturing, devoid of any fortified citadel, excavated?
(a) Chanhudaro
(b) Surkotada
(c) Kot Diji
(d) Alamgirpur
Correct Answer: (a) Chanhudaro [Explanation: Chanhudaro in Sindh was a dedicated industrial and craft township without a protective citadel, specializing in steatite bead-making, seal carving, and shell ornaments.]
