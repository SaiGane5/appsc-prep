# Vijayanagar Empire: Administration, Nayankara System & Architecture


Founded in 1336 CE on the southern banks of the Tungabhadra River by Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty under the spiritual guidance of sage Vidyaranya, the Vijayanagar Empire stood as the supreme guardian of South Indian culture and autonomy. Reaching its zenith under Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529 CE), it combined military-feudal Nayankara governance with grand temple architectural complexes at Hampi (UNESCO site).

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Nayankara System: Military-feudal administrative mechanism where military chieftains (Nayakas / Amaranayakas) received territorial estates (Amaram) in exchange for maintaining troops, war horses, and paying annual tribute to the Rayas.

* Ayagar System: Traditional village autonomous administration governed by a council of 12 hereditary functionaries (Karnam, Talayari, blacksmith, potter, etc.) remunerated through tax-free village land grants (Manyams).

* Vijayanagar Temple Architecture: Grand pillared Mandapas, towering entrance gateways (Raya Gopurams), sculpted monolithic pillars depicting mythical rearing Yali beasts, and dedicated marriage halls (Kalyana Mandapa).

* Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi, 23 Jan 1565): Allied Deccani Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar) routed the Vijayanagar army under regent Aliya Rama Raya, leading to the sacking and abandonment of Hampi.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Vijayanagar emperor authored the celebrated Telugu epic poem 'Amuktamalyada' (detailing the life of Tamil Alvar saint Andal) and the Sanskrit play 'Jambavati Kalyanam'?
(a) Devaraya II
(b) Sri Krishnadevaraya
(c) Bukka I
(d) Rama Raya
Correct Answer: (b) Sri Krishnadevaraya [Explanation: Sri Krishnadevaraya was a versatile scholar-monarch who composed 'Amuktamalyada' (one of the 5 great Telugu mahakavyas) and 'Jambavati Kalyanam' in Sanskrit.]

* Q2. The catastrophic Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi), which resulted in the destruction and sacking of Vijayanagara, took place in which year?
(a) 1526 CE
(b) 1556 CE
(c) 1565 CE
(d) 1576 CE
Correct Answer: (c) 1565 CE [Explanation: On 23 January 1565, the combined armies of the Deccani Sultanates defeated Vijayanagar under regent Aliya Rama Raya at the Battle of Talikota (Rakshasi-Tangadi).]

* Q3. Who among the 'Ashtadiggajas' (eight literary gems in Krishnadevaraya's court) was honoured with the title 'Andhra Kavita Pitamaha' for his masterpiece 'Manucharitra'?
(a) Allasani Peddana
(b) Nandi Timmana
(c) Tenali Ramakrishna
(d) Dhurjati
Correct Answer: (a) Allasani Peddana [Explanation: Allasani Peddana, poet laureate of Krishnadevaraya's court, composed 'Svarochisha Manu Sambhavamu' (Manucharitra) and was acclaimed as 'Andhra Kavita Pitamaha'.]

* Q4. Persian ambassador Abdur Razzaq, who left an awe-inspired eyewitness account stating 'the eye has not seen, nor ear heard of any place to equal Vijayanagara', visited the court of which king in 1443 CE?
(a) Harihara II
(b) Devaraya II
(c) Krishnadevaraya
(d) Saluva Narasimha
Correct Answer: (b) Devaraya II [Explanation: Abdur Razzaq, envoy of Shah Rukh of Persia, visited Vijayanagara in 1443 CE during the reign of Sangama monarch Devaraya II (Gajabetekara).]

* Q5. In the Vijayanagar village administration, what was the hereditary system of twelve village functionaries remunerated through tax-free service land called?
(a) Nayankara System
(b) Ayagar System
(c) Poligar System
(d) Watandar System
Correct Answer: (b) Ayagar System [Explanation: The Ayagar system was the traditional village council of 12 hereditary officials (including headman, accountant, watchman) who administered rural justice and collected local dues.]
