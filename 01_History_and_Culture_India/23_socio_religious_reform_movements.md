# 19th Century Socio-Religious Reform Movements: Bengal, West & South India


The 19th century witnessed an intellectual and socio-religious awakening across India, often termed the 'Indian Renaissance'. Driven by western scientific rationalism and a critical re-evaluation of indigenous traditions, reform movements challenged caste hierarchy, untouchability, idol worship, and the subjugation of women (Sati, female infanticide, child marriage, and enforced widowhood).

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Major Socio-Religious Reform Organizations & Pioneers

| Reform Movement & Year | Founding Leader(s) | Core Doctrinal Philosophy | Social Crusades & Achievements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brahmo Samaj (1828) | Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Debendranath Tagore, Keshab Chandra Sen; Calcutta. | Monotheism; rejected idol worship, polytheism, and priestly mediation; synthesized Upanishadic monism with western ethical rationalism. | Crusade against Sati led to Bengal Sati Regulation XVII of 1829 under William Bentinck; published 'Sambad Kaumudi' and 'Mirat-ul-Akhbar'. |
| Arya Samaj (1875) | Swami Dayananda Saraswati; Bombay (headquarters shifted to Lahore). | 'Go back to the Vedas' (Vedas are infallible and self-revealed); denounced Purana myths, idol worship, child marriage, and hereditary caste; supported Shuddhi movement (reconversion). | Authored 'Satyarth Prakash'; established DAV (Dayanand Anglo-Vedic) schools and Gurukuls (Kangri); supported widow remarriage. |
| Ramakrishna Mission (1897) | Swami Vivekananda (Narendranath Datta); Belur Math, Bengal. | Practical Vedanta; synthesis of spiritual philosophy and service to suffering humanity ('Daridra Narayana' - service to Jiva is worship of Shiva). | Parliament of World's Religions at Chicago (1893); bridged Indian spirituality with western social work. |
| Satyashodhak Samaj (1873) | Jyotirao Govindrao Phule & Savitribai Phule; Pune, Maharashtra. | Anti-Brahminical crusade for social justice; liberation of Shudras, Ati-Shudras, and women from priestly deception. | Opened first girls' school in Pune (1848); authored 'Gulamgiri' (Slavery) and 'Shetkaryacha Asud'; Savitribai championed female education. |
| Aligarh Movement (1875) | Sir Syed Ahmad Khan; Aligarh, UP. | Rational interpretation of Quran; scientific modern education for Muslims; opposed religious dogmatism and purdah. | Founded Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College (1875, later AMU); published 'Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq'. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar: Principal of Sanskrit College; dedicated crusade for widow rehabilitation culminated in the Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act XV of 1856 under Lord Canning; opened 35 girls' schools in Bengal.

* Prarthana Samaj (1867): Founded in Bombay by Atmaram Pandurang with M.G. Ranade and R.G. Bhandarkar; focused on social reform rather than breaking from Hinduism; advocated inter-caste dining, widow remarriage, and depressed classes upliftment.

* Theosophical Society: Founded in New York (1875) by Madame Blavatsky and Col. Olcott; headquarters at Adyar, Madras (1886); popularized by Annie Besant who founded Central Hindu College at Varanasi (1898).

* Key Reform Legislation: Abolition of Sati (1829), Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856), Native Marriage / Civil Marriage Act (1872), Age of Consent Act (1891 - raised girl's marriage age to 12 under B.M. Malabari's crusade), and Sarda Act / Child Marriage Restraint Act (1929 - fixed minimum marriage age at 14 for girls and 18 for boys).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which British Governor-General enacted the historic Regulation XVII of December 1829 declaring the practice of Sati illegal and punishable as culpable homicide?
(a) Lord Cornwallis
(b) Lord William Bentinck
(c) Lord Dalhousie
(d) Lord Wellesley
Correct Answer: (b) Lord William Bentinck [Explanation: Supported by the tireless advocacy of Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Lord William Bentinck promulgated the Sati Regulation Act in 1829, banning Sati in Bengal.]

* Q2. The groundbreaking Marathi treatise 'Gulamgiri' (Slavery), dedicated to the American abolitionist movement against Negro slavery, was authored in 1873 by:
(a) B.R. Ambedkar
(b) Jyotirao Phule
(c) Gopal Hari Deshmukh ('Lokahitawadi')
(d) Mahadev Govind Ranade
Correct Answer: (b) Jyotirao Phule [Explanation: Mahatma Jyotirao Phule wrote 'Gulamgiri' in 1873, drawing a parallel between the oppression of lower-caste Shudras in India and African-American slaves in the US.]

* Q3. Who raised the stirring revivalist slogan 'Go back to the Vedas' and authored the seminal philosophical treatise 'Satyarth Prakash'?
(a) Swami Vivekananda
(b) Swami Dayananda Saraswati
(c) Sri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa
(d) Keshab Chandra Sen
Correct Answer: (b) Swami Dayananda Saraswati [Explanation: Swami Dayananda Saraswati, founder of the Arya Samaj (1875), authored 'Satyarth Prakash' and called for a return to the pure monotheistic teachings of the Vedas.]

* Q4. Through the relentless social crusade of Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar, which milestone social reform act was enacted into law in July 1856?
(a) Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856)
(b) Age of Consent Act
(c) Female Infanticide Prevention Act
(d) Special Marriage Act
Correct Answer: (a) Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (Act XV of 1856) [Explanation: Drafted under Dalhousie and passed by Lord Canning, Act XV of 1856 legalized the remarriage of Hindu widows, vindicating Vidyasagar's legal crusade.]

* Q5. Which social reformer and journalist published the reformist Urdu journal 'Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq' (Social Reformer) to promote modern scientific thinking among Indian Muslims?
(a) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
(b) Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
(c) Mirza Ghulam Ahmad
(d) Altaf Hussain Hali
Correct Answer: (b) Sir Syed Ahmad Khan [Explanation: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founded the journal 'Tahzib-ul-Akhlaq' in 1870 to awaken Indian Muslims to Western science, rationalism, and modern educational ethics.]
