# Mughal Empire: Administration, Mansabdari System, Art & Architecture


Founded in 1526 CE by Zahir-ud-din Babur following his victory at the First Battle of Panipat, the Mughal Empire forged a centralized pan-Indian state that dominated South Asia for over two centuries. Under Akbar the Great (1556–1605 CE), the empire institutionalized the Mansabdari administrative-military hierarchy, Todar Mal's Dahsala land revenue assessment, and a composite cultural ethos based on Sulh-i-Kul (universal peace), culminating in the architectural zenith under Shah Jahan.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Pillars of Mughal Imperial Administration & Culture

| Administrative / Cultural Tier | Institutional Mechanism & Functionaries | Operational Function & Historical Legacy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mansabdari System | Dual ranks: Zat (personal rank and salary) and Sawar (number of cavalrymen required to maintain). | Graded hierarchy of 66 ranks (from 10 to 10,000); non-hereditary military bureaucracy; paid in cash (Naqd) or through revenue assignments (Jagirs). |
| Agrarian & Revenue System (Zabt / Dahsala) | Raja Todar Mal (Finance Minister); Ain-i-Dahsala (1580 CE). | Assessed average produce and prices over the preceding 10 years; state demand fixed at 1/3rd in cash; land classified into Polaj (annually cultivated), Parauti (fallow 1–2 years), Chachar (fallow 3–4 years), and Banjar (uncultivated 5+ years). |
| Religious Policy & Diplomacy | Sulh-i-Kul (Universal Peace), Ibadat Khana (Fatehpur Sikri 1575), Mahzarnama / Infallibility Decree (1579), Din-i-Ilahi (1582). | Abolished Jizya (1564) and pilgrim taxes; forged strategic matrimonial alliances with Rajput clans (Amber, Bikaner); promoted merit-based governance across faiths. |
| Architecture & Miniature Painting | Humayun's Tomb (first double-dome garden tomb), Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza), Taj Mahal, Red Fort. | Synthesis of Persian, Central Asian, and Indian architectural idioms; Pietra dura inlay work; Jahangir patronized realistic miniature portraiture and naturalistic flora/fauna art (Ustad Mansur). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Provincial Administration (Subah): Empire divided into 15 Subahs (provinces) under Subahdar / Sipahsalar; Subahs subdivided into Sarkars (under Faujdar), Parganas (under Shiqdar), and Gramas (Muqaddams and Patwaris).

* Decline Dynamics: Aurangzeb's prolonged Deccan campaigns (1681–1707, 'the Deccan ulcer ruined Aurangzeb as the Spanish ulcer ruined Napoleon'); re-imposition of Jizya (1679); alienation of Rajputs, Marathas, and Sikhs; agrarian uprisings of Jats and Satnamis; and the Jagirdari crisis.

* Mughal Literary Achievements: Abul Fazl authored 'Akbarnama' and 'Ain-i-Akbari'; Babur composed memoirs 'Baburnama' (Tuzuk-i-Baburi in Chaghatai Turkic); Gulbadan Begum wrote 'Humayun-nama'; Dara Shikoh translated 50 Upanishads into Persian as 'Sirr-i-Akbar'.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. In the Mughal administrative apparatus, what did the dual ranking terms 'Zat' and 'Sawar' specifically signify in the Mansabdari system?
(a) Land revenue share and royal tax quota
(b) Personal status/salary rank and cavalry contingent quota
(c) Civil administrative rank and judicial authority
(d) Infantry strength and artillery division size
Correct Answer: (b) Personal status/salary rank and cavalry contingent quota [Explanation: In Akbar's Mansabdari system, 'Zat' determined the officer's personal status and salary, while 'Sawar' specified the exact number of cavalry troopers he was required to maintain.]

* Q2. The landmark 10-year rolling land revenue assessment system, 'Ain-i-Dahsala' (introduced in 1580 CE), was devised by which Mughal finance minister?
(a) Abul Fazl
(b) Raja Todar Mal
(c) Raja Man Singh
(d) Bairam Khan
Correct Answer: (b) Raja Todar Mal [Explanation: Raja Todar Mal designed the Dahsala system, which computed the average yield and prices over the preceding ten years to fix equitable state revenue demand.]

* Q3. In which year did Emperor Akbar abolish the discriminatory religious poll-tax 'Jizya' levied on non-Muslim subjects?
(a) 1556 CE
(b) 1564 CE
(c) 1575 CE
(d) 1582 CE
Correct Answer: (b) 1564 CE [Explanation: As part of his policy of Sulh-i-Kul and religious tolerance, Akbar abolished the pilgrim tax in 1563 and the Jizya in 1564, though Aurangzeb reimposed it in 1679.]

* Q4. Which Mughal prince translated 50 sacred Upanishads from Sanskrit into Persian under the title 'Sirr-i-Akbar' (The Great Secret)?
(a) Prince Khusrau
(b) Dara Shikoh
(c) Shah Shuja
(d) Murad Bakhsh
Correct Answer: (b) Dara Shikoh [Explanation: Eldest son of Shah Jahan, Dara Shikoh was a philosopher-prince who translated the Upanishads into Persian as 'Sirr-i-Akbar' and authored 'Majma-ul-Bahrain' (Mingling of the Two Oceans).]

* Q5. The magnificent architectural monument 'Buland Darwaza' at Fatehpur Sikri was constructed by Akbar to commemorate his victorious military conquest of:
(a) Bengal
(b) Malwa
(c) Gujarat
(d) Kashmir
Correct Answer: (c) Gujarat [Explanation: Akbar erected the massive 54-meter Buland Darwaza (Gate of Magnificence) at Fatehpur Sikri in 1573 to celebrate his conquest of the rich maritime province of Gujarat.]
