# Mauryan Empire: Centralized Administration, Arthashastra & Ashoka's Dhamma


Founded by Chandragupta Maurya in 322 BCE with the guidance of Chanakya (Kautilya), the Mauryan Empire established India's first subcontinental centralized administrative state. Reaching its zenith under Ashoka the Great (268–232 BCE), the empire combined rigorous bureaucratic control with an innovative paternalistic statecraft based on moral universalism ('Dhamma').

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Administrative Machinery of the Mauryas (Arthashastra & Indica)

| Departmental Tier | Key Institutional Functionaries | Administrative Role & Legal Jurisdiction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central Government | King & Mantriparishad; Saptanga State Theory (Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosa, Danda, Mitra). | Samaharta (Chief Tax Assessor & Collector), Sannidhata (Treasurer), Akshapataladhyaksha (Accountant General). |
| Espionage Network | Sanstha (Stationary spies) and Sanchara (Roving spies). | Gudhapurushas, Vishakanyas, and secret agents reporting directly to the emperor on bureaucratic integrity and public sentiment. |
| Military Organization | War Commission of 30 members divided into 6 boards of 5 members each. | Infantry, Cavalry, War Elephants, Chariots, Navy, and Military Transport/Commissariat. |
| Municipal Governance | City Commission of Pataliputra (30 members across 6 boards). | Regulated artisans, recorded births and deaths, licensed merchants, inspected trade weights, and collected 10% sales tax. |
| Provincial & Local | Kumara / Aryaputra (Royal Princes) governing provinces (Taxila, Ujjain, Suvarnagiri, Tosali). | Rajjukas (district land measurement and civil justice), Gopa (accountant of 5–10 villages), Gramika (village headman). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Ashoka's Policy of Dhamma: Rooted in moral universalism rather than sectarian Buddhism; aimed at civic harmony across diverse ethnic domains; denounced animal sacrifice, promoted filial piety, reverence for ascetics, kind treatment of servants, and inter-religious tolerance (Major Rock Edict XII).

* Kalinga War (261 BCE, Major Rock Edict XIII): Turning point that resulted in renouncing 'Bherighosha' (conquest by war) in favor of 'Dhammaghosha' (conquest by moral virtue).

* Ashokan Edicts Classification: 14 Major Rock Edicts, Minor Rock Edicts, 7 Pillar Edicts, and Cave Inscriptions; inscribed in Prakrit (Brahmi and Kharosthi scripts) as well as Greek and Aramaic (Kandahar bilingual edict).

* Mauryan Art & Architecture: Highly polished monolithic sandstone pillars (Chunar sandstone), Sarnath Lion Capital (National Emblem of India), rock-cut Barabar Caves (Sudama and Lomas Rishi caves gifted to Ajivikas), and Yaksha/Yakshini sculptures (Didarganj Yakshini).

### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Critically evaluate Ashoka's policy of Dhamma. Was it a state-promoted Buddhist religious propaganda or an enlightened socio-political instrument of imperial integration? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Define Dhamma through Ashoka's Rock Edicts (II, VII, XI, XII) emphasizing universal humanitarian ethics rather than specific Buddhist rituals; argue against sectarian propaganda: lack of mention of Nirvana, Four Noble Truths, or Eightfold Path; explain socio-political utility: integrating a sprawling empire of Greeks, tribal forest-dwellers, Buddhists, Brahmins, and Jains; conclude that Dhamma was an ideological cement maintaining political cohesion without military repression.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Major Rock Edict of Ashoka contains his famous call for religious tolerance and mutual respect among all sects ('Samavaya eva sadhuh')?
(a) Major Rock Edict V
(b) Major Rock Edict VIII
(c) Major Rock Edict XII
(d) Major Rock Edict XIII
Correct Answer: (c) Major Rock Edict XII [Explanation: Major Rock Edict XII is the landmark declaration of inter-faith tolerance, condemning self-praise of one's own sect and urging dialogue and mutual understanding across all religions.]

* Q2. In Kautilya's Arthashastra, which high-ranking official was designated as the Collector-General of Revenue responsible for fiscal planning and taxation?
(a) Sannidhata
(b) Samaharta
(c) Vyavaharika
(d) Pradeshta
Correct Answer: (b) Samaharta [Explanation: In the Mauryan administrative hierarchy, the Samaharta oversaw revenue assessment and tax collection, whereas the Sannidhata served as the custodian of the royal treasury.]

* Q3. The bilingual rock inscription of Ashoka written in both Greek and Aramaic scripts was discovered at which northwestern frontier site?
(a) Shahbazgarhi
(b) Mansehra
(c) Kandahar
(d) Taxila
Correct Answer: (c) Kandahar [Explanation: The Kandahar bilingual rock inscription in southern Afghanistan was carved in classical Greek and Aramaic to communicate Ashoka's Dhamma to local Hellenistic and Persian populations.]

* Q4. Megasthenes, the Seleucid ambassador to the court of Chandragupta Maurya, famously recorded in his 'Indica' that Indian society was divided into how many classes?
(a) Four classes
(b) Five classes
(c) Seven classes
(d) Nine classes
Correct Answer: (c) Seven classes [Explanation: Megasthenes classified Mauryan society into seven distinct functional groups: Philosophers, Farmers, Herdsmen/Hunters, Artisans/Traders, Soldiers, Overseers/Spies, and Councillors/Magistrates.]

* Q5. Which rock-cut caves, noted for their glass-like mirror polish on granite interiors, were gifted by Ashoka to the non-theistic Ajivika sect?
(a) Kanheri Caves
(b) Barabar Caves
(c) Udayagiri Caves
(d) Bagh Caves
Correct Answer: (b) Barabar Caves [Explanation: In the Barabar Hills near Gaya, Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha dedicated granite rock-cut shrines (such as the Lomas Rishi and Sudama caves) to ascetics of the Ajivika order.]
