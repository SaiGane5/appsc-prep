# Post-Mauryan Indigenous Dynasties: Sungas, Kanvas & Chedis


Following the assassination of the last Mauryan ruler Brihadratha in 185 BCE by his commander-in-chief Pushyamitra Sunga, indigenous Brahminical dynasties reasserted authority in northern and central India. The Sungas and Kanvas revived Vedic sacrificial rites while generously patronizing Buddhist art at Bharhut and Sanchi, while the Chedi dynasty of Kalinga reached imperial zenith under Emperor Kharavela.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Comparative Framework of Indigenous Post-Mauryan Dynasties

| Dynastic House | Founders & Prominent Rulers | Political & Military Feats | Artistic, Religious & Epigraphic Legacy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sunga Dynasty (c. 185–73 BCE) | Pushyamitra Sunga, Agnimitra, Bhagabhadra; Pataliputra & Vidisha. | Repelled Indo-Greek invasions led by Demetrius/Menander; performed two Ashvamedha sacrifices; revived Vedic rituals. | Patanjali authored 'Mahabhasya'; Agnimitra celebrated in Kalidasa's 'Malavikagnimitram'; Heliodorus Garuda Pillar at Vidisha; embellished Bharhut Stupa. |
| Kanva Dynasty (c. 73–28 BCE) | Vasudeva Kanva, Susarman; Pataliputra. | Overthrew the decadent Sunga ruler Devabhuti; brief 45-year rule over Magadha. | Maintained orthodox Brahminical administrative court until overthrown by the Deccan Satavahanas (Andhrabhrityas). |
| Chedi Dynasty of Kalinga (c. 1st Century BCE) | Mahameghavahana, Kharavela; Kalinganagara. | Conquered Magadha (retrieved Jina image stolen by Nandas), subdued Satavahana king Satakarni, routed Tamil confederacy. | Hathigumpha 17-line biographical inscription in Udayagiri; excavated rock-cut Jain caves at Udayagiri and Khandagiri (Rani Gumpha, Hathi Gumpha). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Pushyamitra Sunga & Buddhist Historiography: Early Buddhist texts (Divyavadana) portray Pushyamitra as a persecutor of monks, but archaeological evidence at Bharhut and Sanchi proves substantial royal and merchant patronage of stupa railings and gateways during the Sunga era.

* Besnagar Heliodorus Pillar: Greek ambassador Heliodorus (sent by Indo-Greek king Antialcidas to Sunga king Bhagabhadra) erected a monolithic stone Garuda pillar at Vidisha, identifying himself as a 'Bhagavata' (devotee of Vasudeva-Krishna).

* Hathigumpha Inscription: 17-line Prakrit Brahmi inscription of Kharavela recording his year-by-year military campaigns, irrigation repairs of canal dug by King Nanda 300 years prior, musical expertise, and building of Jain residential caves for monks.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Sunga commander-in-chief assassinated the last Mauryan ruler Brihadratha during an army parade in 185 BCE to establish the Sunga dynasty?
(a) Agnimitra
(b) Pushyamitra Sunga
(c) Vasumitra
(d) Bhagabhadra
Correct Answer: (b) Pushyamitra Sunga [Explanation: Pushyamitra Sunga, the Senapati (commander) of Brihadratha, executed a coup d'état during a military review in 185 BCE, founding the Sunga dynasty.]

* Q2. The famous monolithic stone pillar at Besnagar (Vidisha) with a Garuda capital was dedicated to Lord Vasudeva by which Greek ambassador?
(a) Megasthenes
(b) Deimachus
(c) Heliodorus
(d) Dionysius
Correct Answer: (c) Heliodorus [Explanation: Heliodorus, an envoy from Taxila sent by Indo-Greek King Antialcidas to Sunga King Bhagabhadra, erected the Besnagar pillar and declared himself a 'Bhagavata' (Vaishnavite).]

* Q3. The 17-line biographical Hathigumpha Inscription of King Kharavela is incised on the southern face of which hill near Bhubaneswar?
(a) Dhauli Hills
(b) Udayagiri Hills
(c) Khandagiri Hills
(d) Barabar Hills
Correct Answer: (b) Udayagiri Hills [Explanation: The Hathigumpha (Elephant Cave) inscription is located in the Udayagiri Hills near Bhubaneswar, Odisha, carved in the Brahmi script.]

* Q4. Which famous Sanskrit grammatical treatise was composed by sage Patanjali during the reign of Pushyamitra Sunga?
(a) Ashtadhyayi
(b) Mahabhasya
(c) Siddhanta Kaumudi
(d) Nirukta
Correct Answer: (b) Mahabhasya [Explanation: Patanjali composed the 'Mahabhasya' (a comprehensive commentary on Panini's Ashtadhyayi) during Pushyamitra Sunga's reign and participated in his Ashvamedha sacrifices.]

* Q5. Which play composed by celebrated dramatist Kalidasa is based on the romantic court intrigue between Sunga prince Agnimitra and Malavika?
(a) Abhijnanasakuntalam
(b) Vikramorvasiyam
(c) Malavikagnimitram
(d) Mudrarakshasa
Correct Answer: (c) Malavikagnimitram [Explanation: 'Malavikagnimitram' by Kalidasa dramatizes the love story of Sunga king Agnimitra and Malavika, offering historical glimpses of Sunga-Yavana conflicts.]
