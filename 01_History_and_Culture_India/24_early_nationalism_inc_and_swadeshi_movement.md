# Early Nationalism: Foundation of INC, Moderates, Extremists & Swadeshi


The final quarter of the 19th century witnessed the organized institutionalization of Indian nationalism. Beginning with pre-Congress regional associations and culminating in the founding of the Indian National Congress in 1885, Indian politics evolved through the constitutional agitation of the Moderates, the radical mass mobilization of the Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal), and the explosive mass protests of the Swadeshi and Boycott Movement (1905–1908).

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Ideological & Methodological Comparison: Moderates vs Extremists

| Dimension | Moderate Nationalists (1885–1905) | Extremist / Radical Nationalists (1905–1918) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prominent Leaders | Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, W.C. Bonnerjee, Surendranath Banerjea. | Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai ('Lal-Bal-Pal'), Aurobindo Ghosh. |
| Political Objective | Self-government within the British Empire (Dominion Status); administrative reforms, expansion of legislative councils. | Swaraj (complete self-rule/independence); Tilak declared: 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it!'. |
| Methods of Agitation | Constitutional agitation within the law; 3 P's: Petitions, Prayers, and Protests; believing in British sense of justice. | Direct mass action; Passive resistance; Boycott of British goods and government institutions; Promotion of Swadeshi and National Education. |
| Social Base & Appeal | Narrow urban educated middle-class elite, lawyers, journalists, wealthy merchants. | Broad mass base including youth, students, lower middle class, urban artisans, and rural peasantry. |
| Cultural Philosophy | Anglophile orientation; drew inspiration from Western liberal philosophers (Mill, Burke, Spencer). | Pride in ancient Indian cultural heritage; revived Ganapati and Shivaji festivals (Tilak); religious symbolism (Durga, Vande Mataram). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Pre-Congress Bodies: Landholders' Society (1838), British Indian Association (1851), Indian Association of Calcutta (1876, Surendranath Banerjea and Anandamohan Bose), Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870, M.G. Ranade), Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884), Bombay Presidency Association (1885).

* Foundation of INC (1885): Founded by retired British civil servant A.O. Hume; First Session held at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay (28–31 Dec 1885) under President W.C. Bonnerjee with 72 delegates; 'Safety Valve Theory' proposed by Lala Lajpat Rai vs 'Lightning Conductor' reality.

* Partition of Bengal (16 Oct 1905): Enacted by Viceroy Lord Curzon on administrative grounds but strategically intended to drive a religious wedge between Hindu-majority West Bengal and Muslim-majority East Bengal.

* Swadeshi & Boycott Movement: Rabindranath Tagore composed 'Amar Sonar Bangla' and led Raksha Bandhan rituals; boycott of Manchester cloth and Liverpool salt; National Council of Education established (1906, Bengal National College with Aurobindo Ghosh as principal); chemical industrialization by Acharya P.C. Ray (Bengal Chemicals).

* Surat Split (1907): Fierce confrontation between Moderates (led by Rash Behari Ghosh) and Extremists (Tilak) over extending Swadeshi nationwide, causing an open rupture that weakened Congress for a decade.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Who presided over the historic First Session of the Indian National Congress held at Bombay in December 1885?
(a) Allan Octavian Hume
(b) Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee
(c) Dadabhai Naoroji
(d) Badruddin Tyabji
Correct Answer: (b) Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee [Explanation: W.C. Bonnerjee was elected president of the inaugural INC session at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, attended by 72 delegates.]

* Q2. On which date did the Partition of Bengal take effect, observed across Bengal as a day of national mourning ('Day of Raksha Bandhan' and fasting)?
(a) 15 August 1905
(b) 16 October 1905
(c) 11 December 1905
(d) 1 January 1906
Correct Answer: (b) 16 October 1905 [Explanation: The Partition of Bengal took effect on 16 October 1905; crowds tied Rakhis as a symbol of unshakeable Bengali unity and sang 'Amar Sonar Bangla'.]

* Q3. The famous Surat Split of the Indian National Congress in 1907 took place under the presidency of which Moderate leader?
(a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale
(b) Rash Behari Ghosh
(c) Pherozeshah Mehta
(d) Surendranath Banerjea
Correct Answer: (b) Rash Behari Ghosh [Explanation: The 1907 Surat session, presided over by Dr. Rash Behari Ghosh, dissolved into chaos and ended with the formal expulsion of the Extremists led by Tilak.]

* Q4. Which nationalist pioneer established the popular public festivals of Ganapati (1893) and Shivaji (1895) to mobilize mass political consciousness against colonial rule?
(a) Lala Lajpat Rai
(b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
(c) Bipin Chandra Pal
(d) V.D. Savarkar
Correct Answer: (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak [Explanation: Lokmanya Tilak transformed the domestic religious festivals of Ganeshotsav and Shiv Jayanti into vibrant public platforms for patriotic mobilization.]

* Q5. Who was the first Muslim President of the Indian National Congress, presiding over the Third Session at Madras in 1887?
(a) Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
(b) Badruddin Tyabji
(c) Rahimtulla M. Sayani
(d) Abul Kalam Azad
Correct Answer: (b) Badruddin Tyabji [Explanation: Badruddin Tyabji served as the first Muslim President of the INC at the Madras session in 1887, urging Indian Muslims to unite with their fellow countrymen.]
