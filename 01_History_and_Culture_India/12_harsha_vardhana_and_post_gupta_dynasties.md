# Harsha Vardhana, Kanauj Assembly & Post-Gupta Formations


Following the collapse of the Gupta Empire under Huna invasions, political unity fragmented into regional successor kingdoms: the Pushyabhutis of Thanesar, the Maukharies of Kanauj, the Maitrakas of Vallabhi, and the Gaudas of Bengal. Harsha Vardhana (606–647 CE) reunited northern India, shifted his imperial capital to Kanauj, patronized Mahayana Buddhism, and hosted Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang).

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The Battle of Narmada (c. 618 CE): Harsha attempted to conquer the Deccan but was decisively defeated on the banks of the Narmada River by Western Chalukya King Pulakesin II of Badami, as recorded in the Aihole Inscription ('Harsha, whose joy melted away in fear').

* Kanauj Religious Assembly (643 CE): Grand ecumenical debate hosted by Harsha to publicize Mahayana Buddhism, presided over by Xuanzang; attended by 20 tributary kings, 4,000 monks, and 3,000 Brahmins and Jains.

* Prayag Mahamoksha Parishad: Quinquennial assembly held every five years at the confluence of Ganga and Yamuna, where Harsha emptied his treasury by giving away all accumulated royal wealth in charity to ascetics, orphans, and the poor.

* Xuanzang's Travelogue ('Si-Yu-Ki'): Documented caste rigidity, untouchability, trial by ordeal, widespread robbery (he was robbed twice), Nalanda's scholarly rigour under chancellor Silabhadra, and Harsha's division of royal revenue into four equal shares.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Chalukyan monarch halted the southern imperial expansion of Harsha Vardhana at the Narmada River, as recorded in the Aihole Inscription?
(a) Pulakesin I
(b) Pulakesin II
(c) Vikramaditya I
(d) Kirtivarman I
Correct Answer: (b) Pulakesin II [Explanation: The Aihole Prashasti, composed by court poet Ravikirti, records that Western Chalukya King Pulakesin II defeated Harsha on the banks of the Narmada around 618 CE.]

* Q2. Who was the chancellor (head) of the international Nalanda University when Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) studied Buddhist philosophy there?
(a) Dharmapala
(b) Silabhadra
(c) Chandragomin
(d) Vasubandhu
Correct Answer: (b) Silabhadra [Explanation: The venerable Buddhist scholar Acharya Silabhadra was the supreme head (Kulapati) of Nalanda Mahavihara who taught Yogacara philosophy to Xuanzang.]

* Q3. Which three celebrated Sanskrit dramas were authored by Emperor Harsha Vardhana himself?
(a) Malavikagnimitram, Vikramorvasiyam, Sakuntalam
(b) Ratnavali, Priyadarshika, Nagananda
(c) Mudrarakshasa, Devichandraguptam, Mricchakatika
(d) Mahaviracharita, Malatimadhava, Uttararamacharita
Correct Answer: (b) Ratnavali, Priyadarshika, Nagananda [Explanation: Harsha authored three classical Sanskrit plays: 'Ratnavali' and 'Priyadarshika' (romantic court comedies) and 'Nagananda' (Buddhist drama based on the self-sacrifice of Jimutavahana).]

* Q4. Court poet Banabhatta, who adorned the royal court of Harsha, authored which foundational Sanskrit biographical work?
(a) Rajatarangini
(b) Harshacharita
(c) Kumarapalacharita
(d) Vikramankadevacharita
Correct Answer: (b) Harshacharita [Explanation: Banabhatta composed the 'Harshacharita' (the first formal historical biography in classical Sanskrit) and the celebrated romantic prose romance 'Kadambari'.]

* Q5. According to Xuanzang's accounts, into how many equal shares did Emperor Harsha divide the royal revenues of the state?
(a) Two parts
(b) Three parts
(c) Four parts
(d) Six parts
Correct Answer: (c) Four parts [Explanation: Xuanzang recorded that Harsha divided state revenues into four quarters: one for state administration, one for salaries of public servants, one for rewarding intellectual talent, and one for charity to religious orders.]
