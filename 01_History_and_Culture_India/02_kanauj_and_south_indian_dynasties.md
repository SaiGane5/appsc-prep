## The Kanauj Hegemony and the South Indian Imperial Dynasties: Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Hoysalas, Yadavas, Kakatiyas, and Reddis

The post-Gupta era witnessed the geopolitical shift of imperial supremacy from Pataliputra to Kanauj (Mahodaya) in northern India, accompanied by the tripartite struggle. Simultaneously, peninsular South India flourished as an autonomous epicenter of temple architecture, naval power, local village democracy, and regional vernacular literature under the Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Hoysalas, Yadavas, Kakatiyas, and the Reddi kingdoms.

### 1. The Kanauj Hegemony: Harshavardhana & The Tripartite Struggle


*Evolution of Imperial Power at Kanauj*

| Phase / Empire | Key Rulers & Proponents | Administrative, Cultural & Strategic Contributions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harshavardhana (Pushyabhuti Dynasty, 606–647 CE) | Harsha (Siladitya); recorded by Banabhatta (Harshacharita) and Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang). | Unified northern India; patronized Nalanda Mahavihara; convened the Kanauj Religious Assembly and quinquennial Prayag Mahamokshaparishad; authored Sanskrit plays (Ratnavali, Priyadarsika, Nagananda); halted at Narmada by Pulakeshin II. |
| The Tripartite Struggle (8th–10th Century CE) | Gurjara-Pratiharas (Vatsaraja, Nagabhata II, Bhoja I), Palas of Bengal (Dharmapala, Devapala), Rashtrakutas of Deccan (Dhruva, Govinda III). | Two-century battle for control of Kanauj and the fertile upper Gangetic agricultural revenues; Gurjara-Pratiharas eventually established lasting control under Mihira Bhoja. |


### 2. The Great Deccan Empires: Badami Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas & Kalyani Chalukyas


*Architectural and Literary Achievements of Deccan Dynasties*

| Dynasty & Capital | Paramount Rulers | Architectural Milestones & Monumental Legacies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Badami Chalukyas (Vatapi, 6th–8th C) | Pulakeshin I, Pulakeshin II (defeated Harsha in 618 CE, commemorated in Aihole Inscription by court poet Ravikirti). | Cradle of temple architecture: Vesara style rock-cut and structural temples at Aihole (Lad Khan, Durga temple), Badami cave temples, and Pattadakal (Virupaksha Temple built by Queen Lokamahadevi, UNESCO World Heritage). |
| Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi (7th–11th C) | Kubja Vishnuvardhana (brother of Pulakeshin II), Gunaga Vijayaditya, Rajaraja Narendra. | Ruled coastal Andhra from Vengi (and later Rajahmundry); synthesis of Telugu culture; patronized Nannaya Bhattaraka who commenced the monumental Telugu translation of the Mahabharata (Andhra Mahabharatam). |
| Rashtrakutas of Manyakheta (8th–10th C) | Dantidurga (founder), Krishna I, Amoghavarsha I (Kavirajamarga), Indra III, Krishna III. | Monolithic rock-cut Kailasanatha Temple at Ellora (Cave 16) carved top-down from a single basalt cliff under Krishna I; Jain rock-cut temples; Amoghavarsha wrote Kavirajamarga (earliest Kannada work on poetics). |
| Western Chalukyas of Kalyani (10th–12th C) | Tailapa II, Someshvara I, Vikramaditya VI (patron of Bilhana, author of Vikramankadevacharita, and Vijnaneshvara, author of Mitakshara legal treatise). | Elaborate soapstone (chloritic schist) temples featuring star-shaped (stellate) plans at Gadag, Lakkundi, and Itagi (Mahadeva temple - 'Emperor of Temples'). |


### 3. The Imperial Cholas: Maritime Empire & Village Local Autonomy

- Imperial Expansion & Maritime Supremacy: Founded by Vijayalaya; transformed into an Indian Ocean maritime empire under Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE) and Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE). Conquered Sri Lanka (Anuradhapura), Maldives, and defeated the Srivijaya maritime empire (Sumatra, Malay peninsula) in 1025 CE to secure trade routes to China. Rajendra I assumed the title 'Gangaikondachola' after marching to the Ganges.
- Local Self-Government & Uttaramerur Inscription: Detailed in the two inscriptions of Parantaka I (919 and 921 CE) at Uttaramerur. The village assembly (Sabha / Mahasabha of Brahmadeya villages) operated through autonomous specialized committees (Variyams: Eri-variyam for irrigation tanks, Thotta-variyam for gardens, Pon-variyam for gold/finances).
- Kudavolai Electoral System: Candidates elected via secret ballot by drawing palm-leaf chits from an earthen pot. Strict qualification rules: ownership of minimum land (1/4th Veli), own house, age 35–70, Vedic learning; disqualification for embezzling committee funds or moral crimes.
- Dravidian Architecture & Bronze Casting: Perfection of Dravidian temple vimanas, exemplified by the Brihadisvara (Rajarajeswara) Temple at Thanjavur (66-meter monolithic granite vimana, 80-ton sikhara dome) and Gangaikondacholisvaram; world-famous Nataraja bronzes cast via the cire perdue (lost-wax) process.


### 4. Medieval Successor Kingdoms: Hoysalas, Yadavas, Kakatiyas & Reddis


*Regional Kingdoms of the Deccan and South India (12th–14th Centuries)*

| Kingdom & Seat | Prominent Rulers | Administrative, Agrarian & Cultural Achievements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hoysalas of Dvarasamudra (Halebidu) | Vishnuvardhana, Veera Ballala II | Exquisite soapstone stellate temples covered in multi-layered friezes of elephants, lions, dancers, and epic reliefs (Chennakeshava Temple at Belur, Hoysaleswara at Halebidu, Keshava at Somnathpura - UNESCO World Heritage 2023). |
| Yadavas (Seunas) of Devagiri | Bhillama V, Simhana II, Ramachandra | Controlled northern Deccan and Maharashtra; impregnable hill fort at Devagiri; patronized Sanskrit scholar Sarangadeva (Sangita Ratnakara, seminal treatise on Indian classical music) and Hemadri (author of Chaturvarga Chintamani). |
| Kakatiyas of Orugallu (Warangal) | Prola II, Rudradeva, Ganapatideva, Rani Rudrama Devi, Prataparudra II | Pioneered chain-tank irrigation networks (Ramappa, Pakhal, Laknavaram lakes); patronized foreign merchant trade (Motupalli charter of Ganapatideva); built Ramappa Temple (Palampet, UNESCO site featuring floating bricks and sandbox foundation) and Thousand Pillar Temple; visited by Venetian traveler Marco Polo. |
| Reddis of Kondaveedu & Rajahmundry | Prolaya Vema Reddi, Anavota Reddi, Kumaragiri Reddi, Kataya Vema Reddi | Resisted the Delhi Sultanate post-Kakatiya fall; revived Hindu traditions; fortified Kondaveedu and Kondapalli; patronized Telugu poet Srinatha (Kavi Sarvabhauma) and Kumaragiri's dance treatise Vasantarajiya; celebrated Vasantotsavams. |


### Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


#### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Explain the salient features of the local self-government system under the Cholas with special reference to the Uttaramerur Inscriptions of Parantaka I. How did the Kudavolai system ensure democratic accountability? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Introduce Parantaka I's inscriptions (919 and 921 CE) in Kanchipuram district; detail village assembly types (Ur for general villages, Sabha for Brahmadeya villages, Nagaram for trade guilds); describe the operational committee system (Variyams for water tanks, gardens, auditing); outline Kudavolai lot selection (palm leaf chits in pot drawn by innocent child); discuss rigid eligibility (tax-paying land ownership, Vedic scholarship, age limits) and disqualification provisions (corruption, failure to submit accounts); evaluate democratic ethos vs. caste-based exclusion.
2. Discuss the socio-economic and architectural contributions of the Kakatiya dynasty to the Telugu country. How did their irrigation tank systems transform dryland agriculture? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Outline Kakatiya territorial consolidation under Ganapatideva and Rani Rudrama Devi; detail their revolutionary engineering of interconnected cascade tank systems (Ramappa, Pakhal, Laknavaram) turning the drought-prone Telangana/Rayalaseema scrub plateau into productive paddy heartlands; evaluate the Motupalli Inscription granting protective charters to foreign maritime traders (Abhaya Sasanam); examine architectural hallmarks of Ramappa Temple (sandbox foundations, floating lightweight porous bricks, dynamic bracket figures) and the Warangal fort toranas (Kakatiya Thoranam).
3. Trace the political origins and cultural significance of the Tripartite Struggle for Kanauj. Why did control over Kanauj become the paramount symbol of imperial suzerainty in northern India? (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Explain geopolitical transition from Pataliputra to Kanauj (Harsha's legacy as capital of Uttarpatha); evaluate geographic and economic value of Kanauj (controlling upper Gangetic trade and agricultural revenue); outline the 200-year conflict among Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas, and Rashtrakutas; examine how this perpetual warfare exhausted all three powers, leaving northern India fragmented and vulnerable to Mahmud of Ghazni's raids.


#### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

- Q1. The monolithic rock-cut Kailasanatha Temple at Ellora (Cave 16), carved entirely from top to bottom out of a single rock cliff, was commissioned by which Rashtrakuta king?
(a) Dantidurga
(b) Krishna I
(c) Govinda III
(d) Amoghavarsha I
Correct Answer: (b) Krishna I [Explanation: Krishna I (8th century CE) commissioned the monolithic rock-cut Shiva temple of Kailasanatha at Ellora, one of the greatest engineering feats of ancient world rock architecture.]
- Q2. The Motupalli Inscription (Abhaya Sasanam), which granted safety and liberal customs duties to overseas maritime merchants sailing from foreign lands, was issued by which Kakatiya ruler?
(a) Prola II
(b) Rudradeva
(c) Ganapatideva
(d) Prataparudra II
Correct Answer: (c) Ganapatideva [Explanation: King Ganapatideva issued the famous Motupalli Charter in Guntur/Bapatla coast guaranteeing protection and fixed 1/30th customs duties to foreign merchants trading in pearls, horses, and silks.]
- Q3. The famous Aihole Inscription, which extols the military conquests of Pulakeshin II over Harshavardhana on the banks of the Narmada, was composed by which court poet?
(a) Banabhatta
(b) Ravikirti
(c) Harishena
(d) Bilhana
Correct Answer: (b) Ravikirti [Explanation: Ravikirti, a Jain poet and court scholar of Badami Chalukya king Pulakeshin II, authored the Aihole Prashasti in 634 CE, comparing himself to Kalidasa and Bharavi.]
- Q4. Which Chola emperor constructed the new capital city of Gangaikondacholapuram and led successful naval expeditions across the Bay of Bengal conquering Srivijaya (Sumatra and Malaya)?
(a) Rajaraja I
(b) Rajendra I
(c) Kulottunga I
(d) Parantaka I
Correct Answer: (b) Rajendra I [Explanation: Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE) earned the title 'Gangaikondachola' after his northern military campaign to the Ganges and launched a victorious naval fleet conquering the Kadaram/Srivijaya maritime empire.]
- Q5. The celebrated Telugu poet Srinatha, honored with the title 'Kavi Sarvabhauma' and showered with gold coins (Kanakabhishekam), flourished under the royal patronage of which dynasty?
(a) Eastern Chalukyas
(b) Kakatiyas of Warangal
(c) Reddis of Kondaveedu and Rajahmundry
(d) Aravidu dynasty
Correct Answer: (c) Reddis of Kondaveedu and Rajahmundry [Explanation: Srinatha was the chief court poet of the Reddi kings of Kondaveedu and Rajahmundry (specifically Pedakomati Vema Reddi and Veerabhadra Reddi).]
