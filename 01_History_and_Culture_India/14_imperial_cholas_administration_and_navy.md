# Imperial Cholas: Administration, Uttaramerur Inscription & Maritime Navy


The Imperial Chola dynasty (c. 850–1279 CE), founded by Vijayalaya, established the most sophisticated administrative state and maritime empire in pre-colonial South Asia. Under Rajaraja I and Rajendra I, Chola naval fleets dominated the Indian Ocean, conquered Sri Lanka, and raided the Srivijaya maritime empire in Southeast Asia, while pioneering a democratic village autonomy system documented in the Uttaramerur Inscriptions.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Structural Pillars of Chola Imperial Statecraft

| Governance Dimension | Institutional Mechanism | Operational Functions & Historical Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central Administration | King guided by royal decrees (Tirumukham); Perundanam (higher officials) & Sirudanam (lower officials). | Conducted extensive cadastral land revenue surveys (under Rajaraja I in 1001 CE); revenue collected as Kadamai (1/3rd gross produce). |
| Local Village Autonomy | Three village assemblies: Ur (general village), Sabha/Mahasabha (Brahmin agrahara villages), Nagaram (merchants). | Uttaramerur Inscriptions (Parantaka I, 919 & 921 CE) outline Kudavolai (pot-ticket lottery) election system, strict eligibility criteria, and disqualification for financial corruption. |
| Village Committees (Variyams) | Executive committees operating under the Sabha: Samvatsara-variyam (annual council), Eri-variyam (tank committee), Thotta-variyam (garden committee). | Managed irrigation tanks, collected village taxes, maintained local justice and land dispute resolutions independently. |
| Naval Supremacy & Overseas Trade | Imperial Chola Navy ('Bay of Bengal as a Chola Lake'); merchant guilds (Manigramam, Ayyavole 500, Nanadesi). | Conquered Anuradhapura (Sri Lanka), Maldives; Rajendra I sent naval expeditions across the Malacca Straits conquering Kedah and Srivijaya (Sumatra/Malaya) in 1025 CE. |
| Dravidian Architecture & Bronze Casting | Brihadisvara Temple (Thanjavur), Gangaikondacholapuram, Airavatesvara Temple (Darasuram). | Grand Dravidian vimana towers; lost-wax (cire perdue) casting of Nataraja (cosmic dancing Shiva) bronzes recognized as world art masterpieces. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Uttaramerur Inscriptions (919 and 921 CE, Parantaka I): Inscribed on the Vaikuntha Perumal temple; details democratic ward elections across 30 kudumbus (wards); candidates had to own land, possess Vedic learning, be between 35 and 70 years old, and have not served on a committee in the preceding 3 years.

* Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE): Built the 216-foot Brihadisvara (Rajarajesvaram) Temple at Thanjavur, crowned with an 80-ton monolithic granite cupola; compiled the Shaivite hymns into 'Tirumurai' through Nambi Andar Nambi.

* Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE): Marched armies to the Ganga, assumed title 'Gangaikondachola' and built new capital Gangaikondacholapuram; excavated the vast irrigation reservoir Cholagangam (16 miles long); launched historic trans-oceanic naval war against the Srivijaya Empire.

* Chola Bronze Sculptures: Cosmic Nataraja balance of creation (damaru drum), destruction (agni flame), protection (abhaya mudra), and illusion (crushing demon Apasmara Purusha).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. The famous Uttaramerur Inscriptions, which provide a detailed constitutional blueprint of village assemblies and committee elections, belong to the reign of:
(a) Vijayalaya
(b) Parantaka I
(c) Rajaraja I
(d) Rajendra I
Correct Answer: (b) Parantaka I [Explanation: The two landmark inscriptions at Uttaramerur in Kanchipuram district were issued in the 12th and 14th regnal years of Chola King Parantaka I (919 and 921 CE).]

* Q2. Which Chola monarch assumed the proud title 'Gangaikondachola' (Conqueror of the Ganga) and founded a new imperial capital to commemorate his northern campaign?
(a) Rajaraja I
(b) Rajendra I
(c) Kulottunga I
(d) Rajadhiraja I
Correct Answer: (b) Rajendra I [Explanation: After his victorious military expedition to the banks of the River Ganga defeating Mahipala of Bengal, Rajendra I assumed the title 'Gangaikondachola' and founded Gangaikondacholapuram.]

* Q3. In the Chola village administrative machinery, which specialized committee ('Variyam') was specifically entrusted with the maintenance and desiltation of irrigation tanks?
(a) Samvatsara-variyam
(b) Eri-variyam
(c) Thotta-variyam
(d) Pon-variyam
Correct Answer: (b) Eri-variyam [Explanation: The Eri-variyam was the village tank committee responsible for maintaining irrigation reservoirs, water distribution, desilting canals, and repairing bund breaches.]

* Q4. The colossal Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur, completed in 1010 CE and celebrated for its towering 216-foot Dravidian Vimana, was dedicated to Lord Shiva by:
(a) Aditya I
(b) Rajaraja I
(c) Rajendra Chola I
(d) Sundara Chola
Correct Answer: (b) Rajaraja I [Explanation: Great Chola monarch Rajaraja I commissioned the magnificent Brihadisvara Temple (Peruvudaiyar Kovil) at Thanjavur, a UNESCO World Heritage Great Living Chola Temple.]

* Q5. Which famous merchant guild operated as an autonomous trans-oceanic corporate trading network across South India and Southeast Asia during the Chola era?
(a) Sreni
(b) Manigramam and Ayyavole 500
(c) Mahajan
(d) Gopa
Correct Answer: (b) Manigramam and Ayyavole 500 [Explanation: The Manigramam, Ayyavole 500 (Ainurruvar), and Nanadesi merchant corporations commanded extensive maritime networks trading spices, camphor, textiles, and gems across Southeast Asia.]
