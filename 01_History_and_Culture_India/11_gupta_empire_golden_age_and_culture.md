# The Gupta Empire: Classical Age, Administration, Art & Science


The Gupta Empire (c. 319–550 CE), established by Sri Gupta and consolidated by Chandragupta I, is traditionally celebrated as the 'Golden Age' or 'Classical Period' of ancient India. The era was distinguished by political unification across Aryavarta, decentralized provincial governance, the flowering of Sanskrit literature (Kalidasa), revolutionary breakthroughs in mathematics and astronomy (Aryabhata), and the transition from rock-cut shrines to free-standing stone temple architecture.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Samudragupta (c. 335–375 CE, 'Indian Napoleon'): Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) composed by court poet Harisena in champu style; military strategy: Digvijaya and Prasabhoddharana (violent extermination of 9 North Indian rulers) vs Grahana-Mokshanugraha (capture and release of 12 South Indian kings upon tribute).

* Chandragupta II Vikramaditya (c. 375–415 CE): Defeated the Western Kshatrapas, annexing Malwa and Gujarat; established second capital at Ujjain; court graced by 'Navaratnas' (Nine Gems: Kalidasa, Amarasimha, Varahamihira, Dhanvantari, etc.); Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien recorded peaceful governance without capital punishment.

* Administration & Feudal Transition: Decentralized empire divided into Bhuktis (provinces under Uparikas) and Vishayas (districts under Vishayapatis); proliferation of religious and secular land grants; decline in urban monetary trade, and emergence of earliest epigraphically recorded Sati at Eran (510 CE, Bhanugupta's reign).

* Nalanda Mahavihara: Founded by Kumaragupta I (Mahendraditya) in the 5th century CE; grew into Asia's premier international Buddhist university.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. The Prayag Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription), detailing the military conquests of Samudragupta, was composed in classical Sanskrit by his court poet:
(a) Kalidasa
(b) Harisena
(c) Ravikirti
(d) Banabhatta
Correct Answer: (b) Harisena [Explanation: Harisena, court poet and minister of Samudragupta, composed the 33-line Prayag Prashasti in chaste Sanskrit champu kavya style on the Ashokan pillar at Allahabad.]

* Q2. In his monumental astronomical treatise 'Aryabhatiya' (499 CE), Aryabhata proposed which revolutionary scientific concept centuries before European astronomers?
(a) Elliptical planetary orbits
(b) Earth rotates daily on its own axis
(c) Gravitational attraction of water
(d) Speed of light
Correct Answer: (b) Earth rotates daily on its own axis [Explanation: Aryabhata stated that the starry sphere is stationary and the earth rotates daily on its axis ('prana'), causing the illusion of rising and setting celestial bodies.]

* Q3. The earliest inscriptional reference to the self-immolation of a widow (Sati) in Indian history, dated to 510 CE, was discovered at which site in Madhya Pradesh?
(a) Deogarh
(b) Eran
(c) Bhitargaon
(d) Mandasor
Correct Answer: (b) Eran [Explanation: The Eran stone pillar inscription of King Bhanugupta (510 CE) records that Goparaja, a royal general, died in battle and his devoted wife cremated herself on his funeral pyre.]

* Q4. Which Gupta emperor established the world-renowned Buddhist monastic university at Nalanda in modern Bihar?
(a) Chandragupta II
(b) Kumaragupta I
(c) Skandagupta
(d) Purugupta
Correct Answer: (b) Kumaragupta I [Explanation: Kumaragupta I (bearing the title Mahendraditya) founded the celebrated Nalanda Mahavihara, which later flourished under Harsha and the Pala kings.]

* Q5. Which Chinese Buddhist pilgrim traveled throughout northern India during the reign of Chandragupta II Vikramaditya and documented the peaceful social conditions?
(a) Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)
(b) Faxian (Fa-Hien)
(c) Yijing (I-Tsing)
(d) Song Yun
Correct Answer: (b) Faxian (Fa-Hien) [Explanation: Faxian (Fa-Hien) visited India between 399 and 412 CE during Chandragupta II's reign, noting that people were prosperous, vegetarian, and governed without torture or capital punishment.]
