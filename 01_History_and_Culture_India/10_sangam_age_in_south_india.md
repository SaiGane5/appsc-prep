# The Sangam Age: Polity, Society, Trade & Classical Tamil Literature


The Sangam Age (c. 3rd Century BCE to 3rd Century CE) represents the classical literary and state-formation epoch of ancient Tamilakam (modern Tamil Nadu and Kerala). Named after the literary academies ('Sangams') held under the royal patronage of the Pandyas at Madurai, the era was characterized by the 'Muvendar' (Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas), an ecological taxonomy of five landscapes (Aintinai), and intense maritime commerce with the Roman Empire.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: The Five Ecological Landscapes (Aintinai) of Tamilakam

| Thinai (Landscape) | Geographical Ecology | Primary Deity | Economic Occupation | Inhabitants / Tribe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kurinji | Hilly / Mountainous forests | Murugan (Seyon) | Hunting and honey collection | Kanavar, Kuravar |
| Mullai | Pastoral scrublands & grasslands | Mayon (Vishnu / Krishna) | Cattle-rearing and dairy farming | Ayars, Idaiyars |
| Marudham | Fertile river valleys & plains | Vendan (Indra) | Paddy agriculture and irrigation | Ulavar, Vellalar |
| Neydhal | Coastal seashore & lagoons | Varunan (Kadal-on) | Fishing and salt manufacturing | Paratavar, Meenavar |
| Palai | Arid / Parched wasteland | Korravai (Goddess of War) | Highway robbery and cattle raiding | Maravar, Eyinar |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The Three Royal Houses (Muvendar): Cheras (emblem: Bow; port: Muziris; capital: Vanji; ruler: Senguttuvan who introduced Pattini/Kannagi cult); Cholas (emblem: Tiger; port: Puhar/Kaveripattinam; capital: Uraiyur; ruler: Karikala who built Kallanai dam); Pandyas (emblem: Fish; port: Korkai; capital: Madurai; famous for pearl fisheries and patronizing the Sangams).

* Sangam Literary Corpus: Tolkappiyam (author: Tolkappiyar, earliest grammar and poetics); Pathinenmelkanakku: Ettuthokai (8 anthologies including Agananuru and Purananuru) and Pattupattu (10 idylls); Pathinenkilkanakku (18 ethical works including Tiruvalluvar's Tirukkural).

* Twin Tamil Epics: Silappadikaram ('The Tale of an Anklet' by Ilango Adigal, story of Kovalan, Kannagi, and Madhavi) and Manimekalai (by Sattanar, Buddhist sequel detailing the life of Kovalan's daughter).

* Maritime Commerce with Rome: Periplus of the Erythraean Sea and Pliny's Natural History document trade through Muziris and Arikamedu; exports: black pepper ('Yavanapriya'), beryl, pearls, ivory, fine cotton; imports: Roman gold/silver denarii, Arretine pottery, amphorae wine.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which ancient Tamil treatise is the earliest surviving work on Tamil grammar, rhetoric, and social conventions of the Sangam Age?
(a) Tirukkural
(b) Tolkappiyam
(c) Silappadikaram
(d) Manimekalai
Correct Answer: (b) Tolkappiyam [Explanation: Authored by Tolkappiyar, the 'Tolkappiyam' is the foundational work of classical Tamil literature, covering phonology, syntax, and poetic conventions.]

* Q2. The Chera monarch Cheran Senguttuvan is celebrated in the epic 'Silappadikaram' for introducing which religious cult in Tamil society?
(a) Murugan Cult
(b) Pattini (Kannagi) Cult
(c) Bhagavata Cult
(d) Korravai Cult
Correct Answer: (b) Pattini (Kannagi) Cult [Explanation: Senguttuvan founded the Pattini cult (veneration of Kannagi as the ideal chaste woman), bringing a stone from the Himalayas to carve her idol.]

* Q3. In Sangam poetry, what was the term used by merchants and writers to describe black pepper due to its intense demand among Roman and Greek traders?
(a) Karpu
(b) Yavanapriya
(c) Yavanasura
(d) Marakkar
Correct Answer: (b) Yavanapriya [Explanation: Black pepper from Kerala was known as 'Yavanapriya' (beloved of the Yavanas/Greeks/Romans) and fetched its weight in silver in Roman markets.]

* Q4. Which ancient coastal port site in Tamil Nadu was excavated by Sir Mortimer Wheeler, revealing an Indo-Roman commercial trading station with Roman amphorae and Arretine pottery?
(a) Muziris
(b) Korkai
(c) Arikamedu
(d) Puhar
Correct Answer: (c) Arikamedu [Explanation: Arikamedu (near Puducherry) was an Indo-Roman maritime trade emporium where excavations yielded Roman clay wine amphorae, terra sigillata ware, and glass beads.]

* Q5. Who was the author of the celebrated classical Tamil Buddhist epic 'Manimekalai', written as a sequel to 'Silappadikaram'?
(a) Ilango Adigal
(b) Chithalai Chathanar
(c) Tiruvalluvar
(d) Kapilar
Correct Answer: (b) Chithalai Chathanar [Explanation: Sittalai Sattanar (Chithalai Chathanar), a grain merchant of Madurai, composed the epic 'Manimekalai' recounting the spiritual conversion of Kovalan's daughter to Buddhism.]
