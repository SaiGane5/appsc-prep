# Gandhian Era: Early Satyagrahas, Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement


The return of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi to India in January 1915 fundamentally revolutionized the Indian National Movement. Testing his South African techniques of Truth (Satya) and Non-Violence (Ahimsa) in Champaran, Ahmedabad, and Kheda, Gandhi transformed the freedom struggle from an elite constitutional discourse into a mass popular upsurge, culminating in the nationwide Non-Cooperation and Khilafat Movement (1920–1922).

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Evolutionary Milestones of Gandhi's Early Mass Mobilizations

| Movement / Episode & Year | Geographical Focus & Adversaries | Core Grievance & Tactical Methods | Historical Outcome & Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Champaran Satyagraha (1917) | Champaran, Bihar; European Indigo Planters. | Coercive 'Tinkathia' system (forcing peasants to cultivate indigo on 3/20th of land); First Civil Disobedience in India. | Gandhi refused magistrate's order to leave; Champaran Agrarian Committee formed; Tinkathia abolished with 25% compensation refunded to peasants. |
| Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918) | Ahmedabad, Gujarat; Textile Mill Owners. | Discontinuation of Plague Bonus; Gandhi's First Hunger Strike in India; advocated 35% wage hike based on trusteeship. | Mill owners arbitrated and conceded the full 35% wage increase; established the Textile Labour Association. |
| Kheda Satyagraha (1918) | Kheda, Gujarat; Colonial Revenue Administration. | Severe crop failure (yield below 25% of normal); revenue officials refused land tax suspension; First Non-Cooperation in India. | Government issued secret instructions to suspend revenue collection for all destitute cultivators; Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel emerged as key leader. |
| Rowlatt Satyagraha & Jallianwala Bagh (1919) | Punjab & Nationwide; Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act 1919 (Rowlatt Act - detention without trial for 2 years). | Rowlatt Satyagraha Sabha; arrest of Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr. Satyapal led to Jallianwala Bagh gathering (13 April 1919, Baisakhi day). | General Dyer opened fire on unarmed crowd, killing 1,000+; Rabindranath Tagore renounced Knighthood; Hunter Commission whitewash; triggered loss of moral legitimacy for British rule. |
| Non-Cooperation & Khilafat Movement (1920–1922) | Pan-Indian; Alliance of Congress and Khilafat Committee (Ali Brothers - Shaukat Ali, Mohammad Ali). | Tripartite goal: Redressal of Punjab wrongs, Khilafat restoration (Ottoman Caliphate), and achievement of Swaraj within one year. | Surrender of British titles (Gandhi returned Kaiser-i-Hind); boycott of government schools, courts, and foreign cloth; picketing of liquor shops; foundation of National Universities (Kashi Vidyapeeth, Jamia Millia, Gujarat Vidyapith). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Nagpur Session of INC (December 1920): Ratified the Non-Cooperation program under C. Vijayaraghavachariar; reorganized Provincial Congress Committees on linguistic lines; introduced 4-anna nominal membership fee, opening Congress to the rural masses; established 15-member Congress Working Committee (CWC).

* Mass Upsurge Dynamics: Peasant revolts (Awadh Kisan Sabha led by Baba Ramchandra, Eka Movement), tribal protests (Manyam rebellion under Alluri Sitarama Raju in AP), working-class strikes in tea plantations (Assam) and railways, and student walkouts.

* Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922): Violent clash in Gorakhpur district, UP, where an enraged mob set fire to a police station, killing 22 policemen inside.

* Withdrawal of Movement: Gandhi unilaterally suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement on 12 February 1922 via the Bardoli Resolution, maintaining that India was not yet adequately trained in non-violence; deeply disappointed younger leaders (Nehru, Subhas Bose).

* Birth of Swaraj Party (January 1923): Formed by C.R. Das (President) and Motilal Nehru (Secretary) as 'Pro-Changers' advocating council entry to 'wreck the colonial legislature from within', opposing 'No-Changers' (Rajagopalachari, Rajendra Prasad, Vallabhbhai Patel) who focused on constructive work.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which local peasant leader from Bihar persistently persuaded Mahatma Gandhi at the 1916 Lucknow Congress to visit Champaran and investigate the grievances of indigo ryots?
(a) Raj Kumar Shukla
(b) Brajkishore Prasad
(c) Anugrah Narayan Sinha
(d) J.B. Kripalani
Correct Answer: (a) Raj Kumar Shukla [Explanation: Raj Kumar Shukla, an indigo cultivator from Champaran, persistently urged Gandhi to visit Bihar, leading to Gandhi's historic First Civil Disobedience at Champaran in 1917.]

* Q2. Following the gruesome Jallianwala Bagh Massacre on 13 April 1919, who renounced his British Knighthood in an impassioned protest letter to Viceroy Lord Chelmsford?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi
(b) Rabindranath Tagore
(c) Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
(d) Subhas Chandra Bose
Correct Answer: (b) Rabindranath Tagore [Explanation: Rabindranath Tagore renounced his Knighthood in May 1919, declaring that the time had come when badges of honour make our shame glaring in the incongruous context of suffering.]

* Q3. The Non-Cooperation program was formally endorsed and the constitution of the Indian National Congress reorganized on linguistic lines at which historic annual session in December 1920?
(a) Calcutta Special Session
(b) Nagpur Annual Session
(c) Ahmedabad Session
(d) Gaya Session
Correct Answer: (b) Nagpur Annual Session [Explanation: The December 1920 Nagpur Congress session, presided over by C. Vijayaraghavachariar, ratified the Non-Cooperation program and reorganized Congress committees along linguistic boundaries.]

* Q4. Following the violent death of 22 policemen at Chauri Chaura on 4 February 1922, Mahatma Gandhi suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement through which historic resolution?
(a) Lucknow Resolution
(b) Bardoli Resolution
(c) Delhi Manifesto
(d) Wardha Resolution
Correct Answer: (b) Bardoli Resolution [Explanation: The Congress Working Committee met at Bardoli on 12 February 1922 and passed the Bardoli Resolution formally terminating the civil disobedience and Non-Cooperation movement.]

* Q5. In January 1923, Chittaranjan Das and Motilal Nehru founded which political party to contest elections and wreck the 1919 Montagu-Chelmsford legislative councils from within?
(a) Independent Labour Party
(b) Swaraj Party (Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party)
(c) Forward Bloc
(d) Justice Party
Correct Answer: (b) Swaraj Party (Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party) [Explanation: C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru founded the Congress-Khilafat Swarajya Party (Swaraj Party) in January 1923 to enter legislative councils and obstruct colonial governance from within.]
