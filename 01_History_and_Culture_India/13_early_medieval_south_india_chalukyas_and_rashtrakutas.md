# Early Medieval South India: Badami Chalukyas, Eastern Chalukyas & Rashtrakutas


Between the 6th and 10th centuries CE, the Deccan was dominated by powerful imperial dynasties that bridged North and South Indian cultural traditions. The Badami Chalukyas pioneered Vesara temple architecture in Karnataka; the Eastern Chalukyas anchored Telugu linguistic statehood in coastal Andhra; and the Rashtrakutas emerged as a paramount pan-Indian empire that contested the Tripartite Struggle for Kanauj and excavated the monolithic Kailasanatha rock-cut temple at Ellora.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Dynastic Matrix of Medieval Deccan Hegemons

| Dynasty & Eras | Prominent Monarchs & Capital | Military Feats & Political Reach | Architecture, Literature & Cultural Legacy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Badami Chalukyas (c. 543–753 CE) | Pulakesin I, Pulakesin II, Vikramaditya I; Vatapi (Badami). | Pulakesin II defeated Harsha at Narmada and Kadambas; diplomatic ties with Sasanian Persia. | Vesara architectural idiom at Badami, Aihole ('Cradle of Indian Temple Architecture' - Lad Khan, Durga Temple), and Pattadakal (Virupaksha Temple - UNESCO site). |
| Eastern Chalukyas (c. 624–1075 CE) | Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Gunaga Vijayaditya; Vengi & Rajahmundry. | Defended coastal Andhra against Rashtrakutas; marital alliances with the Cholas leading to the Chalukya-Chola union under Kulottunga I. | Patronized earliest Telugu literary evolution; Nannaya Bhatta commenced translation of Mahabharata into Telugu at Rajahmundry; constructed Draksharama temple. |
| Rashtrakutas (c. 753–982 CE) | Dantidurga, Krishna I, Amoghavarsha I, Krishna III; Manyakheta (Malkhed). | Dantidurga performed Hiranyagarbha ritual; fought Tripartite Struggle against Palas and Gurjara-Pratiharas; captured Kanauj. | Krishna I carved monolithic Kailasa Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora; Amoghavarsha I authored 'Kavirajamarga' (earliest Kannada poetic treatise) and 'Prashnottara Ratnamalika'; Arab traveler Al-Masudi called Rashtrakuta king 'Balhara' (one of the 4 greatest sovereigns of the world). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Aihole Prashasti: Composed in 634 CE by court poet Ravikirti on the Meguti Jain temple at Aihole; provides authentic poetic account of Pulakesin II's conquests from the eastern coast to the western sea.

* Tripartite Struggle for Kanauj: Century-long conflict among the Rashtrakutas of the Deccan, Palas of Bengal (Dharmapala), and Gurjara-Pratiharas of Malwa (Nagabhata II, Mihira Bhoja) to control the agrarian riches and imperial symbol of Kanauj.

* Amoghavarsha I (814–878 CE, 'Ashoka of the South'): Devout Jain patron under preceptor Jinasena; ruled for 64 years; built capital Manyakheta; celebrated for his peaceful, non-aggressive reign and philosophical scholarship.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. The celebrated rock-cut monolithic Kailasanatha Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora was excavated under the royal patronage of which Rashtrakuta monarch?
(a) Dantidurga
(b) Krishna I
(c) Amoghavarsha I
(d) Govinda III
Correct Answer: (b) Krishna I [Explanation: King Krishna I (756–774 CE) commissioned the monolithic rock-cut Kailasanatha temple at Ellora, carved top-down out of a single basalt cliff.]

* Q2. The earliest surviving poetic work in the Kannada language, 'Kavirajamarga', is attributed to which illustrious Deccan ruler?
(a) Pulakesin II
(b) Amoghavarsha I
(c) Vishnuvardhana
(d) Tailapa II
Correct Answer: (b) Amoghavarsha I [Explanation: Rashtrakuta Emperor Amoghavarsha I Nrupatunga authored the celebrated 'Kavirajamarga' (c. 850 CE), the foundational treatise on Kannada grammar and poetics.]

* Q3. The Aihole Inscription of 634 CE, composed in Sanskrit by court poet Ravikirti, is inscribed on which temple structure at Aihole?
(a) Lad Khan Temple
(b) Meguti Jain Temple
(c) Durga Temple
(d) Huchimalli Temple
Correct Answer: (b) Meguti Jain Temple [Explanation: Ravikirti incised the Aihole Prashasti on the eastern wall of the Meguti Jain temple at Aihole during the reign of Western Chalukya King Pulakesin II.]

* Q4. Which 10th-century Arab traveler described the Rashtrakuta king ('Balhara') as one of the four great monarchs of the world alongside the emperors of Rome, China, and Baghdad?
(a) Al-Masudi
(b) Al-Biruni
(c) Ibn Battuta
(d) Abdur Razzaq
Correct Answer: (a) Al-Masudi [Explanation: Arab geographer Al-Masudi visited the Rashtrakuta capital Manyakheta in 915 CE and acclaimed the 'Balhara' (Vallabharaja) as one of the world's four greatest monarchs.]

* Q5. Who was the founder of the Eastern Chalukya dynasty of Vengi in coastal Andhra, appointed as viceroy by his elder brother Pulakesin II?
(a) Vishnuvardhana (Kubja Vishnuvardhana)
(b) Gunaga Vijayaditya
(c) Rajaraja Narendra
(d) Jayasimha I
Correct Answer: (a) Vishnuvardhana (Kubja Vishnuvardhana) [Explanation: Kubja Vishnuvardhana was installed as ruler of Vengi around 624 CE by Pulakesin II, founding the Eastern Chalukya dynasty that ruled coastal Andhra for five centuries.]
