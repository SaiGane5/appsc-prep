# British Imperial Expansion: Marathas, Mysore, Sikhs & Paramountcy Policies


Between 1767 and 1849, the British East India Company dismantled the premier military powers of the subcontinent—Mysore, the Maratha Confederacy, and the Sikh Empire—through a combination of military campaigns and diplomatic instruments of paramountcy. Guided by Lord Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance and Lord Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse, the Company subjugated princely states, erasing Indian sovereignty across South Asia.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Subsidiary Alliance System (Lord Wellesley, 1798–1805): Princely state surrendered foreign relations to Company; stationed British subsidiary force at its own expense or ceded territory; accepted a British Resident in court; barred employing other Europeans. First state to sign: Nizam of Hyderabad (1798), followed by Mysore (1799), Tanjore (1799), Awadh (1801), and Peshwa (1802).

* Doctrine of Lapse (Lord Dalhousie, 1848–1856): If an Indian ruler of a protected state died without a natural male heir, the state 'lapsed' to the British Crown, nullifying traditional Hindu adoption rights. Annexed states: Satara (1848), Jaitpur and Sambalpur (1849), Baghat (1850), Udaipur (1852), Jhansi (1853), and Nagpur (1854).

* Annexation of Awadh (1856): Dalhousie deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah on the pretext of 'misgovernance' (based on James Outram's report), alienating thousands of Awadh sepoys who constituted the backbone of the Bengal Army.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Indian princely ruler was the very first to accept Lord Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance system in 1798?
(a) Nawab of Awadh
(b) Nizam of Hyderabad
(c) Peshwa Baji Rao II
(d) Raja of Travancore
Correct Answer: (b) Nizam of Hyderabad [Explanation: The Nizam of Hyderabad (Nizam Ali Khan) signed the first Subsidiary Alliance treaty with Governor-General Lord Wellesley in September 1798.]

* Q2. In which historic battle during the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War did Tipu Sultan, the 'Tiger of Mysore', die heroically defending his capital?
(a) Battle of Pollilur
(b) Battle of Porto Novo
(c) Fall of Seringapatam (1799)
(d) Battle of Sholinghur
Correct Answer: (c) Fall of Seringapatam (1799) [Explanation: On 4 May 1799, Tipu Sultan was killed defending the breaches of his capital fort at Seringapatam against British forces led by General George Harris and Arthur Wellesley.]

* Q3. Under Lord Dalhousie's aggressive 'Doctrine of Lapse', which was the very first princely state annexed by the British in 1848?
(a) Jhansi
(b) Nagpur
(c) Satara
(d) Sambalpur
Correct Answer: (c) Satara [Explanation: Satara in Maharashtra was the first state annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse in 1848 when Raja Appa Sahib died without a biological male heir.]

* Q4. On what specific administrative grounds did Lord Dalhousie annex the wealthy kingdom of Awadh in February 1856?
(a) Failure to pay subsidiary army arrears
(b) Chronic internal misgovernance
(c) Lack of a biological male heir
(d) Treasonous alliance with Russia
Correct Answer: (b) Chronic internal misgovernance [Explanation: Dalhousie annexed Awadh in 1856 citing persistent 'misgovernance' under Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, acting on the controversial report of Resident James Outram.]

* Q5. By which treaty was the Second Anglo-Maratha War precipitated when Peshwa Baji Rao II fled to the British and signed a Subsidiary Alliance in 1802?
(a) Treaty of Salbai
(b) Treaty of Bassein
(c) Treaty of Purandar
(d) Treaty of Deogaon
Correct Answer: (b) Treaty of Bassein [Explanation: Under the Treaty of Bassein (1802), Peshwa Baji Rao II surrendered Maratha independence to the British in exchange for military restoration against Holkar.]
