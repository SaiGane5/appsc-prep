# Data Interpretation (DI), Data Sufficiency Frameworks, and Probability Theory


Data Interpretation assesses the quantitative ability to synthesize tabular and graphic data into statistical conclusions. Data Sufficiency evaluates whether provided statements provide sufficient mathematical information to answer a question without completing tedious full computations.


## 1. Data Interpretation Visualizations & Conversions


### Table: Key Visual Formats in Data Interpretation

| Chart Type | Visual Nature | Key Conversion Formula / Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pie Chart | Circular sector distribution totaling $360^\circ$ or $100\%$. | $\text{Percentage} = \frac{\text{Angle}}{360^\circ} \times 100\%$; $\text{Angle} = \frac{\text{Percentage}}{100} \times 360^\circ$. |
| Bar Graph | Discrete rectangular bars comparing categorical magnitudes. | Percentage Growth: $\frac{\text{Value}_{t} - \text{Value}_{t-1}}{\text{Value}_{t-1}} \times 100\%$. |
| Line Graph | Continuous time-series tracking temporal trends. | Identify rate of change / slope of line segments. |
| Tabular Data | Multi-dimensional rows and columns. | Ratio comparisons and column aggregations. |



## 2. Data Sufficiency Decision Matrix

* Option A: Statement (I) ALONE is sufficient to answer the question, but Statement (II) alone is not sufficient.

* Option B: Statement (II) ALONE is sufficient to answer the question, but Statement (I) alone is not sufficient.

* Option C: BOTH Statements (I) and (II) TOGETHER are required to answer the question.

* Option D: Statements (I) and (II) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient to answer the question (additional data needed).

* Option E: EITHER Statement (I) alone OR Statement (II) alone is sufficient independently.




## 3. Probability Fundamentals


Probability is the measure of the likelihood that an event will occur: $P(E) = \frac{n(E)}{n(S)}$, where $n(E)$ is the number of favorable outcomes and $n(S)$ is the total sample space. $0 \le P(E) \le 1$.


## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Analytical Data Sufficiency Evaluation

1. Question: Is the integer x divisible by 6?
Statement I: x is divisible by 3.
Statement II: x is divisible by 4.
Evaluate the data sufficiency step-by-step and determine whether statements alone or together suffice. (APPSC Group-1 Mains Analytical, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: To be divisible by 6, x must be divisible by both 2 and 3. Statement I tells us x is divisible by 3 (e.g., x=9, not divisible by 6; x=12, divisible by 6) -> Insufficient. Statement II tells us x is divisible by 4 (meaning x is divisible by 2 and an even multiple of 4; e.g., x=8, not divisible by 6; x=12, divisible by 6) -> Insufficient. Combining Statements I and II: x is divisible by both 3 and 4. Since 3 and 4 are co-prime, x must be divisible by LCM(3, 4) = 12. Every multiple of 12 is necessarily a multiple of 6 (12 = 6 * 2). Thus, both statements together are SUFFICIENT to answer the question definitively.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Two dice are thrown simultaneously. What is the probability of getting two numbers whose sum is at least 10?
(a) 1/6
(b) 1/12
(c) 5/36
(d) 1/4
Correct Answer: (a) 1/6 [Explanation: Total outcomes n(S) = 6 * 6 = 36. Outcomes with sum >= 10: Sum 10: (4,6), (5,5), (6,4) [3]. Sum 11: (5,6), (6,5) [2]. Sum 12: (6,6) [1]. Total favorable outcomes n(E) = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6. Probability = 6 / 36 = 1/6.]

* Q2. In a pie chart showing government budgetary expenditures, the sector for 'Education' subtends a central angle of 54°. What percentage of total budget is spent on Education?
(a) 12%
(b) 15%
(c) 18%
(d) 20%
Correct Answer: (b) 15% [Explanation: Percentage = (Angle / 360°) * 100% = (54 / 360) * 100 = (3 / 20) * 100 = 15%.]

* Q3. From a standard pack of 52 cards, two cards are drawn at random. What is the probability that both cards are Kings?
(a) 1/169
(b) 1/221
(c) 4/663
(d) 2/221
Correct Answer: (b) 1/221 [Explanation: P = [4C2] / [52C2] = [ (4 * 3) / 2 ] / [ (52 * 51) / 2 ] = 6 / 1326 = 1 / 221.]

* Q4. A bag contains 5 red, 4 green, and 3 blue balls. If a ball is drawn at random, what is the probability that it is NOT green?
(a) 1/3
(b) 2/3
(c) 3/4
(d) 5/12
Correct Answer: (b) 2/3 [Explanation: Total balls = 5 + 4 + 3 = 12. Non-green balls = Red + Blue = 5 + 3 = 8. Probability = 8 / 12 = 2/3.]

* Q5. Question: What is the age of Ravi?
Statement I: Ravi is 4 years older than his sister Priya.
Statement II: The ratio of Ravi's age to Priya's age is 5:3.
Which statement(s) are sufficient?
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient
(b) Statement II alone is sufficient
(c) Both Statements I and II together are sufficient
(d) Neither Statement is sufficient
Correct Answer: (c) Both Statements I and II together are sufficient [Explanation: Statement I alone: R - P = 4 (infinite possibilities). Statement II alone: R/P = 5/3 (infinite possibilities). Combining both: 5x - 3x = 4 => 2x = 4 => x = 2. Ravi's age = 5(2) = 10 years. Both statements together are sufficient.]
