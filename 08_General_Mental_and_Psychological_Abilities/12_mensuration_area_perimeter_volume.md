# Mensuration: Planar Areas, Perimeters, and 3D Solid Surface Areas & Volumes


Mensuration involves the numerical measurement of geometric lengths, boundaries, two-dimensional surface areas, and three-dimensional volumetric capacities. In public works, civil engineering administration, and agricultural surveying, mensuration formulas determine land boundaries, canal capacities, and civil infrastructure costs.


## 1. 2D Planar Geometrical Formulas


### Table: Formulas for Area and Perimeter of 2D Figures

| 2D Geometrical Shape | Perimeter Formula | Area Formula | Key Geometric Property |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scalene Triangle | $a + b + c$ | $\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$ (Heron's) | Semi-perimeter $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$. |
| Equilateral Triangle | $3a$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} a^2$ | Height $h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} a$. |
| Rectangle & Square | Rect: $2(l+b)$; Square: $4a$ | Rect: $l \times b$; Square: $a^2 = \frac{d^2}{2}$ | Diagonal $d = \sqrt{l^2 + b^2}$. |
| Trapezium | Sum of 4 sides | $\frac{1}{2}(a + b) \times h$ | $a$ and $b$ are parallel sides, $h$ is distance. |
| Rhombus | $4a$ | $\frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2$ | Diagonals bisect at $90^\circ$; $4a^2 = d_1^2 + d_2^2$. |
| Circle & Circular Sector | Circumference: $2\pi r$ | Area: $\pi r^2$; Sector: $\frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \pi r^2$ | Length of arc $L = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} 2\pi r$. |



## 2. 3D Solid Geometry: Surface Areas & Volumes


### Table: Formulas for Surface Area and Volume of 3D Solids

| 3D Solid | Curved / Lateral Surface Area (CSA) | Total Surface Area (TSA) | Volume ($V$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cube | $4a^2$ | $6a^2$ | $a^3$ (Diagonal $= a\sqrt{3}$) |
| Cuboid | $2(l+b)h$ (Area of 4 walls) | $2(lb + bh + lh)$ | $l \times b \times h$ (Diagonal $= \sqrt{l^2+b^2+h^2}$) |
| Right Circular Cylinder | $2\pi rh$ | $2\pi r(h + r)$ | $\pi r^2 h$ |
| Right Circular Cone | $\pi rl$ (Slant height $l = \sqrt{r^2+h^2}$) | $\pi r(l + r)$ | $\frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h$ |
| Sphere | $4\pi r^2$ | $4\pi r^2$ | $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$ |
| Hemisphere | $2\pi r^2$ | $3\pi r^2$ | $\frac{2}{3} \pi r^3$ |



## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Analytical Mensuration Modelling

1. A metallic solid sphere of radius 10.5 cm is melted and recast into several smaller solid cones, each of radius 3.5 cm and height 3 cm. Find the number of cones formed and evaluate the surface area change. (APPSC Group-1 Mains Analytical, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: 1. In melting and recasting, total volume remains strictly conserved. Volume of sphere = (4/3) * π * R^3 = (4/3) * π * (10.5)^3. 2. Volume of one cone = (1/3) * π * r^2 * h = (1/3) * π * (3.5)^2 * 3 = π * (3.5)^2. 3. Number of cones n = Volume of sphere / Volume of one cone = [ (4/3) * π * (10.5)^3 ] / [ π * (3.5)^2 ] = (4/3) * [ (10.5 * 10.5 * 10.5) / (3.5 * 3.5) ] = (4/3) * [ 3 * 3 * 10.5 ] = 4 * 3 * 10.5 = 12 * 10.5 = 126 cones formed. 4. Surface area comparison shows an exponential expansion in aggregate surface area, significant in metallurgy and material physics.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. If the radius of a cylinder is doubled and the height is halved, what is the ratio of the new volume to the original volume?
(a) 1 : 1
(b) 2 : 1
(c) 4 : 1
(d) 1 : 2
Correct Answer: (b) 2 : 1 [Explanation: Original volume V1 = π * r^2 * h. New volume V2 = π * (2r)^2 * (h/2) = π * 4r^2 * (h/2) = 2πr^2h = 2 * V1. The ratio V2 : V1 is 2 : 1.]

* Q2. The diagonal of a square is 10√2 cm. What is its area?
(a) 50 cm^2
(b) 100 cm^2
(c) 200 cm^2
(d) 100√2 cm^2
Correct Answer: (b) 100 cm^2 [Explanation: Area of a square = d^2 / 2 = (10√2)^2 / 2 = (100 * 2) / 2 = 100 cm^2.]

* Q3. How many bricks of dimensions 25 cm x 12.5 cm x 7.5 cm are required to build a wall 5 m long, 3 m high, and 20 cm thick?
(a) 1,200
(b) 1,280
(c) 1,500
(d) 1,600
Correct Answer: (b) 1,280 [Explanation: Volume of wall in cm^3 = 500 cm * 300 cm * 20 cm = 3,000,000 cm^3. Volume of one brick = 25 * 12.5 * 7.5 = 2,343.75 cm^3. Number of bricks = 3,000,000 / 2,343.75 = 1,280 bricks.]

* Q4. A wheel makes 1,000 revolutions in covering a distance of 88 km. What is the diameter of the wheel?
(a) 14 m
(b) 28 m
(c) 56 m
(d) 7 m
Correct Answer: (b) 28 m [Explanation: Total distance = 88 km = 88,000 m. Distance in 1 revolution (Circumference) = 88,000 / 1000 = 88 m. 2πr = πd = 88 => (22/7) * d = 88 => d = (88 * 7) / 22 = 4 * 7 = 28 meters.]

* Q5. If the surface area of a sphere is 616 cm^2, what is its volume? (Use π = 22/7)
(a) 1,437.33 cm^3
(b) 1,248.50 cm^3
(c) 1,512.00 cm^3
(d) 1,386.66 cm^3
Correct Answer: (a) 1,437.33 cm^3 [Explanation: 4πr^2 = 616 => 4 * (22/7) * r^2 = 616 => r^2 = (616 * 7) / 88 = 7 * 7 = 49 => r = 7 cm. Volume = (4/3)πr^3 = (4/3) * (22/7) * 7^3 = (4/3) * 22 * 49 = 4312 / 3 ≈ 1,437.33 cm^3.]
