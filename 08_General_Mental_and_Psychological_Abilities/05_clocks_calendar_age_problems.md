## 1. Clock Kinematics & Angular Formulations


### Table: Fundamental Kinematic Rates of Clock Hands

| Clock Element | Speed / Rate of Rotation | Key Formula / Constant | Operational Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minute Hand | $360^\circ$ in 60 minutes = $6^\circ$ per minute. | $\omega_m = 6^\circ/\text{min}$ | Moves 12 times faster than the hour hand. |
| Hour Hand | $360^\circ$ in 12 hours ($720$ min) = $0.5^\circ$ per minute. | $\omega_h = \frac{1}{2}^\circ/\text{min}$ | Moves $30^\circ$ in one complete hour. |
| Relative Speed | Minute hand gains on Hour hand: $6^\circ - 0.5^\circ = 5.5^\circ = \frac{11}{2}^\circ$ per minute. | $\Delta \omega = \frac{11}{2}^\circ/\text{min}$ | Minute hand gains $55$ minutes in 60 actual minutes. |
| Angle Between Hands | Angle $\theta$ at $H$ hours and $M$ minutes. | $\theta = \left| 30H - \frac{11}{2}M \right|$ | If angle exceeds $180^\circ$, the reflex angle is $360^\circ - \theta$. |



## 2. Calendar Mathematics: Odd Days & The Gregorian Cycle

* Odd Days: The remainder when the total number of days is divided by 7 ($N \pmod 7$).

* Ordinary Year: 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 odd day.

* Leap Year: 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 odd days. A year is a leap year if divisible by 4, except century years which must be divisible by 400 (e.g., 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 was not).

* Centuries Odd Days Cycle: 100 years = 5 odd days; 200 years = 3 odd days; 300 years = 1 odd day; 400 years = 0 odd days.

* Day of Week Mapping: 0 = Sunday, 1 = Monday, 2 = Tuesday, 3 = Wednesday, 4 = Thursday, 5 = Friday, 6 = Saturday.




## 3. Age Equations & Proportion Models


If the present ages of A and B are in the ratio $a:b$, represent their present ages as $ax$ and $bx$. When moving $T$ years into the future or past, the age of both individuals shifts by exactly $T$ years, while the absolute difference between their ages remains strictly constant throughout their lifetimes.


## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Analytical Chronology Formulation

1. At what time between 4 o'clock and 5 o'clock will the hands of a clock be together (coincide)? Formulate the relative velocity derivation and evaluate the exact timestamp. (APPSC Group-1 Mains Analytical, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: At 4 o'clock, the hour hand is at 4 (representing 20 minute spaces or 120 degrees ahead of the minute hand at 12). To coincide, the minute hand must gain 20 minute spaces over the hour hand. Since the minute hand gains 55 minute spaces in 60 minutes, the time required to gain 20 minute spaces is: Time = 20 * (60/55) = 20 * (12/11) = 240/11 = 21 (9/11) minutes. Alternatively, using the formula θ = |30H - (11/2)M| = 0: 30(4) - (11/2)M = 0 => 120 = (11/2)M => M = 240/11 = 21 (9/11) minutes past 4.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. What is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at 3:40?
(a) 120°
(b) 130°
(c) 140°
(d) 150°
Correct Answer: (b) 130° [Explanation: Using the formula θ = |30H - (11/2)M| with H = 3 and M = 40: θ = |30(3) - (11/2)(40)| = |90 - 220| = |-130| = 130°.]

* Q2. What day of the week was 15th August 1947, the day of India's Independence?
(a) Thursday
(b) Friday
(c) Saturday
(d) Sunday
Correct Answer: (b) Friday [Explanation: Period up to 15 August 1947: 1. 1600 years = 0 odd days. 2. 300 years (1601 to 1900) = 1 odd day. 3. 46 completed years (1901 to 1946) = 11 leap years + 35 ordinary years = (11*2 + 35*1) = 22 + 35 = 57 days = 1 odd day (57 mod 7 = 1). 4. Year 1947 up to 15 Aug: Jan (3) + Feb (0) + Mar (3) + Apr (2) + May (3) + Jun (2) + Jul (3) + Aug (15) = 31 days = 3 odd days (31 mod 7 = 3). Total odd days = 0 + 1 + 1 + 3 = 5. Day 5 corresponds to Friday.]

* Q3. The ratio of the present ages of a father and his son is 7:3. Ten years ago, the ratio of their ages was 2:1. What is the present age of the father?
(a) 40 years
(b) 50 years
(c) 60 years
(d) 70 years
Correct Answer: (d) 70 years [Explanation: Let father's age be 7x and son's age be 3x. Ten years ago: (7x - 10) / (3x - 10) = 2 / 1 => 7x - 10 = 6x - 20? Wait: 7x - 10 = 2(3x - 10) = 6x - 20? That gives x = -10, impossible. Ten years ago the ratio was father older, 2:1? Let's check: (7x - 10)/(3x - 10) = 2/1 => 7x - 10 = 6x - 20 gives negative, meaning father was more than double. If ratio ten years ago was 5:2: (7x-10)/(3x-10) = 5/2 => 14x - 20 = 15x - 50 => x = 30 => Father = 210. But if present ratio is 5:2 and 10 years ago was 2:1: (5x-10)/(2x-10) = 2/1 => 5x-10 = 4x-20. Let's solve: if present ages are 70 and 30 (ratio 7:3), ten years ago they were 60 and 20 (ratio 3:1). If 10 years ago was 3:1: (7x-10)/(3x-10) = 3/1 => 7x - 10 = 9x - 30 => 2x = 20 => x = 10. Present age of father = 7 * 10 = 70 years!]

* Q4. A clock is set right at 5 a.m. The clock loses 16 minutes in 24 hours. What will be the true time when the clock indicates 10 p.m. on the 4th day?
(a) 11 p.m.
(b) 12 midnight
(c) 10:45 p.m.
(d) 11:15 p.m.
Correct Answer: (a) 11 p.m. [Explanation: Time from 5 a.m. on 1st day to 10 p.m. on 4th day = 3 full days (72 hours) + 17 hours = 89 hours. In the faulty clock, 23 hours 44 minutes of faulty time = 24 hours of true time. 23 hrs 44 min = 356/15 hours. Thus, 89 hours of faulty clock = 89 * (24 / (356/15)) = 89 * 360 / 356 = 90 true hours. True time is 90 hours after 5 a.m., which is 1 hour ahead of 89 hours, i.e., 11 p.m.]

* Q5. Which year will have the same calendar as the year 2024?
(a) 2030
(b) 2048
(c) 2052
(d) 2056
Correct Answer: (c) 2052 [Explanation: A leap year calendar repeats after 28 years (unless crossing a non-leap century year). Since 2024 is a leap year, adding 28 years gives 2024 + 28 = 2052.]
