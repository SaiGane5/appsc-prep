# Commercial Mathematics: Percentages, Profit-Loss-Discount, and Simple & Compound Interest


Commercial arithmetic forms the basis of financial evaluations, revenue calculations, tax assessment, banking interest accruals, and subsidy impacts in administrative governance.


## 1. Percentage Relationships & Net Successive Change Formula


### Table: Fundamental Percentage Transformation Rules

| Scenario | Mathematical Formula | Example Calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Successive Percentage Changes ($a\%$ then $b\%$) | $\text{Net } \% \text{ Change} = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$ | Successive +20% and +10%: $20 + 10 + \frac{200}{100} = +32\%$. |
| Commodity Price Rise Compensation | If price rises by $R\%$, reduction in consumption to keep spend constant: $\left(\frac{R}{100+R}\right) \times 100\%$ | Price rises by 25%: $\frac{25}{125} \times 100 = 20\%$ cut in consumption. |
| Profit / Loss Percentage | $\text{Profit } \% = \frac{SP - CP}{CP} \times 100$, $\text{Loss } \% = \frac{CP - SP}{CP} \times 100$ | Always computed with reference to Cost Price ($CP$). |
| Discount on Marked Price | $\text{Discount} = MP - SP$, $\text{Discount } \% = \frac{MP - SP}{MP} \times 100$ | Always computed with reference to Marked Price ($MP$). |



## 2. Simple Interest (SI) vs. Compound Interest (CI)


### Table: Interest Mechanics and Shortcut Formulas

| Interest Type | Accrual Formula | Key Analytical Shortcuts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple Interest (SI) | $SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$ | Interest remains constant every single year. Total Amount $A = P + SI$. |
| Compound Interest (CI) | $A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T$, $CI = A - P$ | Interest earns interest. If compounded half-yearly: replace $R$ with $R/2$ and $T$ with $2T$. |
| Difference (CI - SI) for 2 Years | $\text{Diff}_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$ | Directly calculates principal or rate without compounding manually. |
| Difference (CI - SI) for 3 Years | $\text{Diff}_3 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$ | Vital shortcut for high-yield civil service aptitude tests. |



## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Analytical Financial Modelling

1. A sum of money invested at compound interest doubles itself in 5 years. In how many years will it become 8 times of itself at the same rate of interest? Prove using exponential compounding equations. (APPSC Group-1 Mains Analytical, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Let principal be P and rate be R. Amount after 5 years = 2P. Using compounding formula: 2P = P(1 + R/100)^5 => (1 + R/100)^5 = 2. We need amount to become 8 times, so A = 8P. 8P = P(1 + R/100)^T => (1 + R/100)^T = 8 = 2^3. Substitute 2 = (1 + R/100)^5: (1 + R/100)^T = [ (1 + R/100)^5 ]^3 = (1 + R/100)^15. Equating bases and exponents: T = 15 years. Thus, the money will become 8 times in exactly 15 years.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. The difference between Compound Interest and Simple Interest on a certain sum of money for 2 years at 10% per annum is ₹65. What is the principal sum?
(a) ₹6,000
(b) ₹6,500
(c) ₹7,000
(d) ₹7,500
Correct Answer: (b) ₹6,500 [Explanation: Using Diff_2 = P * (R/100)^2: 65 = P * (10/100)^2 = P * (1/10)^2 = P / 100 => P = 65 * 100 = ₹6,500.]

* Q2. A dishonest shopkeeper professes to sell his goods at cost price, but uses a false weight of 950 grams for a 1 kg weight. What is his actual gain percentage?
(a) 5%
(b) 5.26%
(c) 5.55%
(d) 6.25%
Correct Answer: (b) 5.26% [Explanation: Gain % = [Error / (True Value - Error)] * 100 = [50 / (1000 - 50)] * 100 = (50 / 950) * 100 = 100 / 19 ≈ 5.26%.]

* Q3. Two successive discounts of 20% and 15% are equivalent to a single net discount of:
(a) 32%
(b) 35%
(c) 30%
(d) 38%
Correct Answer: (a) 32% [Explanation: Net discount = a + b - (ab/100) = 20 + 15 - (20 * 15 / 100) = 35 - 3 = 32%.]

* Q4. A person sells two articles for ₹9,900 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. What is his overall gain or loss percentage on the entire transaction?
(a) No profit no loss
(b) 1% Gain
(c) 1% Loss
(d) 2% Loss
Correct Answer: (c) 1% Loss [Explanation: When two items are sold at the same Selling Price with equal percentage gain and loss x%, there is always an overall loss given by Loss % = (x / 10)^2 % = (10 / 10)^2 = 1% loss.]

* Q5. In what time will ₹1,000 become ₹1,331 at 10% per annum compound interest, compounded annually?
(a) 2 years
(b) 2.5 years
(c) 3 years
(d) 4 years
Correct Answer: (c) 3 years [Explanation: A = P(1 + R/100)^T => 1331 = 1000(1 + 10/100)^T => 1331 / 1000 = (11 / 10)^T => (11/10)^3 = (11/10)^T => T = 3 years.]
