# Ratios, Proportions, Direct/Inverse Variations, Partnerships, and Alligation Mixtures


Ratio and proportion define relative magnitudes and scale relationships across quantitative data. In governance, this underpins budget allocations, demographic proportionalities, business partnership dividends, and resource-blending problems.


## 1. Fundamental Properties of Proportions & Types


### Table: Proportionality Relationships and Formulas

| Concept / Term | Mathematical Definition | Formula / Solution Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fourth Proportional | If $a : b :: c : d$, then $d$ is the fourth proportional to $a, b, c$. | $d = \frac{b \times c}{a}$ |
| Third Proportional | If $a : b :: b : c$, then $c$ is the third proportional to $a$ and $b$. | $c = \frac{b^2}{a}$ |
| Mean Proportional | Mean proportional $x$ between $a$ and $b$. | $x = \sqrt{a \times b}$ |
| Compounded Ratio | Compounding of $a:b$ and $c:d$. | $\frac{ac}{bd}$ |
| Direct vs. Inverse Variation | Direct: $y = kx$ ($y/x = \text{const}$). Inverse: $xy = k$ ($y = k/x$). | Direct: more speed = more distance. Inverse: more speed = less time. |



## 2. Rule of Alligation (Weighted Mixture Cross-Method)


When two ingredients at costs/concentrations $c_1$ (cheaper) and $d_1$ (dearer) are mixed to produce an intermediate mean mixture $m$:


### Table: Alligation Cross Formula for Mixtures

| Cheaper Price ($c$) | Dearer Price ($d$) | Mean Price ($m$) | Ratio of Quantities ($q_c : q_d$) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $c$ | $d$ | $m$ | $\frac{\text{Quantity of Cheaper}}{\text{Quantity of Dearer}} = \frac{d - m}{m - c}$ |



## 3. Partnership Profit-Sharing Principles

* Simple Partnership: Profit is shared directly in the ratio of capital investments when time is equal: $P_1 : P_2 = C_1 : C_2$.

* Compound Partnership: When capital is invested for different durations, profit is divided in the ratio of the product of capital and time: $P_1 : P_2 = (C_1 \times T_1) : (C_2 \times T_2)$.

* Working vs Sleeping Partner: Working partners receive an agreed management salary/commission deducted from gross profit before dividing remaining net profit according to capital-time ratios.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. If A : B = 3 : 4, B : C = 8 : 9, and C : D = 15 : 16, what is the combined ratio A : D?
(a) 1 : 2
(b) 5 : 8
(c) 3 : 5
(d) 2 : 3
Correct Answer: (b) 5 : 8 [Explanation: A/D = (A/B) * (B/C) * (C/D) = (3/4) * (8/9) * (15/16). Simplifying: (3 * 8 * 15) / (4 * 9 * 16) = (360) / (576) = 5/8. Therefore, A : D = 5 : 8.]

* Q2. In what ratio must tea at ₹62 per kg be mixed with tea at ₹72 per kg so that the mixture must be worth ₹64.50 per kg?
(a) 3 : 1
(b) 3 : 2
(c) 4 : 3
(d) 5 : 3
Correct Answer: (a) 3 : 1 [Explanation: Using Rule of Alligation: Cheaper c = 62, Dearer d = 72, Mean m = 64.50. Ratio (Cheaper : Dearer) = (d - m) : (m - c) = (72 - 64.50) : (64.50 - 62) = 7.50 : 2.50 = 3 : 1.]

* Q3. What is the mean proportional between 0.08 and 0.18?
(a) 0.12
(b) 0.14
(c) 0.16
(d) 0.18
Correct Answer: (a) 0.12 [Explanation: Mean proportional x = √(a * b) = √(0.08 * 0.18) = √(0.0144) = 0.12.]

* Q4. If 15 men working 8 hours a day can complete a project in 21 days, in how many days can 14 men working 6 hours a day complete the same project?
(a) 25 days
(b) 30 days
(c) 32 days
(d) 35 days
Correct Answer: (b) 30 days [Explanation: Formula: M1 * D1 * H1 = M2 * D2 * H2. 15 * 21 * 8 = 14 * D2 * 6 => 2,520 = 84 * D2 => D2 = 2,520 / 84 = 30 days.]

* Q5. A vessel contains 60 litres of milk. 12 litres of milk is taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated one more time. How much milk is left in the vessel now?
(a) 36.4 litres
(b) 38.4 litres
(c) 40.0 litres
(d) 42.5 litres
Correct Answer: (b) 38.4 litres [Explanation: Amount of pure liquid remaining = Initial * [1 - (x / C)]^n = 60 * [1 - (12/60)]^2 = 60 * (4/5)^2 = 60 * (16/25) = 38.4 litres.]
