# Social Intelligence, Decision-Making Science, Critical Thinking, and Personality Psychology


Social intelligence, cognitive decision-making, and critical thinking constitute the strategic attributes of senior leadership. Civil administrators operate in ambiguous, multi-stakeholder environments where objective evidence evaluation and interpersonal diplomacy determine policy success.


## 1. Social Intelligence: Karl Albrecht's SPACE Model


### Table: Karl Albrecht's Five Dimensions of Social Intelligence (SPACE)

| Dimension | Conceptual Essence | Administrative Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S - Situational Awareness | Ability to 'read' the social context, understand group dynamics, and anticipate social reactions. | Anticipating community grievances before policy changes trigger public demonstrations. |
| P - Presence | Bearing, demeanor, body language, and the impression of confidence and integrity projected to others. | Inspiring confidence and public authority during crises and media press briefings. |
| A - Authenticity | Being honest, transparent, and ethically grounded; avoiding manipulative pretenses. | Fostering genuine trust and credibility among citizen groups and subordinates. |
| C - Clarity | Ability to express ideas clearly, articulate policy vision, and persuade constructively. | Issuing clear, unambiguous government notifications and administrative directives. |
| E - Empathy | Connecting with others, valuing diverse viewpoints, and creating mutual respect. | Bridging communal or caste divisions through sensitive diplomatic dialogue. |



## 2. Administrative Decision-Making Models & Cognitive Biases

* Herbert Simon's Bounded Rationality: Human decision-makers face cognitive limitations, incomplete information, and time pressures. Instead of 'optimizing' (seeking the perfect economic choice), administrators 'satisfice' (choose an option that satisfies minimum criteria for adequacy).

* Confirmation Bias: The tendency to search for, interpret, and recall information in a way that confirms prior beliefs while ignoring disconfirming data.

* Sunk Cost Fallacy: Continuing to invest resources in a failing government project solely because of prior financial investments, rather than evaluating future viability.

* Groupthink (Irving Janis): Tendency for cohesive decision-making groups to prioritize consensus over realistic appraisal of alternatives, leading to flawed public policy.

## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Analytical Decision-Making Case Framework

1. Explain Herbert Simon's concept of 'Bounded Rationality' and 'Satisficing'. How does it explain decision-making in disaster relief operations where complete information is impossible to obtain? (APPSC Group-1 Mains Analytical, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: 1. Theoretical Definition: Simon demonstrated that classical economic rationality (optimizing) assumes perfect knowledge, infinite computing power, and zero time constraints—conditions absent in the real world. Under Bounded Rationality, decision-makers are constrained by cognitive limits, incomplete information, and pressing time. 2. Satisficing: Administrators seek an alternative that is 'good enough' to solve immediate threats rather than searching indefinitely for an elusive 'best' solution. 3. Application to Disaster Relief: In flood or earthquake crises, a District Collector cannot wait for complete damage assessments or aerial drone maps. Immediate decisions (deploying boats, dispatching food packets, hospital triaging) must be made based on partial intelligence. Satisficing saves lives; optimizing costs lives through paralysis by analysis.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Who formulated the concept of 'Bounded Rationality' and the 'Administrative Man' who satisfices rather than optimizes?
(a) Max Weber
(b) Herbert Simon
(c) Elton Mayo
(d) Chester Barnard
Correct Answer: (b) Herbert Simon [Explanation: Herbert Simon won the 1978 Nobel Prize in Economics for his pioneering research on the decision-making process within economic organizations, formulating Bounded Rationality.]

* Q2. In the Big Five (OCEAN) personality taxonomy, high self-discipline, dutifulness, organization, and adherence to rules correspond to which trait?
(a) Extraversion
(b) Conscientiousness
(c) Openness
(d) Agreeableness
Correct Answer: (b) Conscientiousness [Explanation: Conscientiousness is characterized by high levels of thoughtfulness, good impulse control, and goal-directed behaviors.]

* Q3. The psychological tendency of a cohesive committee to suppress dissenting opinions and reach premature unanimity without evaluating critical risks is termed:
(a) Cognitive Dissonance
(b) Groupthink
(c) Halo Effect
(d) Pygmalion Effect
Correct Answer: (b) Groupthink [Explanation: Groupthink, conceptualized by Irving Janis, occurs when the desire for harmony or conformity in a group results in an irrational or dysfunctional decision-making outcome.]

* Q4. In Karl Albrecht's SPACE model of Social Intelligence, the letter 'A' stands for:
(a) Aggressiveness
(b) Adaptability
(c) Authenticity
(d) Ambition
Correct Answer: (c) Authenticity [Explanation: In Albrecht's SPACE model, A stands for Authenticity (being honest, ethical, and true to oneself).]

* Q5. An official refuses to cancel a non-viable infrastructure project because ₹50 crore has already been spent on it, despite new assessments proving it will incur perpetual losses. This exemplifies:
(a) Anchoring Bias
(b) Sunk Cost Fallacy
(c) Framing Effect
(d) Availability Heuristic
Correct Answer: (b) Sunk Cost Fallacy [Explanation: The sunk cost fallacy is the phenomenon whereby a person is reluctant to abandon a strategy or course of action because they have already invested heavily in it, even when abandonment is clearly beneficial.]
