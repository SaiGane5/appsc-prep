# Attitude, Moral Thinkers & Philosophical Traditions: Indian & Western


Attitudes represent psychological tendencies that shape how individuals perceive and respond to social realities, structured through the ABC/CAB model (Cognitive, Affective, Behavioral). In governance, public servants require progressive moral and political attitudes, cultivated through social influence and grounded in the profound moral philosophies of Western thinkers (Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Rawls) and Indian visionaries (Kautilya, Vivekananda, Gandhi, Tagore).

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* The CAB Model of Attitude: Cognitive (beliefs, knowledge, facts), Affective (emotions, feelings), and Behavioral (tendency to act).

* Persuasion and Social Influence: Techniques used by administrators to alter harmful public behaviors (Swachh Bharat Abhiyan, pulse polio campaigns, helmet/seatbelt drives) via credible communicators, emotional framing, and social proof.

* Moral vs Political Attitudes: Moral attitudes are grounded in deep moral convictions (e.g. anti-corruption, gender equality, vegetarianism); Political attitudes determine political beliefs on nationalism, democracy, state welfarism, and social justice.

* Kautilya's Arthashastra on Administrative Ethics: 'In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare; whatever pleases himself he shall not consider as good, but whatever pleases his subjects he shall consider as good'.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. In social psychology, what does the 'CAB Model' of attitude structure specifically stand for?
(a) Conscience, Action, Belief
(b) Cognitive, Affective, Behavioral
(c) Character, Attitude, Behavior
(d) Competence, Aptitude, Benevolence
Correct Answer: (b) Cognitive, Affective, Behavioral [Explanation: The CAB (or ABC) model posits that attitudes comprise Cognitive (beliefs), Affective (feelings), and Behavioral (action tendencies) components.]

* Q2. The thought experiment of the 'Veil of Ignorance' to derive principles of distributive justice was formulated by which 20th-century political philosopher?
(a) Robert Nozick
(b) John Rawls
(c) Michael Sandel
(d) Amartya Sen
Correct Answer: (b) John Rawls [Explanation: John Rawls introduced the 'Veil of Ignorance' in his masterwork 'A Theory of Justice' (1971) to ensure impartial selection of fair social principles.]

* Q3. Which four Cardinal Virtues were identified by Plato in 'The Republic' as essential for the moral health of the individual and the ideal state?
(a) Faith, Hope, Charity, Love
(b) Wisdom, Courage, Temperance, Justice
(c) Truth, Non-violence, Celibacy, Simplicity
(d) Liberty, Equality, Fraternity, Dignity
Correct Answer: (b) Wisdom, Courage, Temperance, Justice [Explanation: Plato's four cardinal virtues are Prudence/Wisdom (Sophrosyne), Courage (Andreia), Temperance (Temperantia), and Justice (Dikaiosyne).]

* Q4. Who famously articulated the ideal of administrative statecraft stating: 'In the happiness of his subjects lies his happiness; in their welfare his welfare'?
(a) Ashoka
(b) Kautilya (Chanakya)
(c) Tiruvalluvar
(d) Manu
Correct Answer: (b) Kautilya (Chanakya) [Explanation: In the Arthashastra, Kautilya instructs the monarch that royal interest is subservient to public welfare ('Prajasukhe sukham rajnah').]

* Q5. The successful nationwide behavior-change campaign of 'Swachh Bharat Mission' utilized which psychological principle to eliminate open defecation?
(a) Coercive penal taxation
(b) Persuasion, community nudging, and social proof
(c) Subsidized commercial advertising
(d) Mandatory legal injunctions
Correct Answer: (b) Persuasion, community nudging, and social proof [Explanation: Swachh Bharat achieved mass behavior change through participatory social nudging, trigger mechanisms by Swachhagrahis, and emotional persuasion.]
