# Ethics & Human Interface: Essence, Determinants & Ethical Dilemmas


Ethics examines moral principles that guide human conduct, distinguishing right actions from wrong. Operating at the interface of human consciousness and social obligation, ethics evaluates human actions through intentions, means, and consequences. In public administration, ethical principles ensure that discretionary bureaucratic power serves the common good with fairness, integrity, and compassion.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Major Western & Indian Ethical Frameworks

| Ethical Framework | Key Philosophers & Traditions | Core Moral Criterion | Application in Public Administration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deontology (Duty Ethics) | Immanuel Kant; Bhagavad Gita (Nishkama Karma). | Inherent rightness of the action regardless of consequences; Categorical Imperative; duty for duty's sake. | Adherence to rule of law, honesty, non-discrimination, refusing bribes regardless of outcomes. |
| Consequentialism / Utilitarianism | Jeremy Bentham ('Quantitative'), John Stuart Mill ('Qualitative'). | Greatest happiness of the greatest number; cost-benefit calculus based on outcomes. | Public policy design: allocating welfare funds, building infrastructure for maximum public benefit. |
| Virtue Ethics | Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics, Golden Mean); Confucius. | Cultivation of moral character and inner virtues (temperance, courage, justice, wisdom, phronesis). | Building administrative integrity, ethical habits, and moral resilience against institutional corruption. |
| Indian Ethos & Dharma | Kautilya (Arthashastra), Mahatma Gandhi (Satya & Ahimsa), Ashoka. | Dharma (righteous moral duty), Nishkama Karma (selfless action), Sarvodaya (welfare of all), Antyodaya. | Empathetic civil service committed to serving the poorest citizen (Taliman test). |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Determinants of Ethics: Individual conscience, family upbringing, religious and cultural traditions, legal statutes and societal norms, peer groups and educational institutions.

* Ethics in Private vs Public Relationships: Private ethics is governed by emotional bonds, affection, and personal loyalty; Public ethics demands universal neutrality, strict non-partisanship, transparency, and loyalty to the Constitution.

* Mahatma Gandhi's Seven Social Sins (Young India, 1925): Politics without principles, Wealth without work, Pleasure without conscience, Knowledge without character, Commerce without morality, Science without humanity, and Worship without sacrifice.

* Gandhi's Talisman: 'Recall the face of the poorest and the weakest man whom you may have seen, and ask yourself if the step you contemplate is going to be of any use to him'.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which philosophical ethical school, founded by Jeremy Bentham and J.S. Mill, evaluates the morality of an action solely on the basis of producing the 'greatest happiness for the greatest number'?
(a) Deontological Ethics
(b) Utilitarianism (Consequentialism)
(c) Virtue Ethics
(d) Intuitionism
Correct Answer: (b) Utilitarianism (Consequentialism) [Explanation: Utilitarianism holds that actions are morally right to the extent that they promote the greatest overall happiness and minimize pain for the largest number of people.]

* Q2. The concept of 'Categorical Imperative'—acting only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law—was formulated by:
(a) Aristotle
(b) Immanuel Kant
(c) John Locke
(d) Thomas Hobbes
Correct Answer: (b) Immanuel Kant [Explanation: Immanuel Kant formulated the Categorical Imperative as the foundational unconditional moral law of Deontology, demanding duty without exceptions.]

* Q3. In which publication did Mahatma Gandhi articulate his famous list of the 'Seven Social Sins' in 1925, including 'Commerce without morality' and 'Politics without principles'?
(a) Harijan
(b) Young India
(c) Indian Opinion
(d) Navajivan
Correct Answer: (b) Young India [Explanation: On 22 October 1925, Mahatma Gandhi published the 'Seven Social Sins' in his weekly journal 'Young India', highlighting moral rot in modern civilization.]

* Q4. In the philosophy of the Bhagavad Gita, the doctrine of performing one's prescribed moral duty with devotion without attachment to the fruits of action is termed:
(a) Svadharma
(b) Nishkama Karma
(c) Purushartha
(d) Samadhi
Correct Answer: (b) Nishkama Karma [Explanation: Nishkama Karma (selfless action without expectation of reward) is the central ethical doctrine of the Gita, urging duty dedicated to the higher good.]

* Q5. Which among the following is recognized as the ultimate internal subjective determinant of ethical judgment in human beings?
(a) Positive law
(b) Conscience (Antaratma)
(c) Public opinion
(d) Social sanctions
Correct Answer: (b) Conscience (Antaratma) [Explanation: Conscience is the supreme inner moral faculty that assesses the ethical quality of human thoughts and actions against intrinsic moral values.]
