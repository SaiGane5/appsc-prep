# Climate of India & AP: Monsoon Mechanism, El Niño & Cyclones


The climate of India is classified as Tropical Monsoon, driven by differential heating between the Indian Ocean and the Asian landmass, seasonal reversals of low-level planetary winds, and upper-tropospheric jet streams. Andhra Pradesh experiences a transitional climatic regime benefiting from both the Southwest Monsoon (June–September) and the retreating Northeast Monsoon (October–December), while remaining acutely vulnerable to devastating post-monsoon Bay of Bengal tropical cyclones.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Climatic Mechanisms & Atmospheric Phenomena Impacting India & AP

| Phenomenon | Mechanism & Driving Forces | Seasonal Timing | Impact on Rainfall in India & Andhra Pradesh |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Southwest Monsoon (Advancing) | Differential heating; shift of ITCZ over Gangetic plain; Somali Jet; heating of Tibetan plateau. | June to September (enters Kerala ~1 June; Rayalaseema ~5 June). | Accounts for 75% of India's annual precipitation; brings moderate rain to coastal AP and Rayalaseema (rain-shadow effect). |
| Northeast Monsoon (Retreating) | High pressure over Central Asia; withdrawal of ITCZ; winds blow from land to sea picking moisture over Bay of Bengal. | October to December. | Brings primary annual rainfall to South Coastal AP (Nellore, Prakasam) and Tamil Nadu (Coromandel coast); major cyclonic season. |
| El Niño Southern Oscillation (ENSO) | Abnormal warming of sea surface temperatures in the central/eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean. | Multi-year cycle (every 3–7 years). | Weakens Walker Circulation; creates descending dry air over India; historically correlated with 60%+ of major Indian droughts. |
| La Niña | Abnormal cooling of central/eastern equatorial Pacific waters. | Multi-year cycle. | Enhances convection over Indian subcontinent; correlated with robust surplus monsoon rainfall and flooding. |
| Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) | Difference in sea surface temperatures between western Arabian Sea and eastern Indian Ocean (Sumatra). | Annual / Seasonal. | Positive IOD warms western Indian Ocean, counteracting El Niño and boosting monsoon rainfall; Negative IOD suppresses rainfall. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Tropical Cyclones in Bay of Bengal: Fuelled by warm sea-surface temperatures (>26.5°C), Coriolis force, and low vertical wind shear; recurving tracks frequently make landfall along the coastal arc of Andhra Pradesh (Nellore to Srikakulam).

* Historic Andhra Cyclones: 1977 Diviseema Cyclone (tidal wave killed 10,000+), 1990 Machilipatnam Cyclone, 2014 Cyclone Hudhud (ravaged Visakhapatnam city with 215 km/h winds), and 2018 Cyclone Titli in Srikakulam.

* Koppen's Climate Classification for AP: Coastal AP classified as 'Aw' (Tropical Savanna with dry winters) and interior Rayalaseema as 'BSh' (Semi-arid Steppe climate with high evapotranspiration).

* Western Disturbances: Extratropical cyclonic storms originating in the Mediterranean Sea carried by Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream; bring vital winter rain/snow to NW India (Punjab, J&K), essential for Rabi wheat crops.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which oceanic-atmospheric phenomenon characterized by abnormal warming of the equatorial Pacific Ocean is historically correlated with weak monsoon rains and droughts in India?
(a) La Niña
(b) El Niño
(c) Positive IOD
(d) Madden-Julian Oscillation
Correct Answer: (b) El Niño [Explanation: El Niño causes warming of the central and eastern Pacific Ocean, weakening the Walker circulation and suppressing ascending air over the Indian subcontinent, triggering droughts.]

* Q2. The southern coastal districts of Andhra Pradesh, particularly Nellore and Prakasam, receive the bulk of their annual rainfall during which season?
(a) Southwest Monsoon (June–Sept)
(b) Northeast Monsoon (October–December)
(c) Pre-Monsoon Thundershowers (April–May)
(d) Western Disturbances (Jan–Feb)
Correct Answer: (b) Northeast Monsoon (October–December) [Explanation: South coastal Andhra Pradesh lies on the Coromandel belt and receives over 60% of its annual rainfall from retreating Northeast monsoonal winds and Bay of Bengal cyclones.]

* Q3. Category 4 Super Cyclone 'Hudhud' made devastating landfall in October 2014 directly over which coastal city of Andhra Pradesh with winds exceeding 200 km/h?
(a) Machilipatnam
(b) Visakhapatnam
(c) Kakinada
(d) Nellore
Correct Answer: (b) Visakhapatnam [Explanation: Cyclone Hudhud made landfall at Visakhapatnam on 12 October 2014, causing catastrophic structural and environmental damage to the port city.]

* Q4. In Koppen's climatic classification of India, the semi-arid drought-prone Rayalaseema plateau of Andhra Pradesh is designated under which climatic code?
(a) Amw
(b) Aw
(c) BShw (Semi-arid Steppe)
(d) Cwg
Correct Answer: (c) BShw (Semi-arid Steppe) [Explanation: Rayalaseema experiences a tropical semi-arid steppe climate with low erratic rainfall, coded as 'BShw' in Koppen's climate classification.]

* Q5. Winter rainfall in northwestern India (Punjab, Haryana, western UP), vital for the cultivation of Rabi wheat, is brought by:
(a) Northeast Monsoon
(b) Western Disturbances
(c) Kalbaisakhi
(d) Mango Showers
Correct Answer: (b) Western Disturbances [Explanation: Western Disturbances are extratropical low-pressure systems originating in the Mediterranean region that bring essential winter showers to North-West India.]
