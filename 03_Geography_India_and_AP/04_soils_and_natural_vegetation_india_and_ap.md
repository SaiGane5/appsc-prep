# Soils & Natural Vegetation of India & AP: Forests & Red Sanders


The pedological and ecological landscape of India reflects the interplay of underlying geology, relief, and rainfall gradients. Andhra Pradesh features fertile riverine alluvium in its deltas, extensive black cotton soils in the Krishna-Tungabhadra plains, and red sandy loams across Rayalaseema. Its forests range from moist deciduous canopies in the Eastern Ghats to the world's second-largest mangrove formation at Coringa and the endangered endemic Red Sanders (Pterocarpus santalinus) in Seshachalam.

### Table: Pedological Taxonomy of Major Soils in India and Andhra Pradesh

| Soil Type | Parent Rock & Mineral Composition | Geographical Distribution in AP & India | Agricultural Characteristics & Crops |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alluvial Soil (Khadar & Bhangar) | Fluvial deposition by river systems; rich in potash and lime; deficient in nitrogen and phosphorus. | Indo-Gangetic plains; Godavari, Krishna, and Pennar delta deltas in AP (20% of state area). | Highest natural fertility; ideal for wet paddy, sugarcane, banana, pulses, and vegetables. |
| Black Soil (Regur / Black Cotton) | Weathering of Deccan basaltic trap; high clay content; self-ploughing (cracks when dry, sticky when wet); rich in iron, lime, magnesium; deficient in nitrogen. | Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP; Guntur, Prakasam, Kurnool, Anantapur in AP (approx 25% area). | Exceptional moisture-retentive capacity; principal soil for cotton, chillies, tobacco, jowar, and oilseeds. |
| Red Soils (Red Sandy / Loamy) | Archean crystalline granites and gneisses; red color due to ferric oxide diffusion; permeable, friable; poor in nitrogen, phosphorus, humus. | Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Odisha; covers 65%+ of Andhra Pradesh (Rayalaseema and upland coastal plains). | Responsive to irrigation and chemical fertilizers; suitable for groundnut, millets, pulses, castor, and mango orchards. |
| Laterite Soil | Intense leaching in tropical areas of high rainfall and temperature; rich in iron and aluminium oxides; acidic; infertile. | Western Ghats, Meghalaya; localized patches in Srikakulam, Visakhapatnam, and Nellore in AP. | Requires heavy manuring; ideal for cashew nut, coffee, and rubber plantations. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Red Sanders (Pterocarpus santalinus): Endemic forest tree restricted entirely to the Seshachalam and Veligonda hills of Rayalaseema (Kadapa, Chittoor, Nellore); precious dense timber used in traditional East Asian acoustics, carvings, and medicines; protected under CITES Appendix II.

* Coringa Mangrove Wetland: Located in the Kakinada bay on the Godavari estuary; second largest mangrove forest formation in India after the Sundarbans; home to fishing cats, smooth-coated otters, and olive ridley sea turtle nesting beaches.

* Forest Cover in AP: According to the India State of Forest Report (ISFR 2021), Andhra Pradesh has a recorded forest area of 29,784 sq km (approx 18.28% of geographical area); leading districts: YSR Kadapa and Visakhapatnam.

* Forest Classifications in AP: Southern Tropical Dry Deciduous (Nallamalas), Tropical Moist Deciduous (Agency hills of Visakhapatnam/East Godavari), Littoral and Swamp (Mangroves of Coringa and Nizampatnam), and Southern Tropical Thorn scrub (Rayalaseema).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which precious commercial timber tree species is geographically endemic exclusively to the Seshachalam and Veligonda hill ranges of Andhra Pradesh?
(a) Teak (Tectona grandis)
(b) Red Sanders (Pterocarpus santalinus)
(c) Sandalwood (Santalum album)
(d) Rosewood (Dalbergia latifolia)
Correct Answer: (b) Red Sanders (Pterocarpus santalinus) [Explanation: Red Sanders (Yerra Chandanam) is strictly endemic to the Seshachalam biosphere reserve and adjoining hill tracts of Rayalaseema.]

* Q2. The Coringa Wildlife Sanctuary, which protects India's second-largest contiguous mangrove forest formation, is located on the estuary of which river?
(a) Krishna Estuary
(b) Godavari Estuary
(c) Pennar Estuary
(d) Vamsadhara Estuary
Correct Answer: (b) Godavari Estuary [Explanation: The Coringa Mangroves near Kakinada span over 235 sq km on the Godavari delta estuary, ranking second only to the Sundarbans.]

* Q3. Which soil type, characterized by high clay content, deep fissures when dry ('self-ploughing'), and moisture-retentive capacity, is predominant in Guntur and Kurnool?
(a) Laterite Soil
(b) Black Soil (Regur)
(c) Red Sandy Soil
(d) Saline Soil
Correct Answer: (b) Black Soil (Regur) [Explanation: Black Cotton Soil (Regur) exhibits high montmorillonite clay content, swelling when wet and cracking deeply when dry, ideal for cotton, tobacco, and chillies.]

* Q4. Which soil covers the largest geographical area (over 65%) across the state of Andhra Pradesh, especially dominant in Rayalaseema?
(a) Black Cotton Soil
(b) Red Soil
(c) Delta Alluvial Soil
(d) Laterite Soil
Correct Answer: (b) Red Soil [Explanation: Red soils (sandy loams and gravels), derived from ancient Archean granites, cover approximately two-thirds of Andhra Pradesh's total area.]

* Q5. Which district of Andhra Pradesh possesses the largest total area of recorded forest cover according to the Forest Survey of India?
(a) Chittoor
(b) YSR Kadapa
(c) Visakhapatnam
(d) East Godavari
Correct Answer: (b) YSR Kadapa [Explanation: YSR Kadapa district, encompassing extensive stretches of the Nallamala, Seshachalam, and Palkonda ranges, holds the largest forest area in the state.]
