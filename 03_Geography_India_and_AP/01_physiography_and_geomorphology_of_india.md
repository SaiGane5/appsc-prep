# Physiography of India & Andhra Pradesh: Structural Divisions & Geomorphology


The Indian subcontinent exhibits extraordinary geomorphological diversity shaped by plate tectonics—from the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates giving birth to the young fold mountains of the Himalayas, to the ancient Archean peninsular shield. Andhra Pradesh is characterized by three distinct physiographic provinces: the Coastal Plains along its 974 km coastline, the dissected Eastern Ghats, and the semi-arid Rayalaseema plateau.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Comparative Physiographic Profile of India and Andhra Pradesh

| Physiographic Province | Geological Age & Rock System | Key Structural Ranges & Features | Economic / Ecological Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Himalayan Mountain System | Tertiary folding (Cenozoic); sedimentary & metamorphic. | Himadri (Greater), Himachal (Lesser), Shiwaliks (Outer); highest peaks (Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi). | Perennial river headwaters, climatic barrier against Central Asian blizzards, tourism, hydro-electric potential. |
| Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra Plain | Pleistocene alluvium deposition in foredeep basin. | Bhabar (porous gravel), Tarai (marshy), Bhangar (older alluvium, kankar), Khadar (fertile floodplains). | Agricultural heartland of India, intensive tubewell irrigation, maximum demographic concentration. |
| Peninsular Plateau | Pre-Cambrian Archean crystalline shield (Gondwanaland). | Deccan Trap (basaltic black soil), Malwa, Chota Nagpur plateau (mineral heartland), Western & Eastern Ghats. | Richest mineral storehouse (iron, coal, bauxite); Western Ghats UNESCO biodiversity hotspot. |
| Coastal Plains & Eastern Ghats of AP | Dharwar & Cuddapah formations; Coastal alluvium. | Eastern Ghats (dissected by Godavari & Krishna; Armakonda / Jindhagada peak 1,690m), Coastal Plains (974 km coastline). | Paddy agriculture in K-G delta, bauxite reserves in Visakhapatnam, major ports and coastal economic zones. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Armakonda (Jindhagada Peak): Situated in the Araku region of Alluri Sitharama Raju district; at 1,690 meters, it is the highest peak in the Eastern Ghats and Andhra Pradesh.

* Cuddapah Supergroup: Proterozoic crescent-shaped sedimentary basin in Rayalaseema; rich in limestone, quartzites, and the world's largest barytes deposits at Mangampeta.

* Coastal Geomorphology: Andhra Pradesh possesses the second-longest mainland coastline in India (974 km, after Gujarat); characterized by the twin deltaic protrusions of Godavari and Krishna with freshwater Kolleru lake and brackish Pulicat lagoon.

* Islands: Andaman and Nicobar volcanic/coral chain (Barren Island active volcano, Indira Point 6°45' N); Lakshadweep atoll archipelago in the Arabian Sea.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which is the highest mountain peak in the Eastern Ghats, situated in the Araku Valley region of Andhra Pradesh?
(a) Mahendragiri
(b) Jindhagada (Armakonda)
(c) Doddabetta
(d) Anamudi
Correct Answer: (b) Jindhagada (Armakonda) [Explanation: Jindhagada Peak (Armakonda), rising to 1,690 meters in the Visakhapatnam-Araku region, is the highest peak in the Eastern Ghats.]

* Q2. What is the total length of the mainland coastline of Andhra Pradesh, which ranks as the second longest in India?
(a) 7516 km
(b) 974 km
(c) 1214 km
(d) 580 km
Correct Answer: (b) 974 km [Explanation: Andhra Pradesh has a mainland coastline of 974 km, ranking second in India after Gujarat (1,214 km).]

* Q3. The crescent-shaped geological formation in Rayalaseema, renowned for massive deposits of limestone, slab stone, and barytes, is known as:
(a) Gondwana Basin
(b) Cuddapah Basin
(c) Dharwar Craton
(d) Vindhyan Basin
Correct Answer: (b) Cuddapah Basin [Explanation: The Proterozoic Cuddapah Basin is world-famous for its mineral wealth, containing the world's largest bedded barytes deposit at Mangampeta.]

* Q4. In the geomorphology of the Northern Plains of India, the narrow porous belt where Himalayan streams disappear underground into boulder gravels is termed:
(a) Tarai
(b) Bhabar
(c) Bhangar
(d) Khadar
Correct Answer: (b) Bhabar [Explanation: The Bhabar is an 8–10 km wide piedmont gravel belt along the Shiwalik foothills where streams sink and flow sub-surface before re-emerging in the wet Tarai.]

* Q5. Which island in the Andaman and Nicobar archipelago contains India's only confirmed active volcano?
(a) Narcondam Island
(b) Barren Island
(c) Havelock Island
(d) Neil Island
Correct Answer: (b) Barren Island [Explanation: Barren Island, situated 135 km northeast of Port Blair in the Andaman Sea, is the only active volcano in South Asia.]
