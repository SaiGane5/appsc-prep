# Kakatiyas of Warangal: Administration, Tank Irrigation & Temple Architecture


The Kakatiya dynasty (c. 1158–1323 CE) established a glorious unified Telugu state spanning Telangana and Coastal Andhra from their fortified capital Orugallu (Warangal). Renowned for the heroic reign of Rani Rudrama Devi, pioneering hydraulic chain-tank engineering (Pakhal, Ramappa, Laknavaram), commercial protection of foreign maritime traders via the Motupalli Charter, and floating-brick temple architecture at Ramappa (UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Kakatiyas forged a enduring regional identity.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Sovereigns & Structural Achievements of the Kakatiya Golden Era

| Monarch & Reign | Military & Political Milestones | Agrarian & Commercial Reforms | Architectural Legacies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rudradeva / Prataparudra I (1158–1195 CE) | Declared sovereign independence from Western Chalukyas; recorded feats in the Thousand Pillar Temple Inscription (Anumakonda). | Patronized Shaivism; commenced construction of Orugallu stone fort. | Built the Trikuta-style Thousand Pillar Temple at Anumakonda. |
| Ganapatideva (1199–1262 CE) | Unified Andhra coast, Rayalaseema, and Telangana; long reign of 63 years. | Issued the landmark Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam (1244 CE) granting protection and fixed customs duties to foreign merchant ships; built massive irrigation reservoirs. | Completed outer stone ramparts and 4 magnificent Torana gateways of Warangal Fort; general Recharla Rudra constructed Ramappa Temple (1213 CE). |
| Rani Rudrama Devi (1262–1289 CE) | One of India's few reigning queens; ruled as 'Rudradeva Maharaja'; repelled Yadavas of Devagiri, Eastern Gangas, and Pandyas. | Visited by Venetian traveler Marco Polo (1293) who praised her administrative justice and diamond mining at Golconda. | Defended Warangal's double fortification; died fighting rebel Kayastha chieftain Ambadeva in Chandupatla (Nalgonda). |
| Prataparudra II (1289–1323 CE) | Subdued internal revolts; reformed Nayankara military recruitment. | Encouraged expansion of agriculture in dry tracts; compiled Telugu legal codes. | Heroically resisted five Delhi Sultanate invasions (Malik Kafur, Khusrau Khan, Ulugh Khan) until Warangal fell in 1323 CE. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam (1244 CE): Pillar charter at Motupalli port (Bapatla district) by Ganapatideva promising foreign traders that their ships would not be confiscated if shipwrecked; fixed customs duty (Krupa) on silk, camphor, pearls, and metals at modest 1/30th value.

* Hydraulic Chain-Tank Engineering: Interconnected irrigation tanks across undulating topography where overflow from upper tanks filled lower reservoirs; constructed massive earthen bunds: Pakhal Lake, Ramappa Lake, Laknavaram Lake, and Sanigaram Lake.

* Nayankara System Origins: Kakatiyas were the original architects of the Nayankara system; Prataparudra divided the realm into 77 Nayankaras assigned to Padmanayaka warrior chiefs, directly ancestral to the Vijayanagar system.

* Ramappa Temple Architecture: Built by sculptor Ramappa under general Recharla Rudra; constructed using lightweight floating porous bricks (sponge bricks) and carved black basalt bracket figures (Madanikas / Salabhanjikas).

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which Kakatiya monarch issued the famous 'Motupalli Abhaya Sasanam' in 1244 CE, granting royal security and fixed customs tariffs to foreign sea merchants?
(a) Rudradeva
(b) Ganapatideva
(c) Rani Rudrama Devi
(d) Prataparudra II
Correct Answer: (b) Ganapatideva [Explanation: King Ganapatideva issued the Motupalli Pillar Charter in 1244 CE promising safety and fair duties to maritime traders from across the Indian Ocean.]

* Q2. The world-renowned Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple at Palampet, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021, was constructed in 1213 CE under the supervision of which general?
(a) Recharla Rudra
(b) Gona Ganna Reddy
(c) Muppidi Nayaka
(d) Prola Bhupati
Correct Answer: (a) Recharla Rudra [Explanation: General Recharla Rudra commissioned the temple in 1213 CE, uniquely named after its chief architect-sculptor Ramappa.]

* Q3. Venetian traveler Marco Polo visited the Andhra coast in the late 13th century at the port of Motupalli during the reign of which illustrious ruler?
(a) Ganapatideva
(b) Rani Rudrama Devi
(c) Prataparudra II
(d) Prola II
Correct Answer: (b) Rani Rudrama Devi [Explanation: Marco Polo visited coastal Andhra around 1293 CE, praising the administrative wisdom and diamond exports under Queen Rudrama Devi.]

* Q4. Which inscription discovered in Nalgonda district definitively established the martyrdom and death date (1289 CE) of Rani Rudrama Devi in battle against Kayastha Ambadeva?
(a) Anumakonda Inscription
(b) Chandupatla Inscription
(c) Bayyaram Inscription
(d) Malkapuram Inscription
Correct Answer: (b) Chandupatla Inscription [Explanation: The Chandupatla stone inscription (dated 27 November 1289) proved that Rani Rudrama Devi died fighting the rebellious Kayastha chieftain Ambadeva.]

* Q5. How many Nayankaras (feudal military commands) were established by Kakatiya King Prataparudra II to reorganize the defense of the kingdom?
(a) 50
(b) 72
(c) 77
(d) 108
Correct Answer: (c) 77 [Explanation: Prataparudra II divided the Kakatiya kingdom into 77 Nayankaras entrusted to loyal Padmanayaka warrior chiefs, laying the foundation for the Nayankara military system.]
