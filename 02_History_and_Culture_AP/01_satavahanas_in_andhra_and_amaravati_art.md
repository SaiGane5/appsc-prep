# Satavahanas in Andhra: Administration, Society, Economy & Amaravati Stupa


The Satavahana dynasty (c. 1st Century BCE to 3rd Century CE) represents the foundational imperial epoch of Andhra history. Transitioning from Kotilingala to Dhanyakataka (Dharanikota/Amaravati) on the banks of the Krishna River, the Satavahanas integrated the Deccan plateau, patronized Mahayana Buddhism under Acharya Nagarjuna, developed the magnificent white marble-carved Amaravati Maha Stupa, and conducted lucrative Indo-Roman maritime commerce.

| Art & Architecture | Amaravati School of Art; soft green/white Palnadu limestone. | Dynamic narrative relief panels depicting Jataka tales, life of Buddha, natural human movements and crowded scenes with profound emotional depth. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Bhattiprolu Inscription: Discovered in Guntur district; contains crystal reliquaries with Buddha relics; script showcases the 'Bhattiprolu script' (system of vowel markers), recognized by epigraphists as the direct ancestor of modern Telugu-Kannada alphabets.

* Acharya Nagarjuna: Patriarch of Mahayana Buddhism; stayed at Sriparvata (Nagarjunakonda) under the patronage of Satavahana king Yajna Sri Satakarni; composed 'Suhrallekha' (Friendly Epistle to King Gautamiputra) and 'Mulamadhyamakakarika'.

* Social Stratification: Four-class functional hierarchy (Maharathis/Mahabhojas, Amatyas/Sarthavahas, Halikas/cultivators, and Kammaras/artisans); flourishing trade guilds (Srenis: Kolika for weavers, Tilapishaka for oil-pressers) functioning as banks.

* Decline: After Yajna Sri Satakarni, the empire fragmented into feudatory successor lines: Ikshvakus in Coastal Andhra, Abhiras in Maharashtra, and Pallavas in the south.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which ancient Buddhist stupa in Andhra Pradesh yielded the famous crystal reliquary with an inscription representing the earliest precursor to the Telugu script?
(a) Amaravati Stupa
(b) Bhattiprolu Stupa
(c) Ghantasala Stupa
(d) Salihundam Stupa
Correct Answer: (b) Bhattiprolu Stupa [Explanation: The Bhattiprolu Stupa in Guntur district contained caskets with the 'Bhattiprolu script' (c. 3rd Century BCE), featuring the earliest developmental vowel signs ancestral to Telugu.]

* Q2. The ancient Roman geographer Ptolemy and the author of 'Periplus of the Erythraean Sea' refer to the ancient port of Machilipatnam on the Andhra coast as:
(a) Maisolos
(b) Barygaza
(c) Muziris
(d) Poduke
Correct Answer: (a) Maisolos [Explanation: Greco-Roman trade texts designate coastal Machilipatnam and the Krishna delta as 'Maisolos', celebrated for manufacturing super-fine translucent cotton muslin.]

* Q3. The great Mahayana Buddhist philosopher Acharya Nagarjuna resided and taught at Sriparvata (Nagarjunakonda) under the royal patronage of which Satavahana monarch?
(a) Simuka
(b) Gautamiputra Satakarni
(c) Yajna Sri Satakarni
(d) Hala
Correct Answer: (c) Yajna Sri Satakarni [Explanation: Acharya Nagarjuna was patronized by King Yajna Sri Satakarni, who built a magnificent multi-storeyed monastery for him at Sriparvata (Nagarjunakonda).]

* Q4. Which Satavahana site in Telangana yielded the earliest dynastic coins bearing the legends of King Simuka, proving eastern Deccan origins?
(a) Dhanyakataka
(b) Kotilingala
(c) Paithan
(d) Kondapur
Correct Answer: (b) Kotilingala [Explanation: Excavations at Kotilingala in Karimnagar (Jagtial) district yielded punch-marked and cast coins of King Simuka, Satakarni I, and Satavahana, confirming early Deccan presence.]

* Q5. In the Satavahana socio-economic structure in Andhra, trade and craft guilds that functioned as financial institutions and received fixed deposits were termed:
(a) Aharas
(b) Srenis
(c) Nigamas
(d) Ganas
Correct Answer: (b) Srenis [Explanation: Srenis were autonomous merchant and artisan corporations (weavers, potters, oil-pressers) that regulated manufacturing standards and acted as public deposit banks.]
