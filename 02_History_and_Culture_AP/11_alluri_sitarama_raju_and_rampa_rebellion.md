# Alluri Sitarama Raju & The Rampa Tribal Rebellion (1922–1924)


The Rampa Rebellion (Manyam Viplavam) of 1922–1924, waged in the dense Agency hill tracts of the Eastern Ghats (Visakhapatnam and East Godavari), represents one of the most formidable anti-colonial guerrilla wars in modern Indian history. Led by the legendary young revolutionary Alluri Sitarama Raju, the tribal Koyas and Konda Reddis took up arms against the draconian Madras Forest Act of 1882 and colonial forced labor, holding the British Empire at bay for nearly two years.

## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Muttadari System: Feudal revenue system in the Agency where hereditary tribal headmen (Muttadars) collected land dues; British officials like Deputy Tahsildar Bastian exploited Muttadars and compelled tribals to perform unpaid hard labor (Vetti).

* Key Lieutenants of Raju: Gam Gantam Dora (fearless commander who fought till the end), Gam Mallu Dora (captured and imprisoned in Andaman Cellular Jail, later elected to Lok Sabha in 1952), and Aggiraju (Pericherla Suryanarayana Raju).

* Guerrilla Tactics: Indigenous knowledge of mountainous forest terrain, whistle communications, traditional bow-and-arrow combined with captured modern firearms, and deep intelligence networks among hill villagers.

* Nationalist Acclaim: Mahatma Gandhi wrote in 'Young India': 'Though I do not approve of his armed rebellion, I pay my homage to the bravery and sacrifice of the late Sitarama Raju'; Netaji Bose lauded him as a jewel of Indian patriotism.

### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which repressive colonial legislation enacted in 1882 severely restricted the traditional 'Podu' (shifting) agriculture of tribal communities, triggering the Rampa Rebellion?
(a) Indian Forest Act 1878
(b) Madras Forest Act 1882
(c) Criminal Tribes Act 1871
(d) Agency Civil Rights Act
Correct Answer: (b) Madras Forest Act 1882 [Explanation: The Madras Forest Act of 1882 banned shifting cultivation (Podu) and access to forest resources, sparking deep tribal unrest in the Godavari Agency.]

* Q2. The opening salvo of the Rampa Rebellion occurred on 22 August 1922 with an audacious armed raid on which British police station led by Alluri Sitarama Raju?
(a) Krishnadevipeta Police Station
(b) Chintapalli Police Station
(c) Rajavommangi Police Station
(d) Rampachodavaram Police Station
Correct Answer: (b) Chintapalli Police Station [Explanation: Raju initiated the armed struggle on 22 August 1922 by raiding the Chintapalli police station in Visakhapatnam district, seizing rifles and ammunition.]

* Q3. In which fierce mountain ambush in September 1922 did Alluri's guerrilla fighters defeat the British forces and kill commanders Scott and Heighter?
(a) Battle of Onkar Ghat
(b) Battle of Damanapalli
(c) Battle of Kirrabu
(d) Battle of Mampa
Correct Answer: (b) Battle of Damanapalli [Explanation: At the Damanapalli Ghats, Raju's guerrillas ambushed the British police column, killing senior commanders Scott and Heighter and capturing massive armaments.]

* Q4. Which ruthless British officer was appointed as the Special Commissioner in 1923 with sweeping martial powers to crush the Rampa Rebellion?
(a) Major Goodall
(b) T.G. Rutherford
(c) Captain Tremenheere
(d) J.A. Travers
Correct Answer: (b) T.G. Rutherford [Explanation: T.G. Rutherford was appointed Special Commissioner of the Agency in 1923, imposing blockades, burning villages, and deploying the Assam Rifles to trap Raju.]

* Q5. On which tragic date in May 1924 was Alluri Sitarama Raju captured near Mampa and shot dead by British police at Koyyuru without a legal trial?
(a) 15 August 1924
(b) 7 May 1924
(c) 26 January 1924
(d) 11 November 1924
Correct Answer: (b) 7 May 1924 [Explanation: Alluri Sitarama Raju was captured at Mampa and shot dead without a trial at Koyyuru on 7 May 1924 by Major Goodall's forces, attaining immortal martyrdom at age 27.]
