# Post-Satavahana Andhra: Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri & Vishnukundins


Following the collapse of the Satavahanas in the early 3rd century CE, coastal Andhra witnessed state-building under the Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda), followed by the Brihatpalayanas, Salankayanas of Vengi, and the powerful Vishnukundins. The period was marked by unique royal dual-patronage (kings performing Vedic sacrifices while queens lavishly financed Buddhist Mahachaityas), the flourishing of international monastic education, and the excavation of multi-storeyed rock-cut cave temples at Undavalli and Mogalarajapuram.

## 1. Institutional & Structural Framework


### Table: Dynastic Profile of Post-Satavahana Andhra Kingdoms

| Dynasty & Eras | Capitals & Prominent Monarchs | Religious Duality & Inscriptions | Architectural & Cultural Legacies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri (c. 225–300 CE) | Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda); Vasishthiputra Chamtamula, Virapurushadatta, Ehuvula Chamtamula. | Kings performed Vedic Ashvamedha and Agnishtoma sacrifices; royal women (Chamtisri, Adavi Chamtisri) built Mahachaityas, viharas, and stupas for Aparamahavinaseliya Buddhist sect. | Nagarjunakonda emerged as Asia's premier Buddhist university; amphitheatre; earliest structural Hindu temples (Sarvadeva temple, Pushpabhadrasvami temple in Sanskrit inscriptions). |
| Salankayanas of Vengi (c. 300–440 CE) | Vengipura (Pedavegi near Eluru); Hastivarman, Nandivarman. | Devotees of Chitrarathasvami (Sun God); Samudragupta's Allahabad inscription mentions defeating Hastivarman of Vengi. | Established Brahminical settlements in Vengi; early copper plate charters in Prakrit and Sanskrit. |
| Vishnukundins (c. 440–616 CE) | Indrapalanagara, Vinukonda, Denduluru; Govindavarma, Madhavavarma II ('Janasraya'). | Devotees of Sriparvata Swami (Mallikarjuna of Srisailam); Madhavavarma II performed 11 Ashvamedha sacrifices and 1,000 Kratus. | Excavated monumental 4-storeyed rock-cut caves at Undavalli (colossal reclining Vishnu/Anantasayana) and Mogalarajapuram; Govindavarma authored Sanskrit treatise 'Janasraya Chhandovichiti'. |



## 2. Salient Dimensions & Critical Analysis

* Royal Female Benefactors at Nagarjunakonda: Princess Chamtisri (sister of Chamtamula I) erected the grand Mahachaitya housing the corporeal relics of the Buddha; Bodhisri, an upasika, financed monasteries for pilgrims from Ceylon (Sinhala Vihara), Kashmir, and China.

* Nagarjunakonda Amphitheatre: Roman-style open-air acoustic stone amphitheatre excavated in the valley, demonstrating intense Greco-Roman cultural interactions alongside Roman gold coin hoards.

* Vishnukundin Coinage: Unique iron and copper-alloy coins featuring a lion with upraised paw on obverse and a vase (Purna Kumbha) flanked by lamps on reverse.

* End of Vishnukundin Rule: Defeated in 624 CE by Western Chalukya monarch Pulakesin II at the Battle of Kunala (Kolleru lake), leading to the installation of Kubja Vishnuvardhana and the founding of the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi.




## Previous Years Questions (PYQs) - APPSC Group-1


### Part A: Mains Analytical & Descriptive Questions

1. Discuss the unique socio-religious phenomenon of dual patronage under the Ikshvakus of Vijayapuri and examine the architectural legacy of the Vishnukundins in Andhra Pradesh. (APPSC Group-1 Mains, 10 Marks)
-> Model Approach: Explain the socio-religious duality: Ikshvaku kings (Chamtamula I) reviving orthodox Vedic sacrifices (Ashvamedha) to assert royal legitimacy, while their royal consorts, sisters, and daughters (Chamtisri) made monumental endowments to Buddhist monasteries, proving societal religious syncretism; highlight excavations at Nagarjunakonda: Sinhala Vihara, acoustic amphitheatre, and earliest Sanskrit Hindu structural shrines; evaluate Vishnukundin rock-cut architecture: transition from Buddhist caves to Brahmanical cave shrines at Undavalli (four storeys, colossal Anantasayana Vishnu) and Mogalarajapuram; conclude on their role in shaping early Andhra classical culture.




### Part B: Screening / Prelims Objective Questions

* Q1. Which royal Ikshvaku princess made the historic endowments recorded in inscriptions for the construction of the Great Mahachaitya at Nagarjunakonda?
(a) Bodhisri
(b) Chamtisri
(c) Bappisri
(d) Rudradharabhattarika
Correct Answer: (b) Chamtisri [Explanation: Princess Chamtisri, sister of King Chamtamula I and chief female patron, built the magnificent Mahachaitya at Vijayapuri (Nagarjunakonda) for the Aparamahavinaseliya Buddhist sect.]

* Q2. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription of Samudragupta records that during his southern campaign (Dakshinapatha), he defeated King Hastivarman of which ancient Andhra dynasty?
(a) Ikshvakus
(b) Salankayanas of Vengi
(c) Vishnukundins
(d) Anandagotras
Correct Answer: (b) Salankayanas of Vengi [Explanation: Harisena's Prayag Prashasti notes that Samudragupta defeated Hastivarman, the Salankayana king of Vengi, before restoring him as a tributary monarch.]

* Q3. The monumental four-storeyed rock-cut cave temple at Undavalli near Guntur, famous for its colossal reclining sculpture of Lord Anantasayana Vishnu, was excavated by:
(a) Satavahanas
(b) Ikshvakus
(c) Vishnukundins
(d) Eastern Chalukyas
Correct Answer: (c) Vishnukundins [Explanation: The rock-cut cave temples of Undavalli and Mogalarajapuram were excavated under the royal patronage of the Vishnukundin monarchs in the 5th and 6th centuries CE.]

* Q4. Which powerful Vishnukundin monarch assumed the title 'Janasraya' and performed eleven Ashvamedha sacrifices and thousands of Vedic Kratus?
(a) Govindavarma
(b) Madhavavarma II
(c) Indrabhattaraka
(d) Vikramendravarma
Correct Answer: (b) Madhavavarma II [Explanation: King Madhavavarma II (440–495 CE) was the greatest Vishnukundin sovereign, celebrated for performing 11 Ashvamedha and 1,000 Agnishtoma sacrifices.]

* Q5. Which sacred family deity (Kula Devata) was revered by the Vishnukundin rulers in their royal copper plate grants?
(a) Lord Venkateswara
(b) Sriparvata Swami (Mallikarjuna of Srisailam)
(c) Amaralingeswara
(d) Narasimha of Simhachalam
Correct Answer: (b) Sriparvata Swami (Mallikarjuna of Srisailam) [Explanation: The Vishnukundin charters invoke 'Sriparvata Swami Padanudhyata' (devotees at the feet of Sriparvata Swami, identified with Mallikarjuna of Srisailam).]
